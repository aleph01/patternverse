*Draw a lesson from failure that leaves the underlying flaw intact.*

An experience appears to offer a useful lesson; the speaker extracts a self-serving or inappropriate one. The audience recognizes what should have changed and sees the character improving the wrong part of the situation.

This is a descriptive working label for the comic mechanism.

## Example 1 · Emo Philips

Prayer for a bicycle

> When I was a kid, I used to pray every night for a new bicycle. Then I realized that the Lord doesn’t work that way, so I stole one and asked Him to forgive me.  

— Emo Philips

**The turn:** Religious learning becomes a way to rationalize theft.

[Source](<https://www.chronogram.com/arts/emo-philips-at-the-beverly-on-october-19-6253238/>) · [Read the source passage](<https://curtwittig.com/wp-content/uploads/IV.-pp.323-402.pdf>)

## Example 2 · Anthony Jeselnik

An entire pack of cigarettes

Jeselnik recalls strict parents forcing him to smoke a whole pack. The familiar disciplinary setup promises a lesson about quitting; the lesson he says he learned is loyalty to a cigarette brand.

— Anthony Jeselnik

**The turn:** The punishment reinforces attachment to the thing it should discourage.

[Source](<https://petermcgraw.org/dropping-babies-with-anthony-jeselnik/>)

## Example 3 · Demetri Martin

Losing his temper in karate class

> Last week I lost my temper in my karate class. Man, I'm not doing that again until I'm a black belt. Because I can tell you there's a difference between taking karate and receiving karate.  

— Demetri Martin

**The turn:** He plans to improve his fighting advantage rather than his temper.

[Source](<https://www.dead-frog.com/comedians/jokes/demetri-martin>)

## What makes the structure work

Make both lessons recoverable: the reasonable one and the character’s substitute. This structure often reveals a stable motive, such as avoiding responsibility or preserving a reward. A random conclusion does not show what the person learned.
