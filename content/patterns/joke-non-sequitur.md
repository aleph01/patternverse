*Abruptly introduce a response or topic that does not follow from the established direction.*

A conversational turn arrives without the connection the audience expects. The break itself becomes conspicuous: a new announcement or concern interrupts an established direction rather than following from it.

## Example 1 · Reggie Watts

A mock expert talk loses its logical thread

Watts speaks in the manner of an authoritative conference presenter, moving from the future and memory to fear, its supposed opposite, and scientific-sounding measurements. He then shifts accent and register into casual hip-hop patter. The grammar and confidence imply a connected argument, while the actual sequence refuses to supply one.

— Reggie Watts

## Example 2 · Eddie Izzard

A metric complaint returns inside the Death Star discussion

While discussing the Death Star, Izzard imagines blunt New York-style dialogue that returns to an earlier American complaint about metric measures. A list of units ends with combine harvesters. Izzard then comments on the isolated audience laugh before returning to the Death Star.

— Eddie Izzard

## Example 3 · Sam Simmons

A conversation about bread becomes a disco routine for footwear

Simmons trades lines with a recorded voice about liking bread. When the recording questions the disco music, he treats it as music for putting bread on one’s feet. The exchange turns into a bread-shoe number, as though this bizarre activity were already an established category of entertainment.

— Sam Simmons

**The turn:** The shift from eating bread to wearing it breaks an ordinary conversational expectation. Once bread shoes are proposed, however, the music and footwear develop an intelligible local premise. This is a surreal transition with a secondary non-sequitur effect, rather than a sequence with no connection at all.

[Source](<https://transcripts.foreverdreaming.org/viewtopic.php?t=188805>)

## What makes the structure work

Make the missing connection perceptible. The listener needs a direction to follow before it breaks: a discussion, a list, a question or the sound of an argument. An ordinary change of topic is weaker evidence, and a surprising but logically relevant answer belongs to a different structure. These examples include both a brief interruption and sustained performances of disconnected reasoning.
