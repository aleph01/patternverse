*Revise your own statement into something less flattering or more revealing.*

A speaker notices an apparent mistake and repairs it. In a comic self-correction, the repair reveals a worse assumption, punctures the speaker’s confidence or exposes the machinery of the performance. The correction becomes the payoff.

## Example 1 · Sarah Silverman

Correcting a description of a boyfriend as half-black

> I can say that, by the way, cause I used to go out with a guy who was half black.  
>
> Who totally broke up with me. Cause I'm a f\*cking loser.  
>
> I just heard myself say that.  
>
> I'm such a pessimist.  
>
> It was a worst attitude. He's a half-white. And he totally broke up with me.  

— Sarah Silverman

**The turn:** The correction reveals the persona’s prejudice.

[Source](<https://transcripts.foreverdreaming.org/viewtopic.php?t=143334>)

## Example 2 · Emo Philips

Taking back a complaint about taking things back

> You know what I hate? Indian givers. No, I take that back.  

— Emo Philips

**The turn:** He explicitly retracts his complaint, making the revision itself the payoff. Taking back words echoes taking back a gift: the two senses of the expression create an appearance of hypocrisy. The speaker changes his stated position, while the pun supplies the extra sting.

[Source](<https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Emo_Philips#E=MO%C2%B2_(1985)>)

## Example 3 · Mitch Hedberg

Reconsidering an offer of a frozen banana

> My friend asked me if I wanted a frozen banana, I said no, but I want a regular banana later, so…yeah.  

— Mitch Hedberg

**The turn:** He begins by refusing, then thinks through what the banana will become and changes his answer. The revision is the payoff, rather than an omitted piece of setup.

[Source](<https://barrypopik.com/blog/my_friend_asked_me_if_i_wanted_a_frozen_banana_joke>)

## What makes the structure work

Useful for an unreliable or oblivious persona. The audience must understand both what was said and why the speaker wants to change it. A correction that merely replaces one random word with another has little to reveal.
