*Accept a claim and follow it to a ridiculous consequence.*

A rule is accepted, then carried further than ordinary practice would allow. The conclusion is ridiculous but traceable: the audience can follow the chain and see exactly where the rule stops being useful.

## Example 1 · Steven Wright

Extending synchronization to a swimming team’s disaster

> If one synchronized swimmer drowns, do the rest have to drown too?  

— Steven Wright

**The turn:** A rule about coordination is extended beyond its proper scope.

[Source](<https://www.standupcomedyclinic.com/65-funny-one-liners-by-steven-wright/>)

## Example 2 · Demetri Martin

A medical emergency during a guessing game

> The worst time to have a heart attack is during a game of charades.  

— Demetri Martin

**The turn:** During charades, onlookers interpret gestures as clues; applying that habit to real distress could delay help. The consequence follows from the game’s interpretive rule, but it is a plausible misunderstanding rather than an impossible obligation. The dangerous outcome is largely supplied by the audience, also making this an Implied Punchline.

[Source](<https://www.fastcompany.com/1681887/advice-for-a-twitter-world-demetri-martin-on-how-to-be-succinct>)

## Example 3 · Steven Wright

Applying arithmetic to being scared half to death

> What happens if you get scared half to death twice?  

— Steven Wright

**The turn:** Treat the idiom as a literal quantity of death, then apply the ordinary rule that two halves make a whole. The question invites the absurd consequence of a second fright completing the death. Literal Interpretation supplies the premise; extending that premise supplies the consequence.

[Source](<https://www.tnonline.com/20120623/steven-wright-comedy-show-visits-penns-peak-sunday/>)

## What makes the structure work

Keep the reasoning legible. The pleasure comes from an excessively faithful application of a rule, not from announcing an arbitrary impossibility. Faulty reasoning can use a similar shape while failing at the inference itself.
