A category guide uses a photograph of one charcoal pitcher. Its reflections and glaze describe a specimen, while “Pitchers” needs a broader cue: body, pouring lip and handle.

Select the features or relationships needed for the task and let incidental appearance go. Abstraction can change viewpoint, turn surfaces into shapes or represent a process. Judge what survives, not how little ink remains.

## What to notice

![Two matching Pitchers guide cards retain the same text and layout. Before, a dark photographic pitcher shows glaze texture, highlights and shadow. After, a flat drawing retains a pouring lip, body, open loop handle, rim line and base line while removing surface detail.](../assets/graphic-design/abstraction-pitcher-category.svg "Only the representation changes inside the fixed 480×320 frame. The drawing interprets the object category; it is not an exact trace.")

[Full-size comparison](../assets/graphic-design/abstraction-pitcher-category.svg) · [Stacked narrow-screen view](../assets/graphic-design/abstraction-pitcher-category-mobile.svg)

Redraw the pitcher with broad body and handle shapes. Preserve lip, handle opening, rim and base lines; remove speckles, highlights and cast shadow. The frame and surrounding text stay fixed.

A general category depends less on one object’s finish while retaining structural cues. Internal lines remain because abstraction need not mean outer contour alone. The drawing regularizes the form and cannot describe a sale item’s actual glaze or precise proportions. Keep its label rather than assume a universal symbol.

## Two variations

### Summarize an arrangement rather than atmosphere

![Two matching Room for two cards retain identical descriptions and prices. Before, a photograph shows a table, two chairs, a plant, cup, notebook, window and door in perspective. After, a labeled conceptual plan retains the table, exactly two chairs, left window and right door while omitting decor and surface appearance.](../assets/graphic-design/abstraction-room-arrangement.svg "Only the photograph becomes a conceptual plan within the existing frame. Surrounding design stays fixed; this is neither a measured plan nor an accessibility-clearance drawing.")

[Full-size comparison](../assets/graphic-design/abstraction-room-arrangement.svg) · [Stacked narrow-screen view](../assets/graphic-design/abstraction-room-arrangement-mobile.svg)

Replace the room scene with a conceptual overhead view: one table, two chairs, left window and right door. Omit decor and perspective, preserving the card’s “Concept arrangement · Not to scale” qualification.

Count and relative position become explicit. The plan comes from the illustrative brief, not measurements recovered from a photo. Ordnance Survey’s generalisation guidance similarly selects by purpose while preserving important relationships. This clean drawing establishes no clearances, accessibility or exact geometry; those need a verified dimensioned plan. Photography may better convey room character.

### Turn materials into known task relationships

![Two matching Pack a seed envelope cards retain the same three numbered instructions. Before, a photograph presents seeds, envelopes, a pencil, tray and greenhouse surroundings in one still life. After, ten seed marks lead by an arrow to a labeled-envelope symbol, then by another arrow to a tray symbol.](../assets/graphic-design/abstraction-seed-packing.svg "Only the photograph becomes a schematic of ten seeds, one envelope and one tray. Its sequence comes from the written procedure, not observed photographic actions.")

[Full-size comparison](../assets/graphic-design/abstraction-seed-packing.svg) · [Stacked narrow-screen view](../assets/graphic-design/abstraction-seed-packing-mobile.svg)

Represent ten seeds, a labeled envelope and a return tray in the instruction order, joined with arrows. Remove greenhouse atmosphere and spare supplies while retaining the written procedure.

The graphic becomes a task model instead of a still life. Its sequence comes from supplied instructions, not a process discovered in the photograph. It still cannot show envelope folding, supply locations or acceptable seed condition. Optional or repeated operations would need further explanation.

## Useful when

Use abstraction for category recognition, spatial summaries or known processes when surface detail competes with the relationship. IBM advises purposeful essential icon elements; Larkin and Simon explain how diagram organization can make particular information and inferences accessible. Neither establishes that simple images always outperform photographs.

### Use Silhouettes

Use one solid tone when contour and meaningful openings carry enough identity. Preserve the source outline rather than improve it inventively. Restore internal detail when the task requires it: recognizing a speaker may need a portrait, not a generic solid head. If a sufficient silhouette disappears, change its ground rather than abstract further.

## Watch out for

Do not delete identifying openings or junctions. Flat styling can preserve every distraction, while a detailed drawing may best distinguish similar parts. Clean lines can imply unwarranted precision; label conceptual views and avoid invented dimensions.

Photography remains appropriate for material inspection, evidence, atmosphere and actual condition. Diagrams need recognizable forms, labels, contrast and equivalent accounts of complex relationships. Less detail alone cannot establish understanding or access.
