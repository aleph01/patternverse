*Treat an expression’s figurative or conventional meaning as literal.*

People routinely understand expressions through context rather than their most literal wording. This structure treats the literal reading as the practical instruction, creating a world where the familiar expression behaves strangely but consistently.

## Example 1 · Steven Wright

Spot remover

> I put spot remover on my dog, and now he’s gone.  

— Steven Wright

**The turn:** Spot removal becomes removal of the spotted animal.

[Source](<https://www.inquirer.com/philly/entertainment/celebrities/steven-wright-keswick-glenside-comedian-20180110.html>) · [Read the attributed joke text](<https://www.standupcomedyclinic.com/65-funny-one-liners-by-steven-wright/>)

## Example 2 · Emo Philips

A threat involving the speaker’s face

> I got into a fight one time with a really big guy, and he said, "I'm going to mop the floor with your face." I said, "You'll be sorry." He said, "Oh yeah? Why?" I said, "Well, you won't be able to get into the corners very well."  

— Emo Philips

**The turn:** He treats the threat as a practical cleaning proposal.

[Source](<https://www.dead-frog.com/comedians/jokes/emo-philips>)

## Example 3 · Steven Wright

Ordering breakfast

> I went into a restaurant. The menu said "breakfast any time." So I ordered French toast during the Renaissance.  

— Steven Wright

**The turn:** Flexible opening hours become unrestricted historical time.

[Source](<https://www.dead-frog.com/comedians/jokes/steven-wright>)

## What makes the structure work

Useful for exposing an idiom, slogan or imprecise promise. The literal consequence should follow from the actual words. A merely unrelated absurdity loses the pleasure of recognizing the linguistic trap.
