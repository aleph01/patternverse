*Deliver praise that contains or becomes an insult.*

Praise begins in a familiar social form, then its qualification reveals a low ranking, an insult or the speaker’s limited enthusiasm. The compliment gives the slight something to undermine.

## Example 1 · Norm Macdonald

Praising Bob Saget at his roast

> Bob has a beautiful face, like a flower. Yeah, a cauliflower  

— Norm Macdonald

**The turn:** The apparently flattering comparison resolves into an insult.

[Source](<https://kesq.com/entertainment/cnn-entertainment/2022/01/10/bob-saget-and-norm-macdonalds-enduring-friendship-2/>)

## Example 2 · Flight of the Conchords

The Most Beautiful Girl (In the Room)

The song adopts a seductive voice, then keeps restricting the praise. A supposedly exceptional woman is ranked against the people immediately around her and against local competition; even her potential modelling career is qualified. Each new standard makes the compliment smaller while the singer remains pleased with it.

— Flight of the Conchords

Praising Timothée Chalamet’s moustache

## What makes the structure work

The positive frame must be audible; a plain insult has no compliment to retract. The device can reveal the speaker’s tactlessness as much as it attacks the recipient. A whole song can sustain the same movement.
