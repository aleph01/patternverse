A studio uses elegant type consistently, but a collection reference makes capital I, lowercase l and numeral 1 hard to distinguish. Style has been preserved at the expense of a specific task.

Choose letterforms for what people must read, where they read it and what the communication should convey. Another family, another optical cut or a more appropriate role for the existing face may supply the answer.

## What to notice

![Two identical photographic studio-journal pages show A day at the wheel and two paragraphs. Before, the paragraphs use Source Serif 4’s display optical design with finer details. After, the same family’s text optical design gives the unchanged words sturdier strokes and different proportions; headings, line breaks and positions stay fixed.](../assets/graphic-design/typeface-reading-cut.svg "Only Source Serif 4’s optical size changes, 60→12 at weight 400. Nominal type size, line breaks, baselines and imagery stay fixed.")

[Full-size comparison](../assets/graphic-design/typeface-reading-cut.svg) · [Stacked narrow-screen view](../assets/graphic-design/typeface-reading-cut-mobile.svg)

The journal’s paragraphs use a display-oriented Source Serif 4 cut. Select its text-oriented optical design, moving the optical-size axis from 60 to 12 while preserving nominal type size, weight, breaks and baselines.

Sturdier details change the paragraph’s texture without adding boldness. Optical design alters the drawing and inherent spacing of the letters, rather than simply scaling them. The text cut is wider and less delicate; longer copy may reflow. Adobe treats optical-size names as suggestions, so proof the actual reading size and output.

## Two variations

### Give a headline a useful voice

![Two identical garden-supper invitations use the same evening photograph, date, price and booking details. Before, Garden supper is set in Jost Regular, a geometric sans serif. After, only that headline changes to a soft Fraunces instance with expressive serif forms, at the same nominal size and baseline.](../assets/graphic-design/typeface-event-tone.svg "Only the headline face changes from Jost Regular to Fraunces 400. Words, nominal size, baseline, alignment, imagery and practical details stay fixed.")

[Full-size comparison](../assets/graphic-design/typeface-event-tone.svg) · [Stacked narrow-screen view](../assets/graphic-design/typeface-event-tone-mobile.svg)

Replace only the garden-supper headline’s Jost with softened, regular-weight Fraunces. Keep logistics in their original face and preserve all sizes and positions.

Its varied contours and expressive serifs are an editorial fit for this informal shared-table brief, not a measured emotional effect. A venue with an established geometric identity might prefer Jost. Neither face makes the service inherently welcoming; test tone when the interpretation matters.

### Choose characters for exact transcription

![Two identical pottery-collection slips show the reference Il1O0-27. Before, Jost makes the capital I and lowercase l look very similar, and O resembles zero. After, Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono adds distinct upright forms and a slashed zero; the reference, size, baseline and surrounding information remain unchanged.](../assets/graphic-design/typeface-reference-code.svg "Only the reference face changes from Jost Regular to Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono Regular. The exact sequence, nominal size, baseline and surrounding design stay fixed.")

[Full-size comparison](../assets/graphic-design/typeface-reference-code.svg) · [Stacked narrow-screen view](../assets/graphic-design/typeface-reference-code-mobile.svg)

Set only “Il1O0-27” in Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono Regular. Its differentiated uprights and slashed zero address ambiguities exposed by a code with little linguistic context. Size and position stay fixed.

The field becomes wider and more mechanical, acceptable for a reference but not necessarily the whole invitation. Inspect the actual characters rather than assume every monospace face distinguishes them. Avoiding ambiguous characters during code generation may help too; this comparison reports no measured error reduction.

## Useful when

Compare faces with real copy: sustained prose, longest headings, codes, names and required scripts. Check character coverage, font delivery and permitted uses alongside reading conditions and visual voice. Same nominal sizes can look different because proportions, strokes and internal spaces differ.

### Pair Complementary Typefaces

Assign roles before adding a partner. An expressive display face can introduce an event while a quieter face handles logistics; one family with suitable cuts may suffice. Keep assignments stable through [[graphic-design-signal-relationships-through-similarity|Signal Relationships Through Similarity]].

## Watch out for

“Serif for print” and “sans for screens” skip the actual design, size and output. Mood cannot compensate for missing characters. A controlled comparison holds nominal settings fixed; a finished layout may need subsequent tuning without shrinking required text to restore old breaks.

No font is universally accessible. The Home Office warns against that claim; adaptable size, spacing and layout matter alongside letterform selection. Selection also cannot repair unclear language or inadequate text-background distinction.
