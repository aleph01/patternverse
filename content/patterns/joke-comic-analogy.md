*Use an unexpected comparison to make a recognizable relationship easier to see.*

A familiar experience can make an unfamiliar or awkward subject instantly legible. A comic analogy maps a revealing relationship between two situations, letting the audience import expectations from one into the other.

## Example 1 · Mitch Hedberg

The sensation of wearing a turtleneck

> A turtleneck is like being strangled by a really weak guy. All day.  

— Mitch Hedberg

**The turn:** A close-fitting collar becomes a weak but persistent attacker. The comparison captures both the pressure and its irritating duration.

[Source](<https://www.gq.com/gallery/mitch-hedberg-comedy-notebooks>)

## Example 2 · Jerry Seinfeld

Cremation and scattering ashes

> Cremation has become the most popular form of burial in the United States… People used to want a big, thick granite stone, their names carved into with a chisel. "I was here dammit!" Cremation is like you're trying to cover up a crime. "Burn the body. Scatter the ashes around. As far as anyone's concerned this whole thing never happened."  

— Jerry Seinfeld

**The turn:** A shared sequence of actions changes the ritual’s frame.

[Source](<https://www.dead-frog.com/comedians/jokes/jerry-seinfeld>)

## Example 3 · Mitch Hedberg

A tortilla wrapped around its filling

> A burrito is a sleeping bag for ground beef.  

— Mitch Hedberg

**The turn:** The tortilla maps to a sleeping bag and the filling to its occupant. The entire comparison and comic image fit into one sentence.

[Source](<https://stnicholasmd.org/download/newsletter/KOINONIA/02-KOINONIA_2026.pdf>)

## What makes the structure work

A strong comparison does more than announce resemblance: its shared logic supports the next turn. Keep track of what corresponds to what. If the comparison requires repeated explanation, its organizing picture may be too complicated.
