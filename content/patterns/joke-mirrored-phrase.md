*Reverse repeated words or elements so their new order changes the relationship.*

The same verbal elements return in a different order, reversing who acts on whom or what belongs to what. The sentence seems balanced while its meaning changes sharply. Its symmetry can make the turn especially compact.

## Example 1 · George Carlin

The same words reversed under television language rules

> You can say prick on television. If it happens to your finger it's alright. You can prick your finger, just don't finger your prick.  

— George Carlin

**The turn:** The same words reverse order, roles and acceptability.

[Source](<https://www.dead-frog.com/comedians/jokes/george-carlin>)

## Example 2 · Yakov Smirnoff

Party versus political party

> In America you can always find a party; in the Soviet Union the party always finds you.  

— Yakov Smirnoff

**The turn:** Swapping subject and object reverses who pursues whom.

[Source](<https://reason.com/1987/12/01/interview-with-yakov-smirnoff/>)

## Example 3 · Gallagher

The Sledge-O-Matic pitch swaps fingerprints and walls

> Sledge-O-Matic removes unwanted fingerprints from walls. Sledge-O-Matic also removes unwanted walls from fingerprints.  

— Gallagher

**The turn:** The first claim promises to remove a mark while preserving the wall. The second swaps the same two nouns: now the wall is removed instead. Their reversed grammatical positions turn a cleaning benefit into destructive absurdity. The mirrored phrase supplies the turn; the mock product pitch also parodies advertising.

[Source](<https://www.resilience.org/stories/2011-08-11/smashing-melon-american-complacency-mallet-russian-grit/>) · [Corroborating published attribution](<https://scomedy.com/quotes/Gallagher?page=2>)

## What makes the structure work

The reordered relationship must matter. A general opposite, contrast or reversal is not automatically this word-order construction. Word meaning may also change, as in Smirnoff’s party example, allowing two devices to work together.
