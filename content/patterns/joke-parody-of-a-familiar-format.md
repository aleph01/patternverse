*Reproduce a recognizable format with content that exposes or clashes with its conventions.*

A performance borrows a recognizable form—its language, rhythm, priorities and promises—then exposes what is formulaic or compromised about it. The audience enjoys both recognizing the format and seeing its rules betrayed.

## Example 1 · Bo Burnham

Country Song — Make Happy

Burnham introduces commercial country music as a marketable image of rural authenticity, then performs an arena-ready imitation. The simple vocabulary, familiar settings and larger-than-life production make the formula recognizable. The performance criticizes the genre by delivering a working example of the thing it is criticizing.

— Bo Burnham

## Example 2 · Tim Minchin

If I Didn’t Have You

The accompaniment and opening sentiment establish a sincere love song. The singer then reasons through how circumstances and chance could have produced a different relationship. Romantic devotion keeps giving way to a probability argument, while the arrangement continues to promise an emotional declaration.

— Tim Minchin

## Example 3 · Bo Burnham

Repeat Stuff

A polished pop performance appears to address an ideal romantic listener, then exposes the machinery behind that intimacy: generic attraction, repeated hooks and marketing to an audience. The music continues to provide the familiar pleasures of the format as the performer reveals how those pleasures are manufactured.

— Bo Burnham

## What makes the structure work

Identify the habits that make the form recognizable: its musical choices, recurring promises, expected emotional arc and relation to its audience. These examples use explanations beside the embedded recordings so the whole performance can demonstrate the structure without isolated lyric fragments.
