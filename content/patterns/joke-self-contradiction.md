*Undermine your own claim through another statement or action.*

A speaker states a principle, then violates it in the same breath or through the behaviour being described. The audience can hold the claim and the counterexample together, often before the speaker acknowledges either.

## Example 1 · George Carlin

Enforcing a principle of tolerance

> I say live and let live. That's my motto. Live and let live. Anyone who can't go along with that, take them outside and shoot the motherfucker.  

— George Carlin

**The turn:** The enforcement directly violates the announced principle.

[Source](<https://www.dead-frog.com/comedians/jokes/george-carlin>)

## Example 2 · Tommy Cooper

Indecision

> I used to be indecisive, but now I am not quite sure.  

— Tommy Cooper

**The turn:** The uncertainty cancels the opening claim.

[Source](<https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2016/jun/22/what-does-this-vote-mean-if-one-feels-utterly-powerless-in-every-other-way>)

## Example 3 · Emo Philips

Denying fatalism

> I’m not a fatalist, but even if I were, what could I do about it?  

— Emo Philips

**The turn:** The denial is delivered through the belief it rejects.

[Source](<https://www.emophilips.com/>)

## What makes the structure work

Make the conflict specific. A person having different moods is not automatically a contradiction. The claim and action should be incompatible under the rule the speaker has just established.
