*Withhold a detail, then reveal that the audience supplied the wrong one.*

A setup often lets listeners supply a detail the comedian never actually stated: who was injured, what an object is, or where the speaker stands. The payoff replaces that inferred detail. The joke briefly makes the audience responsible for its own mistaken picture.

## Example 1 · Mitch Hedberg

Ten days without sleep—or ten days of sleep

> I haven’t slept for ten days, because that would be too long.  

— Mitch Hedberg

**The turn:** We initially read ten days as the time elapsed without sleep. The ending makes it the duration of a single sleep instead.

## Example 2 · Emo Philips

Discovering his wife in bed with another man

> I discovered my wife in bed with another man and I was crushed. So I said, "Get off me, you two!"  

— Emo Philips

**The turn:** An emotional expression becomes literal and discloses his location.

[Source](<https://www.dead-frog.com/comedians/jokes/emo-philips>)

## Example 3 · Rita Rudner

A ballet career ends

> I started off as a ballerina. I had to quit ballet after I injured a groin muscle. It wasn't mine.  

— Rita Rudner

**The turn:** The audience has assigned the injury to the wrong person.

[Source](<https://www.dead-frog.com/comedians/jokes/rita-rudner>)

## What makes the structure work

Leave a plausible opening for the first interpretation, then give a decisive reason to revise it. Concealing every relevant fact creates confusion. The new reading should make the original sentence more precise.
