**Pattern 6 of 49 · Part 1: Start Making Songs**

**Use when:** You can make fragments but have never completed an intelligible musical arc.

A writer has studied many ways songs can work but has never chosen an ending. Every new idea increases the draft's possibilities and makes completion feel further away. A miniature changes the scale of the problem. Its small size allows a beginning, a development, and a return to become actual decisions.

Finish a Tiny Song means completing a limited musical arc with the materials already available. The result is a real small work, not a promise to write a larger one. It need not demonstrate every technique in the course.

## Working choice

Give a small idea a beginning, contrast, return, and ending before expanding its scale. A miniature is a complete small version, not a demand to fit every later song into the same form.

## Limit the commitment

Porch Light's first miniature uses sixteen bars in 4/4 at 84 quarter notes per minute. That lasts approximately 46 seconds, excluding any count-in or freely held final note. It uses the E4–G4–A4 seed, a short answer, and four familiar chords. These labels will receive fuller explanations later; the miniature shows how they can cooperate before they are mastered.

The structure has four four-bar passages: opening, answer, contrast, and return. The names describe jobs rather than a mandatory commercial form. There is no need to force a separate pre-chorus and bridge into a piece this short.

| Passage  | Chords, one bar each  | Musical contribution                 |
| -------- | --------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Opening  | C · Am · F · G        | Introduce the seed and leave it open |
| Answer   | C · Am · F · G        | Keep the rhythm, end more firmly     |
| Contrast | F · G · Am · G        | Move away from the opening setting   |
| Return   | C · Am · F then G · C | Recall the seed and settle on C      |

In the return's third bar, F lasts two beats and G lasts two beats. The other listed chords last four beats. The table therefore accounts for exactly sixteen bars. This miniature is distinct from the later eight-bar verse and eight-bar chorus of the full song.

## Give the opening a recognizable feature

The seed lasts one bar: E4 for one beat, G4 for one beat, and A4 for two. It can appear over the opening C chord, followed by a less busy bar. Repeating a long phrase immediately might overwhelm the small form; repeating this compact gesture establishes identity economically.

An answer can use G4–E4–D4 with the same one–one–two rhythm. That descent contrasts with the seed's rise. Because D over a G chord is a chord tone, placing the answer near the end of the opening phrase can leave the harmony ready for a return without requiring a new melodic vocabulary.

The musical question is simply whether the second gesture sounds connected to the first. It is not whether it satisfies an abstract quota of originality. Preserving the rhythm gives the two gestures a shared feature.

## Make one consequential contrast

The third passage begins on F rather than C. The accompaniment can hold longer notes while the melody reaches C5 briefly. Harmonic starting point, register, and density now differ from the opening. The contrast need not introduce an entirely unrelated tune.

A more restrained alternative keeps the melody in its original range and removes the accompaniment for one bar. That creates contrast through absence. Both options can work, but using them simultaneously might make it difficult to tell which contributes the more useful effect.

The contrast earns its place when the final return feels different because it happened. If the miniature sounds like three versions of an opening followed by a stop, its middle may need a clearer relationship to the ending rather than more notes.

## Choose an ending the phrase can support

The last bar uses a C chord and a sustained C5 in the melody. Compared with the seed's A4, this gives the miniature a clearer tonic arrival. A short rest after the note lets the ending occupy time instead of being immediately replaced by another loop.

This is one ending option, not a universal requirement. An unresolved final note could support a different intention. A fade could make the music seem to continue beyond the recording. For this first miniature, a definite closing gesture makes completion easy to recognize.

The important decision is accepting one version as the finished small work. Further possibilities can be saved separately. A miniature that expands indefinitely has stopped serving its intended constraint.

## A complete world in Diamond Day

Vashti Bunyan's **Diamond Day**, on [Just Another Diamond Day](https://music.apple.com/us/song/269891652), offers a compact vocal counterpoint to the course's instrumental miniature. Its successive short stanzas share a recognizable melodic setting and a modest acoustic frame. New images carry the listener through repeated musical material; the recording does not need a large contrasting bridge to make that repetition purposeful.

The useful comparison is proportion. A small amount of musical vocabulary can support a whole work when the phrases have clear relations and the ending feels chosen. Porch Light's sixteen-bar sketch takes a different route, using an explicit contrasting passage and return. Diamond Day is not evidence that Bunyan used that four-part plan, nor that a finished song must last only seconds. It shows why completing a limited idea need not mean producing a disposable exercise. A writer can enlarge the work later, but the current version should already offer something intelligible from beginning to end.

## What completing the miniature reveals

A finished short piece exposes relationships that a loop conceals: whether the opening is memorable after a contrast, whether the ending arrives too early, and whether the middle contributes anything. Those are compositional observations, not judgments about the writer's talent.

The same process can produce an instrumental sketch, a single verse with refrain, or a short vocal song. The materials may differ while the manageable arc remains. This pattern helps when drafts continually grow without becoming whole, or when technical learning has postponed actual composition. It is not a demand that all future songs be short.
