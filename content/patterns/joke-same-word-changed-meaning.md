*Repeat a word while changing its meaning: antanaclasis.*

A word returns, but its meaning changes. Repetition gives the sentence apparent continuity while the change of sense redirects it. The traditional rhetorical name is antanaclasis; the same operation can involve a repeated phrase.

## Example 1 · Mitch Hedberg

The military command and actual relaxation

> I never joined the army because "at ease" never seemed that easy to me. It seemed rather uptight, still. I do not relax by putting my arms behind my back and parting my legs slightly, that does not equal ease to me. At ease is not being in the military. I'm eased bro, cause I'm not in the military.  

— Mitch Hedberg

**The turn:** The repeated phrase changes from posture to relaxation.

[Source](<https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Mitch_Hedberg>) · [Read the Strategic Grill Locations transcription](<https://temporaryehliss.blogspot.com/2015/04/mitchhedberg-transcript.html>)

## Example 2 · Yakov Smirnoff

Party versus political party

> In America you can always find a party; in the Soviet Union the party always finds you.  

— Yakov Smirnoff

**The turn:** One repeated word changes meaning.

[Source](<https://reason.com/1987/12/01/interview-with-yakov-smirnoff/>)

## Example 3 · Jerry Seinfeld

The changing senses of left — stand-up routine excerpt in a TV interview upload

Seinfeld starts with being left-handed and complains that the word carries negative associations. He moves through awkward feet, an insulting compliment and leftover food, then arrives at an empty party: everyone has departed. The last use of the word changes it from the direction associated with his hand to the past tense of leaving.

— Jerry Seinfeld

**The turn:** The same audible word returns with different senses: left as a direction, inside familiar expressions and finally as a verb meaning departed. The repeated form holds the list together while its meaning changes. The empty-party payoff makes the switch especially clear; this is wordplay through repetition, with a tag sequence as an additional structure.

[Source](<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SK3H4MzPJ_Q>) · [Read Seinfeld’s own account of the joke](<https://www.wnycstudios.org/podcasts/tnyradiohour/articles/jerry-seinfeld-on-making-a-life-in-comedy-and-also-pop-tarts?tab=transcript>)

## What makes the structure work

Both meanings need to be recoverable. Merely mentioning the same thing twice is repetition, not this device. Seinfeld makes the change from a direction to the past tense of leaving explicit. Smirnoff’s example comes from advertising and remains identified as adjacent performed comedy.
