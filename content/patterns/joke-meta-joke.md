*Make the joke’s construction, writing or performance part of its subject.*

The making, telling or reception of the joke becomes part of its subject. A performer can expose an apparent writing decision, predict a laugh or comment on a failed line while continuing to produce comedy.

## Example 1 · Mitch Hedberg

Deciding whether a funny idea is worth recording

> See I write jokes for a living, man. You know, I sit in my hotel at night, I think of something that’s funny, then I go get a pen, and I write it down. Or, if the pen’s too far away, I have to convince myself that what I thought of – ain’t funny  

— Mitch Hedberg

**The turn:** Joke selection becomes the subject of a joke.

[Source](<https://kar.kent.ac.uk/62270/3/0%205th%20draft.pdf>) · [Read the source passage](<https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Mitch_Hedberg>)

## Example 2 · Bo Burnham

A water-bottle accident turns out to be scripted

Burnham knocks over his water bottle, apparently making a mistake. A prerecorded musical response immediately reveals that the spill was planned. The unexpected precision of the response exposes the trick: even this apparent interruption belongs to the act.

— Bo Burnham

## Example 3 · James Acaster

Kneeling lets a stand-up pretend his show has not started

Acaster delivers an extended opening while kneeling. His supposed loophole is that stand-up comedy cannot officially begin until he stands up, so the material already being performed does not count against the show’s running time. When he finally stands, he starts his watch.

— James Acaster

## What makes the structure work

A reference to comedy is not enough; the audience’s awareness of the performance should help create the payoff. Excess explanation can drain the joke it describes. The commentary needs a comic movement of its own.
