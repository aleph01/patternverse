*Establish a familiar expression, then change its expected continuation.*

A familiar expression arrives with most of its wording and authority intact, then changes direction. Recognition supplies part of the setup: listeners bring the conventional ending or moral, and the comedian bends it.

## Example 1 · Steven Wright

Leaving a body to science

> When I die, I’m gonna leave my body to science fiction.  

— Steven Wright

**The turn:** A familiar formula ends in an unexpected category.

[Source](<https://maximumfun.org/transcripts/bullseye-with-jesse-thorn/transcript-bullseye-with-jesse-thorn-steven-wright/>) · [Read the attributed joke text](<https://www.standupcomedyclinic.com/65-funny-one-liners-by-steven-wright/>)

## Example 2 · Mitch Hedberg

Giving up following his dreams

> I'm sick of following my dreams. I'm just going to ask where they're goin' and hook up with them later.  

— Mitch Hedberg

**The turn:** An inspirational phrase becomes a practical travel arrangement.

[Source](<https://www.dead-frog.com/comedians/jokes/mitch-hedberg>)

## Example 3 · Tim Vine

A once-in-a-lifetime holiday

> I’ve just been on a once-in-a-lifetime holiday. I’ll tell you what, never again.  

— Tim Vine

**The turn:** The travel cliché changes from praise into rejection.

[Source](<https://www.chortle.co.uk/news/2010/08/23/11598/comics_disrupt_bbc_news>) · [Read the published passage](<https://www.beyondthejoke.co.uk/content/12969/tim-vine-interview>)

## What makes the structure work

Preserve enough of the cliché for the audience to recognize what changed. A twist works best when it reveals a plausible contrary attitude rather than merely swapping in an unusual noun.
