*Describe undesirable behaviour using flattering language: paradiastole.*

An apparent defect receives an approving description. The new frame may reveal a real residual benefit, or expose the speaker’s determination to avoid admitting failure. The tension is between the condition and its label.

## Example 1 · Mitch Hedberg

A broken escalator

> I like an escalator, man. Because an escalator can never break. It can only become stairs.  

— Mitch Hedberg

**The turn:** A failure becomes a residual advantage.

[Source](<https://99percentinvisible.org/episode/episode-43-the-accidental-music-of-imperfect/transcript/>)

## Example 2 · Rita Rudner

Buying a $300 item for $150

> Men and women we look at money so differently. Men look at how much money is spent. Women look at how much money is saved. If it just cost $300 and if I get it for $150 what have I done ladies? Saved $150. Of course. My husband thinks I've spent $150. What's it like in there?  

— Rita Rudner

**The turn:** Spending receives the language of thrift.

[Source](<https://www.dead-frog.com/comedians/jokes/rita-rudner>)

## Example 3 · Mitch Hedberg

Dental cavities — Strategic Grill Locations

He introduces cavities in his teeth, then advertises them as places to store things. The dental defect becomes available storage space, complete with an offer to accommodate a pea.

— Mitch Hedberg

**The turn:** A defect is marketed as useful extra capacity.

[Source](<https://temporaryehliss.blogspot.com/2015/04/mitchhedberg-transcript.html>)

## What makes the structure work

Make the alternative description understandable enough to compete with the obvious one. Functional reframing and dishonest self-justification are related variations, not identical attitudes. The surrounding persona tells the audience which is operating.
