A product promotion draws a complete circle over a pitcher, adding a line across the ceramic surface buyers need to inspect. Let the pitcher cover the overlapping arc: enough curvature can remain to suggest a circle behind it.

Leave part of a form hidden or undrawn while preserving useful evidence for the whole. Corners, curves and familiar identifying features constrain the interpretation. Some forms appear to continue behind an object; others suggest an extent without a crisp illusory edge. These are related, not identical effects.

## What to notice

![Before and after workshop invitations keep the same photograph and offer details. Before, a complete rectangle surrounds the offer. After, the middle of each edge disappears, leaving four fixed corner fragments that suggest the same rectangular field.](../assets/graphic-design/implied-workshop-frame.svg "Only four middle edge segments disappear. Corners, overall extent, stroke style, text and imagery stay fixed.")

[Full-size comparison](../assets/graphic-design/implied-workshop-frame.svg) · [Stacked narrow-screen view](../assets/graphic-design/implied-workshop-frame-mobile.svg)

Hide only the middle of each edge around a workshop offer. Four corners retain their 60-unit arms and original rectangular extent; every word stays fixed. The same field is suggested with less continuous linework.

Corners and aligned content support its scope, but the boundary is less explicit. A form field, cut line or exact diagram region may deserve the full outline. The original is not inherently wrong: this brief calls for a lighter field.

Kellman and Shipley’s interpolation theory and subsequent boundary-clarity experiments support attending to the evidence that remains. They do not establish a universal percentage of any logo or icon that may be removed.

## Two variations

### Let the object account for the missing contour

![Before and after product promotions use the identical pitcher cutout, ground, and copper circle. Before, the full circle is drawn over the pitcher. After, the same circle sits behind it, leaving visible arcs that suggest its hidden continuation.](../assets/graphic-design/implied-product-circle.svg "Only the circle’s layer changes. Its geometry and styling, the product photograph, placement and surrounding design stay fixed.")

[Full-size comparison](../assets/graphic-design/implied-product-circle.svg) · [Stacked narrow-screen view](../assets/graphic-design/implied-product-circle-mobile.svg)

Place the same circle beneath the same pitcher cutout. Neither geometry nor placement changes. Visible arcs now suggest a circle interrupted by an object in front, leaving the ceramic available for inspection.

Too little exposed contour might become an unrelated flourish. More visible arc or another motif could then be needed. Intentional overprinting could justify the original. Both versions retain the same partly transparent generated cutout, including subtle compositing traces.

### Restore identifying evidence instead of removing more

![Before and after download screens keep the filename, PDF specification, button, photograph, and layout fixed. Before, disconnected icon corners suggest a plain rectangular frame. After, an angled upper corner and visible internal fold suggest a document while other gaps remain.](../assets/graphic-design/implied-print-file.svg "Only the upper-right contour changes and a fold stroke appears. Overall extent, remaining fragments, labels and interface stay fixed.")

[Full-size comparison](../assets/graphic-design/implied-print-file.svg) · [Stacked narrow-screen view](../assets/graphic-design/implied-print-file-mobile.svg)

A fragmented rectangular icon offers little evidence of a document. Replace its upper-right square corner with an angled corner and short fold stroke. Keep its overall extent, lower fragments, gaps, line weight and position.

The added fold supports a familiar document convention while much of the perimeter remains implied. Recognition is still an audience question. A complete standard icon may work better at small sizes, and the filename, “PDF · A3 · 2.4 MB” and “Download PDF” label remain necessary.

## Useful when

Use implied forms when a continuous graphic perimeter adds unnecessary weight or crosses useful content, and fragments can still support a coherent whole. An intervening object can explain an interruption more plausibly than arbitrary gaps.

The intervention may add a diagnostic feature, not always subtract one. Judge the remaining evidence rather than the number of strokes saved. Nielsen Norman Group similarly recommends testing icon understanding and using clear labels.

## Watch out for

Do not remove the notch, fold or internal mark that distinguishes the object. Fragments with incompatible directions can support several competing forms. Thin gaps and strokes can disappear in reproduction, so inspect actual sizes and consider fuller variants.

This pattern concerns graphic form. It cannot justify leaving fees, conditions or instructions for readers to reconstruct. Recognizing a sheet-like shape also does not establish knowledge of its format, action or consequence.
