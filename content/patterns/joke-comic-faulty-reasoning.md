*Confidently draw an unjustified conclusion from recognizable evidence.*

A speaker reasons carefully from a mistaken model, confuses categories or draws a conclusion that does not follow. The chain remains understandable enough for the audience to locate the error and enjoy the speaker’s confidence.

## Example 1 · Demetri Martin

Trophies as evidence of skill

> I used to play sports. Then I realized you can buy trophies. Now I’m good at everything.  

— Demetri Martin

**The turn:** He treats possession of the prize as equivalent to the achievement it usually represents.

[Source](<https://demetrimartin.wordpress.com/2010/05/26/australias-got-talent-hopeful-rips-off-demetri-martin/>)

## Example 2 · Emo Philips

The brain judges its own importance

> I used to think that the brain was the most wonderful organ in my body. Then I realized who was telling me this.  

— Emo Philips

**The turn:** The first judgement relies on an interested source: the organ being praised also supplies the praise. The punchline notices that weak basis and withdraws confidence in it. Faulty reasoning is exposed rather than proudly continued, with Comic Self-Correction supplying the final movement.

[Source](<https://www.cms.org/lies-my-brain-tells-me/>) · [Attribution history](<https://quoteinvestigator.com/2021/01/10/brain-said/>)

## Example 3 · Mitch Hedberg

A floating cocktail lime as protection against drowning

> Look at all the limes in this god damn thing! This fuckin' thing is tropical! Look at the limes, how they float. That's good news. Next time I'm on a boat and it capsizes, I will reach for a lime. Like I'll be water-skiing without a life preserver, people will say "What the hell?" and I'll pull out a lime. I'm saved by the buoyancy of citrus.  

— Mitch Hedberg

**The turn:** He confuses buoyancy with adequate support.

[Source](<https://kit548emt.blogspot.com/2009/04/mitch-all-together-mitch-hedberg.html>)

## What makes the structure work

A bad conclusion is not necessarily faulty reasoning: it might be selfish but logically sound. Identify the actual mistaken inference. The most useful examples reveal why the speaker finds the argument convenient or persuasive.
