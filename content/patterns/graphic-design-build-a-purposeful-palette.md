A programme gives nearly every detail a new color. The swatches are attractive, but readers cannot tell whether the differences mean categories, priorities or decoration.

Choose the relationships the palette must support, then decide which colors earn a place. Limiting swatches, using neighboring hues and using opposing hues solve different problems; none is a universal improvement.

## What to notice

![Two open-studios posters use the same pottery photograph and identical text. Before, the invitation, date, admission information and address each have a different dark hue. After, these four lines share the existing dark green. The terracotta Open studios title remains unchanged.](../assets/graphic-design/palette-open-studios.svg "Only four text fills change to existing dark green. Assigned ink colors reduce from six to two, excluding photographic and paper colors.")

[Full-size comparison](../assets/graphic-design/palette-open-studios.svg) · [Stacked narrow-screen view](../assets/graphic-design/palette-open-studios-mobile.svg)

Change the open-studios invitation, date, admission and address to its existing dark green. Keep the terracotta title, wording, typography, positions and photograph. Six assigned inks become two.

Supporting information now shares a compact vocabulary while existing size and placement preserve its roles. Some playful variety disappears. If the original hues identified venues with matching signs, consolidating them would remove useful navigation; this fictional event has no such code.

## Two variations

### Connect related sessions through neighboring hues

![Matching workshop programmes show the same pottery photograph and three labeled session bands. Before, Clay bowl is sand, Print a tote cyan and Bookbinding lilac. After, the bands are teal, cyan and blue. All workshop names, times, prices, positions and typography remain unchanged.](../assets/graphic-design/analogous-workshop-family.svg "Only the outer session surfaces change to neighboring HSL hues near 170°/190°/210°. Central cyan, approximate luminance/saturation, content and geometry stay fixed.")

[Full-size comparison](../assets/graphic-design/analogous-workshop-family.svg) · [Stacked narrow-screen view](../assets/graphic-design/analogous-workshop-family-mobile.svg)

Retain the middle cyan band and move sand toward teal and lilac toward blue. Keep three bands, their dimensions, text and approximate saturation/luminance relationships fixed.

Teal–cyan–blue reinforces the shared programme without reducing its number of swatches. Names, times and prices still identify sessions. The original eclectic palette could suit independent activity identities, especially where established colors recur elsewhere. Color should not sever those connections merely to look harmonious.

### Distinguish peers through opposing hue families

![Two identical studio reports show directly labeled horizontal bars for Riverside and Hill. Before, Riverside uses blue and Hill a neighboring blue-violet. After, Hill alone uses muted orange in both months. The values 42, 28, 48 and 36, common scales and all geometry remain fixed.](../assets/graphic-design/complement-studio-categories.svg "Only Hill’s bars change from blue-violet to muted orange, opposite Riverside’s blue on the specified HSL wheel. Approximate luminance, values and chart geometry stay fixed.")

[Full-size comparison](../assets/graphic-design/complement-studio-categories.svg) · [Stacked narrow-screen view](../assets/graphic-design/complement-studio-categories-mobile.svg)

Keep Riverside blue and change Hill to muted orange in both report panels, approximately preserving relative luminance. Values, bar lengths, labels and scales stay fixed: March remains 42/28 sessions and April 48/36.

The orange, which appears brown at this darkness, supplies a hue distinction without giving Hill greater area or rank. It does not mean “better” or “urgent.” Similar luminance may make the hues converge in grayscale, and complements are not distinguishable to every viewer. Direct studio labels remain essential. A quieter report could use neighboring hues or one common bar color.

## Useful when

Build the palette when arbitrary hues imply unintended categories, related material lacks a shared character or peers need a more deliberate distinction. Preserve identity and functional colors with genuine jobs.

A color wheel describes relationships; it does not establish mood or readable text. Lightness, saturation, area, neighboring colors and reproduction affect the result. The same set also needs stable assignments across views: selection cannot repair inconsistent use.

## Watch out for

Do not chase a prescribed color count at the cost of status or product distinctions. A multicolored design can be appropriate when its variety has a clear purpose. An opposing accent may overwhelm through area or saturation; neighboring hues may become too similar. Adjust the actual distribution rather than trust a theoretical label.

Test necessary foreground/background pairs and provide meaning beyond hue. A grayscale preview can expose dependence, but is not a complete accessibility or print-production test. Preserve documentary imagery rather than recolor it for superficial palette unity.
