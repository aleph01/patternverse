*Make the same expression support two meanings.*

One expression does two jobs. Its ordinary reading fits the setup, while another meaning supplies the comic turn. The phrase may be suggestive, but sexual content is optional: ambiguity is the essential ingredient.

## Example 1 · Steven Wright

Shopping at a general store

> I went to the General store, they wouldn’t let me buy anything specific.  

— Steven Wright

**The turn:** General shifts from shop category to opposite of specific.

[Source](<https://www.standupcomedyclinic.com/65-funny-one-liners-by-steven-wright/>)

## Example 2 · Tim Vine

Selling a vacuum cleaner

> I’ve decided to sell my Hoover… well, it was just collecting dust.  

— Tim Vine

**The turn:** The complaint also describes the appliance doing its job.

[Source](<https://www.beyondthejoke.co.uk/content/12969/tim-vine-interview>)

## Example 3 · Jimmy Carr

An accusation of infidelity

> My girlfriend said, "Have you been having sex behind my back?"  
>
> I said, "Who did you think it was?"  

— Jimmy Carr

**The turn:** The same phrase supports betrayal and location.

[Source](<https://www.dead-frog.com/comedians/jokes/jimmy-carr>)

## What makes the structure work

Choose an expression whose two readings are both intelligible in context. Explaining the dictionary meaning after the turn usually repeats work the audience has already done. Unlike repeated-word wordplay, the crucial expression need only appear once.
