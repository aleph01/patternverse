*Give an object, institution or abstraction human motives or behaviour.*

An object, animal or abstract system receives human motives, feelings or conversational habits. Its ordinary behaviour becomes evidence of a personality, allowing an everyday observation to turn into a relationship or scene.

## Example 1 · Steven Wright

Why he stopped driving

> I had to stop driving my car for a while, because the tires got dizzy.  

— Steven Wright

**The turn:** The rotating objects acquire a human bodily response.

[Source](<https://www.standupcomedyclinic.com/65-funny-one-liners-by-steven-wright/>)

## Example 2 · Jerry Seinfeld

Imagining a racehorse’s thoughts

> I don’t think horses know they are racing. I think the horses are standing there thinking. “I know there’s a bag of oats at the end of this trail and I want to get there first.  

— Jerry Seinfeld

**The turn:** Human-style motivation is assigned to animals: anthropomorphism.

[Source](<https://weekend-workshop.s3.amazonaws.com/13%20COMEDY%20STRUCTURES.pdf>)

## Example 3 · Mitch Hedberg

Questioning an oscillating fan

> I've got an oscillating fan at my house. The fan goes back and forth. It looks like the fan is saying "No". So I like to ask it questions that a fan would say no to. Do you keep my hair in place? Do you keep my documents in order? Do you have three settings? Liar! My fan fucking lied to me. Now I will pull the pin up. Now you ain't sayin' shit.  

— Mitch Hedberg

**The turn:** An appliance becomes an evasive conversational partner.

[Source](<https://kit548emt.blogspot.com/2009/04/mitch-all-together-mitch-hedberg.html>)

## What makes the structure work

Anchor the invented personality in something observable. A fan’s movement or an animal’s repeated action can supply that starting point. Once established, the personality should produce consequences rather than remain a decorative description.
