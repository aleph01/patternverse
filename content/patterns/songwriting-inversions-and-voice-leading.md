**Pattern 32 of 49 · Part 6: Bass Movement and Harmonic Color**

**Use when:** A progression has suitable chords, but every change jumps heavily or interrupts the vocal line.

A chord progression seems to jump around even though each chord sounds fine. The player is using the same root-position shape on every root, moving the whole hand considerable distances. Keeping some notes near their previous positions can create a different connection without changing the progression itself.

## The move

Keep useful common tones and move the remaining voices deliberately while giving the bass its own path.

Start with C–E–G and move to A–C–E. Keep C and E in the same upper registers while G moves to A, rather than lifting the whole hand to a new root-position block. Let a separate bass supply C then A if those roots are wanted below. Compare that continuity with an intentionally wider leap at the chorus boundary. The chord names alone cannot record this distinction; note the essential bass and upper movement when it defines the arrangement.

Smooth motion is a choice, not a law; a large jump can mark a boundary, and the lowest sounding note determines inversion.

## Separate root from bass

An inversion identifies which chord member is in the bass. Voice leading describes how individual musical lines move from one harmony to the next. Voicing describes a chord's spacing and arrangement. The three ideas interact, but they answer different questions.

A C-major triad contains C, E, and G. With C lowest, it is in root position. With E lowest, it is first inversion, commonly charted C/E. With G lowest, it is second inversion, C/G. The root remains C in all three.

A slash chord does not always indicate an inversion. C/D asks for a C chord over D, which is not a C-triad member. It creates another combined sonority. The chart should make clear whether the slash requests a bass note, not a second complete chord.

Inversions do not prescribe every upper note. C/E can be voiced in several octaves and spacings. Two performances can therefore share an inversion while sounding quite different.

## Follow three voices

An original close-position connection moves from C major to Am:

| Voice  | C chord | Am chord | Motion         |
| ------ | ------- | -------- | -------------- |
| Low    | C4      | C4       | Remains        |
| Middle | E4      | E4       | Remains        |
| High   | G4      | A4       | Rises one tone |

The second upper structure is Am with C lowest in these three voices, so it is first inversion if no lower bass overrides it. A separate bass playing A would instead make the whole sonority root-position Am. The full texture determines the bass, not the keyboard hand alone.

![Three voice-leading paths show two common tones held while G moves to A.](/diagrams/songwriting/voice-leading.svg 'Follow each line separately. A small change between chords can coexist with a different bass movement below.')

This distinction matters in arrangement. A keyboard player can preserve common tones while the bass moves through roots. The result combines smooth upper lines with clear low harmonic direction.

## Apply the connection to Porch Light

The verse moves C–Am–F–G, each lasting two bars. A root-position keyboard version may jump down for Am and again for F. A smoother version can keep C and E through the first change, then retain A and C as Am becomes F. The third moving voice supplies the new chord identity.

A deliberate bass alternative uses C–B–A–G under C, G/B, Am, and C/G. The upper chord sequence has changed as well as the bass pattern. This is an alternative progression, not merely another voicing of C–Am–F–G. Naming that difference prevents a misleading comparison.

Smoothness is not always the goal. Parallel block movement can create a strong unified gesture. Large leaps can establish contrast or instrumental character. Voice-leading analysis makes the motion visible; it does not forbid it.

## God Only Knows: follow the low line as well as the chord

The Beach Boys' **God Only Knows**, in the [official Pet Sounds audio](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DGOhIhgZxFo) ([official album context](https://thebeachboys.com/blogs/news/the-beach-boys-on-pet-sounds-the-beach-boys)), rewards hearing the opening accompaniment and verse as several connected lines. The bass supplies a path through the harmony while upper sounds sustain, answer, or move differently. Following that low path alone gives a different account from listening only for a succession of chord names.

A listening impression of smoothness is a starting observation, however, not proof that every upper voice moves by the smallest interval. The original C-to-Am comparison makes the narrower claim checkable: keep C and E fixed in the upper parts while G moves to A, with a separate C-to-A bass. Chord symbols alone could describe a much wider version too. God Only Knows supplies the musical context for attending to those separate strands; the course example supplies the exact note comparison. When a bass path or inner line defines an arrangement, record that line explicitly rather than expecting the chord names to reproduce it. A detailed reduction of the Beach Boys recording would need its actual voicings.

## Check the important lines

A held melody may share a note with the new chord or create a tension. An inner voice may move by semitone while the bass leaps. A guitarist may be unable to realize the exact keyboard spacing. These practical conditions influence which connection is useful.

Low-register voicings also need care. Several close notes far down an instrument can produce a dense sound even when their abstract voice leading is smooth. A more open spacing may preserve clarity while retaining the same chord members.

This vocabulary helps when accompaniment feels jumpy, when a bass line should descend under changing chords, or when a fixed melody needs a subtler setting.
