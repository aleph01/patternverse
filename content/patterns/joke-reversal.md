*Establish a direction or expectation, then turn it toward its opposite.*

A sentence seems to establish the direction of a story; the next makes it point the other way. A reversal changes a relationship, motive or outcome while preserving enough of the setup for the turn to feel earned.

## Example 1 · Mitch Hedberg

Past drug use

> I used to do drugs. I still do, but I used to, too.  

— Mitch Hedberg


*1:37–1:46*

**The turn:** The past tense invites us to assume he quit; the next sentence cancels that assumption. His behaviour never changes. The reversal is of our expectation, so Assumption Shift is the more precise primary label.

## Example 2 · Anthony Jeselnik

Mother opposes buying a motorcycle after her brother’s fatal accident

> When I finished high school I wanted to take all my graduation money and buy myself a motorcycle. Buy my mom said no. See, she had a brother who died in a horrible motorcycle accident when he was 18. And I could just have his motorcycle.  

— Anthony Jeselnik

**The turn:** An apparent safety objection becomes a cost-saving offer.

[Source](<https://www.dead-frog.com/comedians/jokes/anthony-jeselnik>)

## Example 3 · Rita Rudner

Shopping for a new outfit after a breakup

> One time I love to shop is after a bad relationship. I go and buy a new outfit and it makes me feel better. Sometimes if I see a really great outfit I'll break up with someone on purpose.  

— Rita Rudner

**The turn:** The remedy becomes the reason for the problem.

[Source](<https://www.dead-frog.com/comedians/jokes/rita-rudner>)

## What makes the structure work

Useful when an observation has an obvious conclusion. The stronger alternative makes the earlier wording newly meaningful. An assumption shift changes what listeners believe happened; a reversal can instead change what happens next.
