*Respond earnestly where the audience recognizes a misunderstanding.*

A speaker approaches the wrong problem with wholehearted seriousness. The tone promises a reasonable contribution while the reasoning reveals a misunderstanding, an unsuitable standard or a radically misjudged priority.

## Example 1 · Steven Wright

Racing drivers’ apparent hurry

> I watched the Indy 500 and I was thinking if they left earlier, they wouldn’t have to go so fast.  

— Steven Wright

**The turn:** He earnestly treats racing as delayed travel.

[Source](<https://www.standupcomedyclinic.com/65-funny-one-liners-by-steven-wright/>)

## Example 2 · Sarah Silverman

Taking health advice the wrong way

> Someone told me that carrots are good for your eyes. What they failed to tell me is that you have to take them orally.  

— Sarah Silverman

**The turn:** The persona earnestly reveals a basic misunderstanding.

[Source](<https://www.dead-frog.com/comedians/jokes/sarah-silverman>)

## Example 3 · Mitch Hedberg

Explaining blurry photographs

> I think Bigfoot is blurry, that’s the problem. It’s not the photographer’s fault.  

— Mitch Hedberg

**The turn:** He earnestly defends the photographer by making blur a property of the creature.

[Source](<https://temporaryehliss.blogspot.com/2015/04/mitchhedberg-transcript.html>)

## What makes the structure work

Let the persona’s commitment remain intelligible. Performing stupidity without a consistent point of view gives the audience less to recognize. Misplaced focus concerns what receives attention; misplaced sincerity concerns how earnestly the mistaken response is offered.
