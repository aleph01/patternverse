*Put contrasting elements next to one another so the mismatch becomes the joke.*

Two things become funny through being placed together: a public image beside private behaviour, a prestigious achievement beside an embarrassing distinction, or a fantasy beside mundane obligation. The audience notices the mismatch.

## Example 1 · Jerry Seinfeld

The pictured couples on greeting cards

> They have the greeting cards with the couples on the front. They photograph them. These hazy focus people. They're always having picnics. There's always a tree, a pond… who are these people? I don't know them. I don't want them on my card either. What am I going to write inside there anyway? "Here's another couple having a better relationship than us."  

— Jerry Seinfeld

**The turn:** The image and the implied real relationship clash.

[Source](<https://www.dead-frog.com/comedians/jokes/jerry-seinfeld>)

## Example 2 · Louis C.K.

Sexual mystery beside domestic routine

> Who wants to blow their husband? You want to blow a guy that you've been dating. And he's mysterious and you suck his cock and go home. Who wants to blow a guy and then go to Ikea with him all day?  

— Louis C.K.

**The turn:** Erotic fantasy meets mundane domestic obligation.

[Source](<https://www.dead-frog.com/comedians/jokes/louis-ck>)

## Example 3 · Trevor Noah

American dominance in several categories

> Does America really need to be the best at everything? You already dominate the world in economics, military power and obesity.  

— Trevor Noah

**The turn:** An undesirable distinction joins two prestigious ones.

[Source](<https://officialjimmycarr.substack.com/p/problems-problems-problems-i-see>)

## What makes the structure work

Choose details whose relationship exposes an attitude or contradiction. Unrelated things can be surprising without producing a clear comparison. An analogy stresses a shared relationship; juxtaposition often stresses the distance between its subjects.
