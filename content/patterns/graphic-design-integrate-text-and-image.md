A newsletter shows clay-making and finished tableware, but places both offer blocks far below, after a general introduction. The pairing is sensible; readers must still leave the pictures and look back to connect them.

Coordinate what words and pictures contribute, which elements refer to one another and where they meet. Integration can mean adjacency, an exact leader or moving text away from a face. It does not require overlap.

## What to notice

[Full-size comparison](../assets/graphic-design/integration-newsletter-captions.svg) · [Stacked narrow-screen view](../assets/graphic-design/integration-newsletter-captions-mobile.svg)

Move each complete offer directly beneath its photograph, preserving columns, wording, typography and internal spacing. Images and introduction stay fixed. The hands meet workshop dates and the £28 price; the finished set meets its separate £24 offer.

Readers can find complementary information locally instead of maintaining a connection across intervening copy. The bottom grows emptier, which need not be filled. An editorial feature could intentionally delay commercial details. These are separate offers: the set is not presented as a class outcome.

Moreno and Mayer found contiguity benefits in particular instructional materials. This newsletter is an editorial application, not an experimentally validated prediction about sales or comprehension.

## Two variations

### Give a label an exact referent

![Two matching product-detail sheets show the same dark pitcher with Pouring lip and Loop handle labels above it. Before, the labels have no explicit connection to the object. After, two thin elbow leaders connect them to the lip and handle opening. Product size, position, source image and all text remain unchanged.](../assets/graphic-design/integration-pitcher-labels.svg "Only two leaders appear, ending at lip and handle opening. Product image, scale, placement and wording stay fixed; no product-performance claim is added.")

[Full-size comparison](../assets/graphic-design/integration-pitcher-labels.svg) · [Stacked narrow-screen view](../assets/graphic-design/integration-pitcher-labels-mobile.svg)

Add restrained elbow leaders from “Pouring lip” and “Loop handle” to their actual features. Keep labels, pitcher and specifications fixed; endpoints remain distinct and avoid unnecessary crossings.

A beginner gets a location rather than merely a vocabulary list. The lines do not prove drip-free pouring or comfortable handling. They also make the sheet more diagrammatic. A lifestyle advertisement may need neither; crowded or tiny endpoints may call for a detail view instead.

### Preserve what the portrait contributes

![Two matching studio-conversation posters reuse the same portrait and cream title panel. Before, the panel covers the person’s face. After, the unchanged panel moves down over the lower apron area, revealing the face. The photograph, crop, words, typography, colors and paper presentation remain fixed.](../assets/graphic-design/integration-maker-poster.svg "Only the 440×155 title panel moves, y320→y590. Text and portrait stay fixed; apron detail is covered instead of the face.")

[Full-size comparison](../assets/graphic-design/integration-maker-poster.svg) · [Stacked narrow-screen view](../assets/graphic-design/integration-maker-poster-mobile.svg)

Move the complete opaque “Meet the maker” panel down over the apron. Keep its fill, dimensions and type, with portrait and crop fixed. The invitation stays legible while the fictional speaker’s face becomes visible.

Apron detail is sacrificed, which could matter in a workwear story. An obscured face may be an intentional concept under another brief. Here the words introduce a conversation and the portrait supplies its human subject; integration negotiates their space.

## Useful when

Use this when viewers must connect verbal information to a scene, feature or offer. Choose whether the relationship needs proximity, a precise endpoint or protection from overlap. A relevant image can still be assigned to the wrong words; alignment alone cannot establish that pairing.

Exact-feature leaders are an annotation technique, not decoration. A label covering its own referent or a line ending between features defeats the relationship. Text over photography also needs contrast against its actual local ground.

## Watch out for

A caption saying only “a pitcher” may add little, though deliberate identification and accessibility can justify repetition. Explain a feature, context or useful contribution rather than describe the obvious automatically.

On phones, keep captions with their images instead of stacking all pictures before all descriptions. Complex images also need text alternatives conveying essential information and relationships. Visual adjacency does not establish that nonvisual account or a meaningful implemented reading order.
