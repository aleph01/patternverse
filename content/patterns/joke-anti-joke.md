*Invite the expectation of a conventional joke, then deliberately frustrate it.*

A familiar comic situation promises the normal kind of payoff, then deliberately withholds it or substitutes something pointedly inadequate. The audience’s recognition of the broken contract supplies the comic event.

## Example 1 · Norm Macdonald

An intentionally corny roast insult

> You’ll never be over the hill, not in the car you drive.  

— Norm Macdonald

**The turn:** The sentence itself is a conventional car-and-age pun. The anti-comedy operates at the scale of the roast: deliberately corny material frustrates an expectation of aggressive contemporary insults. The cited participants’ recollections establish that framing; the isolated line does not establish an anti-joke on its own.

[Source](<https://www.maxim.com/entertainment/burned-oral-fing-history-comedy-central-roast/>)

## Example 2 · Norm Macdonald

A fat-joke setup receives an ordinary medical answer — video-podcast exchange

Macdonald recounts a doctor calling him fat, then asks how fat he is—the familiar invitation to an exaggerated insult. The answer is instead a straightforward warning about a heart attack or stroke. When his companion objects that this is not funny and says he expected a joke, Macdonald treats expecting comedy from a cardiologist as the misunderstanding.

— Norm Macdonald

**The turn:** The setup activates a recognizable joke formula, but its usual exaggerated punchline is deliberately replaced with an ordinary medical answer. The follow-up explicitly exposes the listener’s frustrated expectation, making the broken comic contract the payoff. This demonstrates an anti-joke within the exchange itself; it also becomes a Meta-Joke when the missing joke is discussed.

## Example 3 · Bo Burnham

An announced song lasts only a few screamed moments

At the piano, Burnham announces A World on Fire as though introducing a normal song. Instead of developing a melody or story, he produces a brief burst of screaming and banging on the keys, then moves on. The title has been fulfilled with almost absurd literalness.

— Bo Burnham

## What makes the structure work

The missing or inadequate payoff needs to be recognizable as a choice. The doctor exchange prepares an exaggerated fat joke and supplies a literal answer instead; the corny anti-roast and drastically shortened song frustrate expectations at performance scale. Identify the promised payoff and how it is withheld. Simply writing an unsuccessful joke does not create the same effect, and a final comment on the failure can add a Meta-Joke.
