A studio duplicates last month’s notice. The longer title clips, a reduced-price condition lands beneath the booking address, and a review inherits an old photograph. The file can be copied; its behavior with new content was never designed.

Build a template around **content roles and responses to variation**: what editors replace, what must stay together, and what happens when content is longer, conditional or absent. Approved variants in a labeled design file can do this without automation.

## What to notice

![Before and after sets show the same short-title and long-title workshop notices. The short notice stays unchanged. Before, the long notice’s two-line title window hides “a folded rim.” After, the title window expands to show the third line, and its photograph and practical details move down together. All words, type sizes and photographic crops remain fixed.](../assets/graphic-design/template-variable-titles.svg "The short input stays fixed. Only the long title’s window and downstream group change, gaining/shifting 44 units; wording, type sizes, crop and notice dimensions stay fixed.")

[Full-size comparison](../assets/graphic-design/template-variable-titles.svg) · [Paired narrow-screen views](../assets/graphic-design/template-variable-titles-mobile.svg)

The short “Clay bowl” notice fits. “Hand-building a serving bowl with a folded rim” needs a third title line, but the copied two-line window hides its ending. Checking only the original input misses the failure.

Let the title band gain 44 units and move the photograph and practical details down together. The full title appears while type sizes, crop, notice dimensions and the €65 price stay fixed. The rule supplies a response an editor would otherwise improvise.

This costs lower-page space. The template supports up to three approved title lines; longer inputs need another variant or review. Breaks are supplied editorially, not generated automatically.

## Two variations

### Require the condition beside the price

![Before and after sets show standard-price and reduced-price workshop offers. The standard offer is unchanged. Before, the reduced offer places its student-ID condition at the foot, beneath the booking address. After, the same two condition lines sit directly below the €18 price, and the booking address moves below them. All copy, typography and photographs remain unchanged.](../assets/graphic-design/template-conditional-offers.svg "The standard offer stays fixed. Only the reduced offer’s supplied condition and booking-address groups move; eligibility is required input, never inferred from price.")

[Full-size comparison](../assets/graphic-design/template-conditional-offers.svg) · [Paired narrow-screen views](../assets/graphic-design/template-conditional-offers-mobile.svg)

The €45 standard offer stays unchanged. In the €18 reduced offer, the booking address separates the price from “Students with current ID only.” Make eligibility required for this variant and move its two supplied lines immediately below the price, with booking beneath.

The condition travels with the offer rather than becoming footer debris. This needs additional space before the action; identical baselines across variants would undermine the relationship. A required field can flag missing wording, but an editor must still verify the policy.

### Define an honest fallback for missing content

![Before and after sets show October and November studio-review cards. October retains its photograph, figures and short note. Before, November inherits October’s photograph and clips two lines of its data note. After, November becomes a shorter text-only card showing 10 workshops, 68 of 80 places filled, all three note lines and its November source reference.](../assets/graphic-design/template-missing-image-and-notes.svg "October stays fixed. November hides the inherited photo, moves data/note/reference up 220 units, shortens the card by 220 and reveals the full note. Values remain unchanged.")

[Full-size comparison](../assets/graphic-design/template-missing-image-and-notes.svg) · [Paired narrow-screen views](../assets/graphic-design/template-missing-image-and-notes-mobile.svg)

October has a photograph and short note. November has no supplied photograph and needs three note lines, but the copied card retains October’s image and clips two qualifications.

Use a text-only variant: hide the inherited image, move data and reference upward, shorten the card, and reveal the full note at unchanged type size. November still reports 10 workshops and 68 of 80 places filled, now alongside its provisional-count qualification and closing date. October stays fixed.

Unequal card heights are acceptable here. Fixed-size print needs another arrangement. Missing values also require an explicit policy; they must not inherit last month’s figures or silently become zero.

## Useful when

Templates earn their upkeep when the task recurs and editors need dependable choices. Give inputs meaningful names, identify the current master, and specify who approves exceptions. Keep filled examples beside it, including long titles, conditional content and absent assets.

Adobe’s parent pages maintain repeating artwork across inherited pages. Content obligations need separate rules. [Create and apply parent pages](https://helpx.adobe.com/indesign/desktop/create-and-organize-pages/create-and-manage-parent-pages/create-parent-pages.html).

Realistic inputs expose capacity and fallback problems that empty frames conceal. Victorian Government guidance similarly recommends realistic, accurate prototype content. [Low-fidelity prototyping](https://www.vic.gov.au/low-fidelity-prototyping).

## Watch out for

Automatic shrinking, clipping and stale defaults suggest the template is protecting appearance at the expense of meaning. Define safe edits and escalation points; test the exported result in its intended medium. A prototype does not establish production readiness. [GOV.UK: Making prototypes](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/design/making-prototypes).

For a one-off exhibition or unfamiliar format, bespoke composition may be more appropriate. If exceptions dominate, narrow the template’s scope rather than multiplying near-duplicate files.
