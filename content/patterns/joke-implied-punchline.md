*Give the audience enough information to complete the comic thought without stating it.*

The setup points toward a conclusion clearly enough that the comedian can leave part of it unstated. The audience supplies the missing event, accusation or consequence, making the inference part of the pleasure.

## Example 1 · Eddie Izzard

Advertising’s sexual promises reach dog food

Izzard explores how advertising uses sexual suggestion to sell ordinary products. When that logic reaches dog food, an uncomfortable implication opens up. Izzard leaves it hanging and moves on, letting the audience complete the connection rather than spelling it out.

— Eddie Izzard

## Example 2 · Steven Wright

A childhood sandbox

> When I was a little kid, we had a quicksand box. I was an only child… eventually.  

— Steven Wright

**The turn:** The audience supplies the missing fate of his siblings.

[Source](<https://www.dead-frog.com/comedians/jokes/steven-wright>)

## Example 3 · Emo Philips

A German relative complains about finding no good bagels

> My sister married a German. He complained he couldn't get a good bagel back home. I said, "Well, whose fault is that?"  

— Emo Philips

**The turn:** The historical accusation is left for the audience to supply.

[Source](<https://www.dead-frog.com/comedians/jokes/emo-philips>)

## What makes the structure work

Give listeners enough direction to infer something definite. A pause cannot carry information that has not been prepared. Delaying a punchline that is eventually spoken is a related performance choice, but not permanent omission.
