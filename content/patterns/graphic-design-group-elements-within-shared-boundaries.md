A supplier puts delivery terms for two tableware sets inside the mug set’s box. The words say “Both sets,” but the outline suggests otherwise. A buyer considering bowls may wonder whether the same charge applies.

Make the region describe the intended unit. An outline or background panel says **these elements belong together**. Name that unit—one session, offer or address—then include its members and leave shared information outside.

## What to notice

![Two identical photographic studio programmes show Clay bowl and Print a tote sessions. Before, the names, rooms, times, prices and inclusions have no enclosing outlines. After, one thin rectangle surrounds all the details for each session; every word and position remains unchanged.](../assets/graphic-design/boundaries-session-records.svg "Only two outlines appear, each enclosing a complete session record. Copy, typography, positions and imagery stay fixed.")

[Full-size comparison](../assets/graphic-design/boundaries-session-records.svg) · [Stacked narrow-screen view](../assets/graphic-design/boundaries-session-records-mobile.svg)

Yard Studio’s programme separates each activity’s name from its room, time and price across two columns. The rows are aligned, but visitors must track across them to assemble a complete session.

Add one thin outline around each record. “Clay bowl,” “Studio 1,” “10:00–12:00,” “€45 / person” and its inclusion line now share a region. Keep the programme-wide booking line outside. Every word and position stays fixed; the enclosure supplies the relationship across the columns.

This is **common-region grouping**. Palmer’s foundational demonstrations show that enclosure can influence grouping even against competing proximity or similarity cues. That supports the mechanism, not a promise that boxes improve every programme. [Palmer, 1992](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1516361/).

## Two variations

### Leave shared terms outside local offers

![Two identical tableware sheets show a mug photograph and two priced set options. Before, the mug-set outline extends down around Both sets, Delivery €6 and Dispatch in 3 days, while the bowl-set outline ends above them. After, only the mug outline becomes shorter, leaving the shared terms outside both set regions.](../assets/graphic-design/boundaries-shared-delivery.svg "Only the left outline shortens, from 285 to 140 units. Shared terms remain in place, outside both offer boundaries.")

[Full-size comparison](../assets/graphic-design/boundaries-shared-delivery.svg) · [Stacked narrow-screen view](../assets/graphic-design/boundaries-shared-delivery-mobile.svg)

The €24 mug offer’s extended outline encloses “Both sets,” “Delivery €6” and “Dispatch in 3 days.” Shorten only that outline so it ends below the mug description. The terms remain in place, outside both offers: less enclosure repairs the misleading membership.

The note still aligns with the left column, so its “Both sets” label matters. A wider redesign could reinforce its shared scope through position as well. The photograph conveys material character; it does not document both complete sets.

### Enclose the choice rather than the attribute

![Two identical day-pass sheets show Desk and Studio offers beneath a workspace photograph. Before, five horizontal rectangles group headings, prices, hours, room descriptions and booking labels into separate rows. After, two vertical rectangles each contain one complete offer. Text, alignment, spacing, photograph and presentation remain unchanged.](../assets/graphic-design/boundaries-offer-membership.svg "Only the boundary path changes: five horizontal attribute regions become two vertical offer regions. Text, positions, colors and imagery stay fixed.")

[Full-size comparison](../assets/graphic-design/boundaries-offer-membership.svg) · [Stacked narrow-screen view](../assets/graphic-design/boundaries-offer-membership-mobile.svg)

Common’s day-pass sheet initially encloses headings, prices, hours and booking labels in horizontal rows. Replace those five regions with two vertical ones: Desk and Studio each become a complete offer. Keep the shared Wi-Fi and coffee statement outside.

Row alignment still permits comparison, while each enclosure explains what comes with one choice. If readers instead needed to audit one attribute across many venues, horizontal grouping or a comparison table could serve them better. The original is a mismatch for this task, not inherently poor design.

## Useful when

Use shared boundaries when members are spread apart, repeated records are easy to misassociate, or scope needs to be explicit. A light outline or surface may be enough; allow clearance between content and edge. McGill illustrates both region and proximity as grouping methods. [Proximity and region](https://www.mcgill.ca/web-services/resources/toolkits/digital-design-toolkit/design-principles/proximity-and-region).

## Watch out for

A box around every sentence creates fragments. If spacing already explains membership, another boundary may add clutter. Coordinate enclosure with proximity rather than making them contradict each other.

Exclusive boxes also struggle with overlapping categories: an event can be both “Evening” and “For beginners.” Labels or filters may express that relationship better.

Online, a rectangle alone supplies no programmatic grouping. Use appropriate semantic structures, such as fieldsets and legends for related form controls. [W3C: Grouping Controls](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/forms/grouping/).
