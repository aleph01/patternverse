A schedule’s key says green means Open, amber Waitlist and red Full. Someone unable to distinguish two session dots can read that key and still cannot identify the relevant status.

Give necessary color-coded meaning another visible carrier at the item itself. Keep useful hue coding, but make hue identification unnecessary. A word, pattern or defined shape must convey the same distinction rather than merely decorate it.

## What to notice

![Two identical pottery schedules show the same photograph, sessions, dates, capacities and green, amber and red status dots. Before, the reader must match each dot to the Open, Waitlist and Full legend. After, those status words also appear beside their respective session dots.](../assets/graphic-design/beyond-color-session-status.svg "Only Open, Waitlist and Full labels appear. Colored dots, legend, workshop facts, typography and positions stay fixed.")

[Full-size comparison](../assets/graphic-design/beyond-color-session-status.svg) · [Stacked narrow-screen view](../assets/graphic-design/beyond-color-session-status-mobile.svg)

Add “Open,” “Waitlist” and “Full” beside the existing session dots. Colors, order, capacities and all other information stay fixed. No availability changes: total class capacity is not the number of remaining places.

Each row states its own status instead of requiring color matching. The labels cost room; denser schedules may need defined short codes or another arrangement. W3C’s Use of Color guidance and G14 technique support text alternatives to color coding, not a claim of complete accessibility for this print example.

## Two variations

### Repeat the pattern in both line and key

![Two matching workshop-booking reports contain the same photograph, blue Actual line and orange Plan line. Before, both series and their legend samples are solid. After, only the Plan line and its matching key sample become dashed. Values, axes, line paths, colors and stroke widths remain unchanged.](../assets/graphic-design/beyond-color-actual-plan.svg "Only Plan becomes dashed in plot and key: 10-unit dashes, 7-unit gaps. Actual, values, colors, coordinates and line widths stay fixed.")

[Full-size comparison](../assets/graphic-design/beyond-color-actual-plan.svg) · [Stacked narrow-screen view](../assets/graphic-design/beyond-color-actual-plan-mobile.svg)

Make Plan dashed in the chart and its legend; keep Actual solid. Preserve hues, three-unit strokes, coordinates and February–May observations, including the vertical scale starting at 40.

Readers can trace the same two series through a crossing without identifying blue and orange. Dashes must survive reduction and printing; many series may need markers, direct labels or separate panels. This visual repair does not supply a complete text alternative. W3C G111 describes patterns alongside color as one technique.

### Name the error where correction is needed

![Two matching mobile booking forms show Alex Morgan in the Name field and an empty Email address field with a red outline. Both say Check your details. The revised form adds Email address is missing and Enter it to receive your pass beneath that field; the original colors, inputs, button, photograph and £18 price remain unchanged.](../assets/graphic-design/beyond-color-email-error.svg "Only two inline error lines appear. The empty Email field keeps its red outline; other fields, controls, price and imagery stay fixed.")

[Full-size comparison](../assets/graphic-design/beyond-color-email-error.svg) · [Stacked narrow-screen view](../assets/graphic-design/beyond-color-email-error-mobile.svg)

Add “Email address is missing” and “Enter it to receive your pass” beneath the empty field. Preserve the red outline, Alex Morgan’s name, controls and price.

The words identify the affected field, its problem and a next step; the outline remains supplementary. W3C distinguishes identifying an error in text from suggesting correction, though one message can serve both. Live layouts also need room, programmatic associations and appropriate announcement behavior; the static example verifies none of those.

## Useful when

Use reinforcement whenever color carries a category, status, action or instruction that readers need. Another route to the same information helps when hue is unavailable or ambiguous. A text legend alone is insufficient if distant marks still connect to it only through color.

For grayscale diagnosis, inspect a temporary preview, repair the missing meaning and return to color. Direct labels or an underline can retain the original palette. [[graphic-design-establish-value-contrast|Establish Value Contrast]] houses the grayscale test and its limits.

## Watch out for

An unexplained triangle may distinguish shapes without explaining Waitlist. Define mappings, keep labels current and make all cues agree; a green dot beside “Full” introduces a contradiction. Inspect pattern and shape differences at actual size.

Decorative hues need no category labels when they convey no information. A paint catalogue can appropriately make color the subject, with names and codes supporting identification. Visible redundancy also does not provide semantic information to assistive technologies; structure and equivalent accounts remain separate responsibilities.
