A square product photograph clips a cup handle while retaining linen on the opposite side. The full source already contains the complete set. Shift the window rather than find another photograph or enlarge its box.

Crop for the task: tighten, shift or widen to preserve useful subjects and relationships. Cropping selects retained content; fitting that selection into a fixed frame changes its apparent scale. The closest view is not automatically the strongest.

## Two variations

### Exchange peripheral space without changing scale

After, the square window shifts right and retains the complete handle and saucer. The source inset shows two equal-size boundaries.](../assets/graphic-design/crop-tableware-edge.svg "Only crop origin changes, x128→x400. Both 1024×1024 windows remain at y0 within a 400×400 frame; product scale stays fixed. No zoom or retouching.")

[Full-size comparison](../assets/graphic-design/crop-tableware-edge.svg) · [Stacked narrow-screen view](../assets/graphic-design/crop-tableware-edge-mobile.svg)

Move the square cup-and-saucer source window right without changing its width or height. The handle and saucer fit inside the frame at the same scale; less useful linen and plant context leave it.

A lifestyle story might value that setting, while a detail image could deliberately cut across a product. This brief calls for inspecting the pair. The image still cannot verify 320 ml capacity, 14 cm diameter or material specifications.

### Widen to restore the decisive context

Return the room image to a wider source area, preserving its frame and layout. Furniture appears smaller while the door and room boundaries return. No extension manufactures new surroundings.

For an enclosed-room offer, those boundaries matter more than the cup or notebook. A table-setting feature could prefer the original close view. The wider generated scene still proves neither acoustic privacy nor accessibility or current availability.

## Useful when

Crop when a relevant source contains the right material but gives it the wrong share of the frame or excludes a useful edge. Name what must survive before trimming: a hand’s contact, a product opening, a doorway or a relationship between subjects.

A tighter window changes apparent size; an equal-size shifted window changes which content survives. A wider selection can provide more practical impact when missing context is the problem. Adobe distinguishes cropping from resizing; in a fixed layout their effects can interact.

## Watch out for

Do not always move closer. Inspect edges as carefully as the center for partial handles, limbs and tools. Deliberate cutting can work, but an accidental missing feature needs a reason to remain.

A banner and square card expose different boundaries. CSS image fitting can clip an unchanged source, so inspect delivered aspect ratios and narrow layouts rather than approve only the original file.

Documentary crops must preserve honest context. WBUR’s guidance treats that as an accuracy question, not a preference for cleaner composition. Removing inconvenient surroundings can make a photograph misleading even while improving its graphic shape.
