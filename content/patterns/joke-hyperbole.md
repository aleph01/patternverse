*Exaggerate beyond plausibility while preserving a recognizable feeling.*

A recognizable observation is pushed beyond ordinary scale until it becomes impossible or extravagantly disproportionate. The exaggeration gives an attitude a concrete shape that a measured description could not provide.

## Example 1 · Steven Wright

Instant coffee

> I put instant coffee in a microwave and almost went back in time.  

— Steven Wright

**The turn:** Two forms of speed become impossible acceleration.

[Source](<https://www.standupcomedyclinic.com/65-funny-one-liners-by-steven-wright/>)

## Example 2 · Rodney Dangerfield

An impossibly severe reaction to an unattractive baby

> When I was born, I was so ugly that the doctor slapped my mother.  

— Rodney Dangerfield

**The turn:** Ordinary insecurity about appearance is pushed to an extravagant consequence: the doctor supposedly reacts against the mother instead of attending to the baby. The so-ugly claim magnifies a recognizable feeling of unattractiveness, giving the exaggeration a concrete event.

[Source](<https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2004/oct/06/comic-rodney-dangerfield-dies-at-82/>)

## Example 3 · Phyllis Diller

Being old enough for a blood type to become obsolete

> You know you're old if they have discontinued your blood type.  

— Phyllis Diller

**The turn:** Feeling out of date is extended beyond ordinary aging into a biological impossibility: even the person’s blood belongs to a discontinued model. Age is the property exaggerated; product obsolescence gives that extreme age a vivid comic form.

[Source](<https://www.brainyquote.com/quotes/phyllis_diller_383092>)

## What makes the structure work

Keep the exaggeration attached to the original observation. Bigger numbers alone may add scale without adding a comic idea. A vivid consequence or impossible image usually gives the audience more to grasp.
