*Prioritize the least appropriate aspect of a situation.*

A situation contains an obvious central concern, but the speaker fixates on something peripheral. The selective attention reveals a value system: vanity, irritation, curiosity or convenience can outrank what should matter most.

## Example 1 · Anthony Jeselnik

Chocolate addiction

Jeselnik takes his chocolate-loving girlfriend to see a drug addict. The setup suggests a warning about addiction; his actual concern turns out to be why she is not as thin as the person he has pointed out.

— Anthony Jeselnik

**The turn:** An apparent health intervention becomes criticism of her appearance.

[Source](<https://www.dead-frog.com/comedians/jokes/anthony-jeselnik>)

## Example 2 · Sarah Silverman

Being judged by an audience

> I don’t care if you think I’m r\*cist. I just want you to think I’m thin.  

— Sarah Silverman

**The turn:** The persona prioritizes appearance over the moral accusation.

[Source](<https://transcripts.foreverdreaming.org/viewtopic.php?t=143334>)

## Example 3 · Norm Macdonald

The supposedly worst part of a scandal

Macdonald reports someone ranking hypocrisy as the worst thing about Bill Cosby’s conduct. He rejects that ranking and places the assaults themselves ahead of the offender’s inconsistent public image.

— Norm Macdonald

**The turn:** The misplaced priority belongs to the person who ranks hypocrisy above the assaults. Macdonald exposes and corrects that ranking. The bit therefore teaches misplaced focus by criticizing somebody else’s judgement, rather than by making the performer’s persona embody it.

[Source](<https://podcasts.apple.com/gb/podcast/norm-macdonald-on-bill-cosbys-hypocrisy-strip-club/id1841924988?i=1000796656265>)

## What makes the structure work

Establish the important concern clearly enough for the misplaced attention to register. The persona need not announce the moral comparison. The audience can often recognize the distorted priorities from one concrete choice.
