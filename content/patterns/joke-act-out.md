*Shift from reporting a scene to performing its people, creatures or objects.*

A performer stops reporting a situation and briefly becomes someone inside it. Dialogue, voice or embodied reaction lets the audience encounter the scene directly. One comedian can supply several incompatible points of view.

## Example 1 · Brian Regan

Becoming the patient who ignored the doctor — I Walked on the Moon

Regan describes returning to a doctor after failing to follow the instructions he was given. He then performs the patient’s admission instead of leaving it as an account of what happened. The audience encounters his unhelpful patient-self inside the consultation, with the admission becoming a comic event of its own.

— Brian Regan

**The turn:** The shift is from the comedian explaining a visit to the comedian being the patient during it. An act-out can inhabit an earlier version of oneself; it does not require an invented stranger or two speaking roles. Dan Soder’s discussion of this routine explicitly identifies the enacted admission as an act-out.

## Example 2 · Eddie Izzard

Playing Darth Vader and the cafeteria worker — Circle

Darth Vader tries to order food with the authority of an imperial villain; the cafeteria worker insists on ordinary serving procedures. Izzard plays both sides of the encounter, moving between the threatening customer and the unimpressed worker. The disagreement about needing a tray makes their incompatible kinds of authority visible in one performed scene.

— Eddie Izzard

Switching roles lets the audience experience each response as it happens. Context Transplant supplies the situation; Act-Out is the method that makes its opposing characters present.

## Example 3 · Jim Carrey

Becoming Wile E. Coyote — TV guest performance on Johnny Carson

During his Tonight Show appearance, Carrey briefly becomes Wile E. Coyote after a cartoon mishap. Exaggerated bodily movement carries the character’s predicament, with a harmonica adding a cartoon sound effect. The recognizable reaction is performed physically instead of being explained in a narrated account. The linked archive recording includes this demonstration within a longer guest appearance.

— Jim Carrey

**The turn:** The performer’s body becomes the character undergoing the event. It is also a cartoon impression, but its structural fit comes from physically enacting the character’s experience.

## What makes the structure work

Look for the moment the performer stops explaining and starts living inside the scene. Regan inhabits his own patient-self; Izzard alternates between opposing characters; Carrey performs a cartoon predicament largely through movement and sound. A change of role can be vocal, physical or both. Dialogue in quotation marks alone does not establish an act-out, and a physical act-out need not contain dialogue. The examples use recordings and performance-specific descriptions so the enacted behavior, rather than a possible reading of the words, supports the classification.
