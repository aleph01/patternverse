*Build toward a substantial revelation, then deliver a disproportionately small payoff.*

A long or imposing build-up makes a substantial payoff seem due. An anticlimax deliberately pays it off with something small, banal or disproportionately simple. The collapse in scale is the comic movement.

## Example 1 · Norm Macdonald

The short moth joke, recalled in an interview

> A moth flies into a podiatrist’s office and then says, “Oh, man, I have problems.” The podiatrist says, “Why are you here? You should see a psychiatrist.” He goes, “Because the light was on.”  

— Norm Macdonald

**The turn:** A psychological complaint receives the ordinary explanation that a moth follows light. This short recollection mildly deflates the apparent crisis; it does not contain the lengthy tragic buildup of the expanded Conan performance. That longer rendition demonstrates anticlimax much more clearly.

## Example 2 · Jerry Seinfeld

Describing the world’s heaviest man

> Have you ever seen that guy who has the record for fattest man in the world? Bob Hughes, the fattest man in the world… 1400 pounds. Ladies and gentlemen, the man has let himself go  

— Jerry Seinfeld

**The turn:** The extreme weight receives a disproportionately mild everyday judgement. Understatement is the central mechanism; the small evaluation also creates a brief anticlimactic drop after the imposing announcement, without a long narrative buildup.

[Source](<https://www.dead-frog.com/comedians/jokes/jerry-seinfeld>)

## Example 3 · Norm Macdonald (joke), retold by Seth Meyers

A billionaire’s birthday, in a television news-desk joke

Macdonald’s news item identifies the birthday girl as the richest in the world, then asks what that level of wealth is like. Its illustration of extraordinary extravagance is merely that the party had two cakes. **The turn:** The superlative wealth and explicit question prepare us for an extraordinary display. Two cakes are a disproportionately modest answer. The anticlimax comes from that collapse in promised scale, rather than simply from mild wording; the buildup is brief but imposing.

## What makes the structure work

The build-up has to create a recognizable expectation and remain interesting enough to sustain it. Simply providing a weak ending is insufficient. Understatement can produce an anticlimax, but one concerns scale of language and the other the shape of expectation.
