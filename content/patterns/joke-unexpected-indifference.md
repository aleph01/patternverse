*React casually or positively to something normally upsetting.*

A setup invites alarm, resentment or urgency. The speaker responds with calm acceptance or pleasure instead. The emotional mismatch can reveal selfishness, unusual preferences or an unexpectedly practical attitude.

## Example 1 · Mitch Hedberg

A clothing-care instruction

> This shirt is dry clean only. Which means it’s dirty.  

— Mitch Hedberg

**The turn:** The cleaning instruction is translated into an admission that he has not followed it. His casual acceptance supplies the underreaction, although the setup creates little alarm. The sharper verbal mechanism is the unexpected redefinition of what the label tells us about the shirt.

## Example 2 · Tig Notaro

Deadpan underreaction — Live

> Good evening. Hello. I have cancer. How are you?  

— Tig Notaro

**The turn:** The announcement of a grave diagnosis sits inside an ordinary greeting, and routine small talk immediately resumes. That verbal restraint demonstrates unexpected underreaction. It does not establish that Notaro is indifferent to her illness or reveal her private emotional response.

[Source](<https://www.pbs.org/newshour/show/tig-notaro-laughs-through-her-darkest-moments>)

## Example 3 · Anthony Jeselnik

His grandmother’s death — Thoughts and Prayers

Jeselnik describes his grandmother’s death as something he expected to find unbearable, then reports that it was perfectly manageable. The grief anticipated by the setup never arrives.

— Anthony Jeselnik

**The turn:** The persona withholds the emotional response his setup promises.

[Source](<https://www.springfieldspringfield.co.uk/movie_script.php?movie=anthony-jeselnik-thoughts-and-prayers>)

## What makes the structure work

Establish a reaction the audience expects, then let the performed response fall conspicuously short. Here indifference describes the comic presentation, not a claim about the comedian’s private feelings. Notaro’s opening is deliberate deadpan underreaction; Jeselnik’s response belongs to an invented callous persona.
