A participant books from a poster, receives a ticket in another style and reads a reminder with swapped type roles. Each piece is plausible; their family relationships must be re-established at every encounter.

Define stable roles across related materials, along with permitted variation. “The serif carries event names; the sans carries practical facts” is more useful than a list of fonts. Shared language coordinates the family without prescribing one composition.

## What to notice

After, both adopt the poster’s title and fact roles while keeping their own sizes, layouts, copy and photographs.](../assets/graphic-design/language-cross-format-roles.svg "Only four font-family assignments change in ticket/reminder; the poster stays fixed. Sizes, anchors, copy, layouts and imagery remain identical within each pair.")

[Full-size set](../assets/graphic-design/language-cross-format-roles.svg) · [Paired narrow-screen views](../assets/graphic-design/language-cross-format-roles-mobile.svg)

Keep the poster as reference. Assign its serif/sans roles to ticket and reminder titles and facts, preserving nominal sizes and positions. The ticket retains one-person admission and YD1042; the reminder retains “Tomorrow” and its apron request.

The same relationship survives different formats and moments. Either assignment could be coherent; serif titles are not universally better. Another script, reading condition or coverage requirement may justify another face, with that exception recorded.

A recurring treatment gives viewers evidence connecting encounters. GOV.UK recommends consistency without compulsory uniformity; Carbon separates color roles from values supplied by themes. Extending role-versus-value reasoning to print is an editorial application, not a requirement to use software tokens.

## Two variations

### Bound a motif’s job

![Two sets repeat the same poster, ticket and reminder with identical typography and imagery. Before, paired-square marks appear beside the brand lines and also beside selected dates, prices and preparation text. After, the four extra marks disappear, leaving one programme signature beside each brand line.](../assets/graphic-design/language-motif-function.svg "Only four extra motifs disappear. Three brand signatures and all other content stay fixed; the motif is fictional, not a standardized service symbol.")

[Full-size set](../assets/graphic-design/language-motif-function.svg) · [Paired narrow-screen views](../assets/graphic-design/language-motif-function-mobile.svg)

Keep the paired-square signature beside the brand line in each artifact. Hide four additional marks attached to other content while leaving everything else fixed.

The motif identifies the programme instead of suggesting unexplained offers, selections or special instructions. The result is quieter. Repeated ornaments could suit another invitation if they do not imply false information. Generic squares alone do not establish external distinction from other organizations.

### Let content vary inside the language

![Before, three cards for Clay bowl, Seed packing and Coffee tasting all show the same general studio photograph. After, their image areas show clay materials, seed-packing materials and coffee respectively. Each card retains its own title, date, price, time and reservation text, along with the shared type roles and paired-square signature.](../assets/graphic-design/language-controlled-image-variation.svg "Only three image selections change by activity. Frames, fitting, copy and typography stay fixed; photographic subjects and content intentionally differ.")

[Full-size set](../assets/graphic-design/language-controlled-image-variation.svg) · [Paired narrow-screen views](../assets/graphic-design/language-controlled-image-variation-mobile.svg)

Replace generic studio imagery on three session cards with activity-specific clay, seed-packing and coffee photographs. Preserve typography, signature, frames, fitting, dates and prices.

The family stays recognizable through rules while images explain different offers. Uniformity no longer occupies a useful content slot. Images vary in texture and weight, so inspect each in its shared frame. An umbrella announcement could reasonably reuse one general scene.

## Useful when

Establish a shared language for programmes, campaigns or services spanning several artifacts. Specify stable roles, allowed variables and reasons for exceptions in one maintainable reference. Name who can amend it and test difficult content across formats.

A palette and logo can leave content assignments undecided. Conversely, a small explicit agreement can coordinate titles, practical information, a signature and image slots without documenting every detail. Repeated needs should update the rule instead of multiplying unexplained personal variants.

## Watch out for

Do not confuse coherence with identical layouts, pictures or facts. Different moments have different jobs. Longer titles, access information and language coverage must be accommodated rather than suppressed to preserve a style.

Independent commissions or sub-brands may need separate identities. Shared language cannot decide which materials should belong to one family or establish whether people recognize it. Judge those relationships against purpose and audience evidence, then maintain only rules worth carrying forward.
