A programme’s posters and tickets consistently use paired squares. In a directory, another provider uses a similar mark. “The workshop with the squares” could now mean either organization.

Develop cues against the alternatives people actually encounter. A mark, type treatment or photographic approach must differ usefully, survive its required sizes and become associated with the intended source. Looking unusual to its designer answers only part of that task.

## What to notice

Names, workshop details, photography and layout remain unchanged.](../assets/graphic-design/distinctive-comparison-set.svg "Only Common’s two marks change; Gather and the listing stay fixed. Replace Common’s two directory marks with opposing solid corners while leaving Gather’s overlapping squares unchanged. Keep every name, image, price, position and color fixed.

A different silhouette distinguishes this comparison set, repeated consistently across Common’s entries. Names remain visible while the new association is unlearned. Another organization may use similar corners: broader review and audience attribution testing could reject the candidate. This establishes neither originality nor legal availability.

Romaniuk distinguishes an asset’s fame—association with its brand—from uniqueness relative to alternatives. A fresh design can differ without having either a learned audience association or durable advantage.

## Two variations

### Keep the distinction at native size

![Before and after booking confirmations and separate production proofs. Before, the opposing corners consist of six fine parallel rails. After, those rails become solid bands in the same bounds. The proof shows each version at 24 pixels and enlarged four times; booking copy, clay photograph and all placements stay fixed.](../assets/graphic-design/distinctive-small-size.svg "Only three mark renderings change. Bounds, anchors, text and imagery stay fixed; pixel labels refer to native 1×, and the enlargement explains construction.")

[Full-size comparison and proof](../assets/graphic-design/distinctive-small-size.svg) · [Paired narrow-screen views](../assets/graphic-design/distinctive-small-size-mobile.svg)

The engraved corner mark’s rails occupy fractions of pixels in a 24px slot. Merge them into solid bands while preserving its outer bounds and corner arrangement. Apply the same change to confirmation and production proof; booking details remain fixed.

Substantial areas carry the geometry where fine lines become faint. The large proof explains construction; the native sample shows survival. Delicacy is lost and may remain suitable in a large print variant. Define variant uses, test association across them and proof print separately. IBM likewise recommends small-size preparation and comparison with existing icons.

### Inspect unintended promises

![Before and after studio membership announcements with the same room photograph and offer. Before, a shield-and-check mark sits beside the Common member heading. After, the same mark slot contains opposing corners. The headline, price, terms reference, URL, typography and photograph are unchanged.](../assets/graphic-design/distinctive-unintended-meaning.svg "Only the identity mark changes. Verification/protection is a possible shield-and-check reading, not an observed response; the replacement is not a proven distinctive asset.")

[Full-size comparison](../assets/graphic-design/distinctive-unintended-meaning.svg) · [Narrow-screen comparison](../assets/graphic-design/distinctive-unintended-meaning-mobile.svg)

Replace the membership proposal’s shield/check with the corner candidate inside the same 48px bounds. Preserve “From €24/month · See terms” and all offer details.

The explicit verification/protection imagery disappears. That is appropriate because this offer makes neither promise. Corners still could suggest framing or expansion and need interpretation checks. A genuine verification service could appropriately use a labeled verification indicator. The new mark does not certify anything; words explain membership.

## Useful when

Use this pattern when an internally consistent identity blends into its real shelf, listing, icon group or feed. Compare relevant neighbors rather than only business competitors. Change a candidate feature selectively to learn what supplies distinction.

Repeated pairing with a name gives viewers opportunities to learn the source association. Existing familiarity is evidence: do not replace an ordinary-looking cue merely for freshness. Test attribution—this source, another source or nothing—not just visibility or aesthetic preference.

## Watch out for

A surprise on every campaign prevents dependable association. Preserve recognition-bearing characteristics and document size adaptations. Novelty alone cannot establish recognition or useful meaning.

Conventional search, information and access symbols have functional jobs; making them proprietary can obstruct those jobs. Most routine prices and navigation also need no distinctive treatment. Keep labels available where interpretation is uncertain, as GOV.UK recommends for icons. Audience evidence, not a polished presentation, should decide whether a candidate earns adoption.
