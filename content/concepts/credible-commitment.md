## A recognizable situation

Ira offers a camera for sale online. Sam wants it sent before he pays. Ira would then have given up the camera and paid postage while Sam still controlled the money.

Sam repeats that he will pay when it arrives. Ira wants to know what makes that later payment dependable. Paying everything upfront would reverse their positions: Sam would have paid before Ira decided whether to send the camera.

This hypothetical sale can benefit both. The difficulty is arranging control over the exchange before either participant takes an exposure they cannot accept.

## The underlying pattern

**A credible commitment makes a future course of action dependable by restricting available choices or changing the consequences of reversal.** For it to influence someone else's decision, they must be able to recognize and rely on the mechanism before becoming vulnerable.

The compact test is: **When the moment comes, what makes following through preferable—or makes backing out unavailable?**

Signaling asks what an action reveals about someone. Commitment asks what makes their later conduct dependable, even when their incentives are already understood. The same arrangement can do both, but the questions differ.

Thomas Schelling's 1956 _An Essay on Bargaining_ is a foundational analysis of strategic self-binding. It explains how visibly restricting one's own future choices can influence another person's behavior, while examining the institutions needed to make restrictions effective. Commitment is not simply a declaration of determination. [Original essay](https://userpages.umbc.edu/~nmiller/POLI388/BARGAINING.TCS.pdf)

## What keeps it going

The timing matters. Before dispatch, Sam wants Ira to send the camera. After receiving it, he could gain by withholding payment. Ira anticipates that later incentive and declines now, under the deliberately unenforced benchmark.

This is not necessarily an information problem. Both can understand the incentives perfectly. Better knowledge may make the refusal more certain.

**Backward induction** means examining later decisions first, then working back to earlier choices. A promised action cannot support cooperation merely because it would make the overall plan attractive; it must survive the later decision point.

Kydland and Prescott's 1977 analysis of policy **time inconsistency** develops the related problem: a plan desirable before people respond can cease to be the preferred action afterward, even with a fixed policy objective. Their results depend on their models of expectations and policy choice, rather than proving that every rigid rule improves policy. [Original paper](https://www.econ.puc-rio.br/mgarcia/Macro%20II%20-%20Mestrado/KydlandPrescott1977.pdf)

## Why the obvious responses fail

**Repeat the promise.** Repetition changes little when it adds no consequence or constraint. Honest preferences can matter, but the model below deliberately excludes an intrinsic reward from keeping promises.

**Wait until after the work to arrange protection.** By then the seller has given up possession and incurred postage; reversing the exchange may be difficult. The promised safeguard must exist before the vulnerable action.

**Threaten a response nobody would carry out.** A costly punishment needs its own credibility. Naming a penalty does not establish detection, authority, collection, or willingness to enforce it.

**Pay everything upfront.** This protects the seller while exposing the buyer to non-delivery. It can move the vulnerability rather than remove it.

## Possible ways out

These are practical intervention families; each changes a specific part of the situation.

**Move control before vulnerability.** Funds held by a reliable independent service can be released against verifiable delivery. The buyer must lose unilateral power to cancel payment after satisfactory performance. This requires funding, a workable delivery test, and dependable custody; it costs money and flexibility.

**Make reversal unattractive.** A funded bond, enforceable remedy, or sufficiently valuable future business can outweigh the gain from reneging. A party can offer the arrangement, but relevant authorities or counterparties must actually support its consequences. Reputation requires observable conduct and credible future dealings; it does not automatically discipline an anonymous one-off exchange.

**Change decision rights.** Delegation or a binding procedure can remove a later discretionary choice. The delegate's authority, incentives, and resistance to interference become part of the problem. A rule that the original decision-maker can quietly override provides weaker protection.

**Reduce the amount exposed.** Smaller milestones or simultaneous exchange can limit losses. They may leave residual discretion and create extra administration. An indivisible item may not permit useful smaller stages; simultaneous exchange needs practical access.

Choosing to honor a promise is a strategy within the original game. Installing escrow, enforceable penalties, or a new exchange sequence changes that game.

## A worked example

### Put payment beyond unilateral control before dispatch

Ira can decline or send the camera. If it arrives under the unsecured proposal, Sam can pay or withhold while keeping it. Both know that an honestly completed sale is worthwhile. In this one-sale benchmark, each values only the stipulated own-material result, with no future business, intrinsic cost of breaking a promise, or effective recovery mechanism.

Sam then prefers withholding after receipt. Ira anticipates this and refuses to send. Refusal, with withholding planned if delivery occurred, is a **subgame-perfect equilibrium**: planned actions are optimal at each decision point under these assumptions. A firmer assurance alone changes none of them.

Instead, Sam deposits the agreed payment with an independent holder before dispatch. They define what counts as delivery of the described camera, the evidence needed, and how non-delivery or disputes are handled. Once satisfactory delivery is verified, Sam cannot unilaterally divert the reserved payment. Non-delivery does not simply hand the money to Ira.

For the hypothetical successful continuation, custody and verification are reliable, both accept the terms and fees, and Ira can cover packing and postage until release. He sends the camera, satisfactory delivery is verified, and the holder releases payment. This is an assumed sequence, not a description of a named platform's protections.

The arrangement constrains a later option; it does not persuade Sam to honor the promise while leaving his control unchanged. The reserved money is tied up, the holder becomes another dependency, and condition disputes can be hard to resolve. A dispatch record alone need not prove that the correct, working item arrived.

Reserved payment also does not finance costs before release. If Ira needs money to obtain stock, pack, or ship, a separate financing arrangement is required. An advance would change who bears non-delivery risk. Without dependable custody or workable verification, in-person exchange, a different transaction, or no sale may be preferable.

## Limits and lookalikes

Commitment has a cost: lost discretion can prevent useful adaptation. Exceptions need intelligible triggers and trustworthy decision rights. An unrestricted escape clause may undo assurance; an inflexible rule may preserve a bad arrangement.

Self-commitment concerns one's own later choices. In Ashraf, Karlan, and Yin's randomized Philippine savings study, clients offered an account restricting withdrawals had higher average bank balances after twelve months than controls. Take-up was voluntary, and the product also included goal-setting features. This supports a particular product's effect, not universal welfare gains or the sale model’s predictions. [Study](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/sites/default/files/research-paper/16_19_Tying%20odysseus%20to%20the%20mast.Dean,Ashraf_May2006.pdf)

Commitment can also support coercive threats or entrench unfair terms. Being believable and being desirable are separate judgments. A signed document alone does not establish practical enforceability.

## Related patterns

[[signaling-and-credibility|Signaling and Credibility]] concerns inference from observable actions. Commitment changes future options or incentives; it can also provide a signal.

[[trust-games|Trust Games]] expose someone to a later discretionary response. Commitment can change that vulnerability rather than merely improve confidence about intentions.

[[bargaining-problems|Bargaining Problems]] concerns selecting acceptable terms. Reliable execution can unlock agreement while leaving the division of benefits unresolved.
