## A recognizable situation

Lea wants her first design job. Every junior vacancy she is considering asks for previous paid work in a similar role. She has studied, made a portfolio, and can demonstrate relevant skills, but the employers she has approached count only employment as the experience they require.

She applies for a job to gain that experience. The answer is that she needs experience before she can get the job. More applications return her to the same missing condition.

This hypothetical version of a familiar Catch-22 holds the available routes still: no employer currently accepts her alternative evidence or offers an entry position. The obstacle is getting the first opportunity that would satisfy the condition for being offered it.

## The underlying pattern

**A Catch-22 makes the route to a remedy depend on conditions that block that route.** In its sharpest form, doing what would secure release becomes the reason release is refused.

The name comes from Joseph Heller's 1961 novel. In its military bureaucracy, a pilot can be excused from combat on grounds of insanity, but requesting exemption demonstrates the rational concern for survival that disqualifies him. This is literary satire, not a clinical account. [Heller, publisher's description](https://www.simonandschuster.net/books/Catch-22/Joseph-Heller/9781451621174)

Everyday usage also includes circular prerequisites: experience is required for employment, while employment is treated as the only way to gain experience. The narrower self-defeating rule and the broader prerequisite loop overlap; identifying the exact obstruction helps select a response. [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/catch-22)

In this collection, Catch-22 belongs primarily to dependency and feasibility traps. The diagnostic question is whether the stated rules leave an attainable qualifying state or permitted sequence.

## What keeps it going

Lea needs a first paid role to acquire the accepted experience, but the employers in this example require that experience before offering the role. Study and personal projects are available, yet they do not count at the gate she needs to pass. Repeating a rejected form of evidence cannot change that rule.

The strict loop depends on those conditions. If a reachable employer accepts a portfolio, a placement supplies qualifying experience, or a genuine entry role is available, the search may remain hard without being blocked in this way. The example is not a claim that all employers impose the same requirement.

Separate decisions can maintain the difficulty. An employer may use previous employment as evidence of working to deadlines and with colleagues. Each employer can prefer someone already tested elsewhere without providing the first opportunity itself. Understanding that preference does not give Lea the authority to change it.

This is the broad everyday meaning of Catch-22: the means of satisfying a requirement are themselves withheld until it is satisfied. Heller's narrower literary form adds a twist: requesting release is taken as evidence against eligibility for release. A first opportunity can start Lea's loop; that kind of bridge would not necessarily solve a rule that disqualifies someone precisely when they try to qualify.

Berti and Simpson's conceptual analysis emphasizes that power can restrict people's ability to challenge contradictory requirements. Recognizing a trap and having authority to alter it are different capacities. Their paper develops a theoretical account, drawing on existing organizational research; it does not test a universal escape method. [Berti and Simpson, 2021](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/bitstream/2438/19254/3/FullText.pdf)

## Why the obvious responses fail

**Send the same evidence more often.** More applications may uncover a different employer, but under the stated gate the portfolio still does not count as employment. Distinguish finding a real alternative from expecting repetition to alter the same rule.

**Get experience first.** This is useful only if someone identifies an accessible route that produces experience the employer accepts. Merely naming the missing prerequisite does not supply it.

**Work without pay.** That may fail both practically and at the gate: Lea needs income, and the stipulated employers require paid experience. An unpaid task is not automatically an accepted or affordable bridge.

**Explain the unfairness to the same reviewer.** The reviewer may agree while lacking authority to change screening. A sympathetic answer is different from an authorized entry route.

## Possible ways out

The following intervention families are an editorial synthesis. Their usefulness depends on the actual rules and available authority.

**Verify what counts.** Ask whether the stated requirement is mandatory and which alternatives are accepted. A work sample, supervised placement, or comparable responsibility may already qualify. This can reveal a route, although repeated clarification consumes time and may return the same answer.

**Create a paid first opportunity.** An employer able to change recruitment can offer a defined entry role with supervision and assessment. Someone must fund the work, provide guidance, and accept uncertainty about performance. Calling ordinary unsupported work a trial does not supply those conditions.

**Replace the proxy with relevant evidence.** The employer can assess current capability rather than treat past employment as the sole acceptable evidence. That changes the gate; it does not guarantee that Lea meets the actual requirements. Assessment takes time and needs fair, usable criteria.

**Seek a decision spanning the gates.** Take the specific obstruction to someone empowered to revise the procedure. An exception can resolve one case while leaving unequal access for later applicants. A durable revision takes longer and can face resistance.

**Change the route or acknowledge the limit.** Another employer, role, or accepted training route may serve the underlying goal. Switching can be costly or unavailable. If every accessible route has the same gate and nobody can alter it, there is no start within those assumptions. That is a feasibility limit, not evidence of insufficient persistence.

## A worked example

### Supply an accepted first opportunity

Lea's repeated applications do not produce the paid experience they require. She asks a prospective employer what that experience is meant to establish. In this hypothetical diagnosis, the concern is dependable work on real briefs, rather than a skill that only years of employment could produce.

A hiring manager with authority creates a paid, supervised entry assignment. It has a defined scope, an available supervisor, affordable compensation, and assessment criteria explained before Lea accepts. The employer explicitly accepts satisfactory work in that assignment as evidence for the next role; it does not demand the same prior employment to enter it.

The new route is therefore genuine: enter the assignment, do assessed work, and produce evidence the ordinary hiring process will accept. In the stipulated successful outcome, Lea performs satisfactorily and becomes eligible for consideration. Selection for a later job still depends on vacancies, performance, and other applicants. The illustration establishes an entry route, not a promised job.

The employer pays and supervises someone whose performance was initially uncertain. Lea spends time on an opportunity that may not lead to another offer. If the assignment is inaccessible, unaffordable, or not accepted as relevant experience, the proposed bridge has not broken the loop.

The change preserves assessment while replacing prior employment as the only way to enter. It cannot be achieved by Lea declaring her own evidence equivalent without the employer accepting it.

### A documented alternative route in another setting

Banking access offers a real illustration of changing an entry requirement. HSBC UK's No Fixed Address service operates through selected branches and participating support organizations. A caseworker arranges an appointment; correspondence uses the address associated with the account. It provides a specified route for people without a fixed home address, rather than requiring them to obtain one first. [HSBC UK](https://www.hsbc.co.uk/help/money-worries/no-fixed-address/)

This describes an available process, not universal access or measured impact. In July 2026 the FCA reported continuing difficulties with basic-account provision, including non-standard identification and address barriers. A route on paper can still be poorly implemented. [FCA findings](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/banks-told-improve-access-basic-accounts)

## Limits and lookalikes

A **trade-off** permits choices with different costs. A **resource shortage** may leave no affordable option without involving contradictory rules. Neither automatically constitutes a Catch-22.

A **circular prerequisite** can sometimes be started with an outside input. A **double bind** concerns incompatible communications within a relationship that constrains challenge or escape. The terms overlap in ordinary usage, but their mechanisms need separate examination. [Tracy, 2004](https://www.sarahjtracy.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/08/Tracy-Dialectic-Contradiction-Double-Bind.pdf)

Also distinguish a rule from its interpretation. Accepted alternative experience or an available first-entry role may invalidate the diagnosis of a closed loop. Conversely, reframing cannot grant authority or opportunities that do not exist.
