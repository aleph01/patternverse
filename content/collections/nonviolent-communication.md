These eight guides draw on Nonviolent Communication (NVC), the approach developed by Marshall Rosenberg. The pattern titles are editorial names rather than an official NVC taxonomy. Examples and dialogue are original, fictional illustrations. Explanations of how a pattern may help are practical interpretations, without a guarantee of a particular conversational result.

The articles can be read independently. They do not require speaking in a fixed observation–feeling–need–request formula. Ordinary language is usually enough when the underlying distinctions remain clear.

- **The conversation gets stuck arguing over accusations:** [[nvc-separate-observation-from-interpretation|Separate Observation from Interpretation]].
- **Expressing hurt turns into a debate about intentions:** [[nvc-distinguish-feelings-from-conclusions|Distinguish Feelings from Conclusions]].
- **Preferred solutions seem incompatible:** [[nvc-separate-needs-from-strategies|Separate Needs from Strategies]].
- **A wish for improvement means different things to each person:** [[nvc-make-the-request-concrete|Make the Request Concrete]].
- **A request feels difficult to refuse:** [[nvc-leave-room-for-no|Leave Room for No]].
- **Advice misses what someone wants help with:** [[nvc-understand-before-solving|Understand Before Solving]].
- **Your own reactions make it hard to choose a response:** [[nvc-give-yourself-empathy-first|Give Yourself Empathy First]].
- **A contribution or its meaning goes unnoticed:** [[nvc-make-appreciation-specific|Make Appreciation Specific]].

Each article includes worked examples, common mistakes, limits, related patterns, and sources. They preserve attention to power, obligations, safety, and the possibility that a disagreement remains unresolved.
