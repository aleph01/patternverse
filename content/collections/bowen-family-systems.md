Bowen family systems theory examines people’s functioning within their relationships and across generations. These six guides explore particular concepts, relationship processes, clinical approaches, and a research tool within that tradition. They distinguish Bowen’s formulations, later interpretations, and original teaching examples.

Choose a starting point from the situation you recognize:

- **You agree, then resent the agreement:** [[bowen-i-position|I-Position]] considers defining a position and following through while someone else may remain disappointed.
- **You keep becoming the messenger:** [[bowen-detriangling|Detriangling]] examines participation in a relationship triangle without taking over someone else’s relationship.
- **You take over tasks, while someone else increasingly gives way:** [[bowen-overfunctioning-underfunctioning-reciprocity|Overfunctioning-Underfunctioning Reciprocity]] follows both sides of the exchange and distinguishes assistance from anxious supervision.
- **You talk frequently but know little about each other:** [[bowen-person-to-person-relationship|Person-to-Person Relationship]] explores more personal contact without demanding disclosure or agreement.
- **Family concern becomes organized around one child:** [[bowen-family-projection-process|Family Projection Process]] examines adult interpretations and responses while preserving necessary care and support.
- **A family resemblance has become an explanation:** [[bowen-family-diagram|Family Diagram]] separates family structure, dated observations, relatives’ accounts, and unanswered questions.

Five articles include interactive comparisons of corresponding exchanges; Family Diagram includes a three-generation illustration and an observation table. The fictional examples keep disagreements, practical responsibilities, and uncertainty visible. The articles discuss evidence and limits, including differences in power, safety, capacity, and obligations.
