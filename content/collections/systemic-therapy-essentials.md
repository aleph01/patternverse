Systemic therapy considers people within significant relationships, histories, communities,
and material circumstances. These guides explore what needs consideration,
how responses recur, how different accounts can become available for inquiry,
what already offers usable support, how wanted change could be recognized,
how an agreed alternative could be tried, and how its effects inform the next decision.

These seven titles are an editorial synthesis. Systemic traditions do not all use
the same terms or methods.

- [[map-the-relevant-system|Map the Relevant System]] brings relevant relationships,
  resources, histories, and practical conditions into view.
- [[map-the-loop|Map the Loop]] reconstructs sequences of responses and examines how
  attempted solutions may participate in their recurrence.
- [[open-another-perspective|Open Another Perspective]] explores different meanings
  while keeping interpretations open to correction.
- [[find-what-already-helps|Find What Already Helps]] investigates helpful exceptions,
  capabilities, relationships, and the conditions that make them available.
- [[describe-the-preferred-future|Describe the Preferred Future]] translates hopes
  into meaningful, recognizable differences without presuming agreement about them.
- [[change-one-part-of-the-interaction|Change One Part of the Interaction]] explores
  an agreed alternative in a response, role, boundary, or arrangement.
- [[learn-from-feedback|Learn from Feedback]] examines different participants'
  experiences and uses that information to reconsider the work.

The articles combine clearly labeled fictional teaching scenarios with attributed
published clinical cases. Their accounts and figures aid inquiry, not diagnosis or proof of
causation. There are no guided practices or worksheets in this reading edition.
