A melody has rhythm, direction, register, harmonic relationships, and breathing space. This part develops a compact motif into a phrase and a focal hook, then shows how selective changes preserve its identity.

The E4–G4–A4 seed appears in several contexts. Each example states which feature changes and accounts for its durations. A four-bar demonstration is labeled separately from the full song's eight-bar verse, so a useful teaching variation does not silently replace the reference.

Enter with a melodic problem, and use the linked rhythm and pitch explanations as optional refreshers. The lyrical setting in Part V will make the musical phrase answer to actual words.

## Patterns in sequence

20. [[songwriting-start-with-a-motif|Start with a Motif]] — Develop a short rhythmic and melodic identity into a longer phrase.
21. [[songwriting-contour-and-register|Give the Melody a Destination]] — Reserve a contour change or register peak for the phrase that deserves the listener's attention.
22. [[songwriting-melody-and-harmony|Support or Challenge the Melody Note]] — Keep a melodic note fixed while comparing harmony that settles it, colors it, or delays its resolution.
23. [[songwriting-phrases-and-breathing|Let the Phrase Breathe]] — Give a musical sentence enough sounding time and enough space to make its next entrance clear.
24. [[songwriting-write-a-hook|Write a Hook]] — Give the listener a distinctive musical or lyrical feature worth remembering.
25. [[songwriting-develop-without-losing-the-idea|Develop Without Losing the Idea]] — Change selected features of a motif while preserving a recognizable connection.
