Start with five connected discoveries: a note has a pitch; an interval measures a distance; a scale arranges a note collection; a tonal home orients a passage; and a chord combines notes. Each pattern below takes one main relationship before the course brings them together.

## A first route through the foundations

1. **Hear distances.** [[songwriting-pitch-and-intervals|Intervals and Melodic Shape]] compares a phrase with a moved version and a changed version. Count half steps before learning all the interval names.
2. **Build a palette.** [[songwriting-build-a-scale|Build a Scale from Steps]] constructs major scales on C and D, then compares major with natural minor around C.
3. **Establish home.** [[songwriting-scales-and-tonal-home|Establish a Tonal Home]] keeps an opening phrase fixed and compares C-centered and A-centered endings.
4. **Combine notes.** [[songwriting-build-and-hear-chords|Choose Chords by Their Notes]] takes alternate scale notes, hears them separately and together, then changes one chord member.
5. **Connect the family.** [[songwriting-chord-families-and-numbers|Think in Chord Numbers]] builds chords on the scale's degrees and connects note names to chord numbers in the editor.

The first four patterns have short comparisons in the existing player. Their prose spells every note and duration, so the explanation also works without audio. The fifth opens the chord editor. A pitch name, a scale degree, a chord numeral, and a beat count name different things; the course identifies which relationship each number describes.

## After the foundations

The final two patterns use those relationships to transpose a song and shape a harmonic arrival. The circle of fifths is introduced there as a map of keys and signatures. Parallel-mode comparisons, modes, and altered minor harmony return in [[songwriting-part-6|Part 6: Bass Movement and Harmonic Color]]; they do not need to be learned during the first encounter with a scale.

Experienced readers can enter at the decision their draft needs. Earlier links are optional refreshers, not enforced prerequisites. The practical destination is a phrase whose note palette, home, and chords can be heard and explained.

## Patterns in sequence

13. [[songwriting-pitch-and-intervals|Intervals and Melodic Shape]] — Track distances and directions between notes.
14. [[songwriting-build-a-scale|Build a Scale from Steps]] — Construct a note palette from whole and half steps.
15. [[songwriting-scales-and-tonal-home|Establish a Tonal Home]] — Make a pitch feel like home through bass, repetition, and phrase endings, then choose the surrounding palette.
16. [[songwriting-build-and-hear-chords|Choose Chords by Their Notes]] — Inspect the chord tones beneath a fixed melody before replacing a familiar chord shape.
17. [[songwriting-chord-families-and-numbers|Think in Chord Numbers]] — Describe roots and chord qualities relative to a tonal center while keeping durations explicit.
18. [[songwriting-circle-of-fifths|Transpose the Song to Fit the Voice]] — Move melody and harmony together to a pitch level that suits the intended performer.
19. [[songwriting-progressions-and-arrival|Decide Where the Harmony Arrives]] — Coordinate chord order, change rate, and phrase endings to make a passage settle, stay open, or continue.
