**Reference collection · Songwriting**

Use the material below for a quick lookup or comparison. The related patterns at the end provide practical routes back into writing.

The three YouTube listening cards above pair a musical question with a time window: Radiohead's *True Love Waits* compares live and studio settings, Four Tet's *Two Thousand and Seventeen* explores repetition and changing context, and Bill Withers' *Lean on Me* focuses attention on melodic distances. The same cards appear in the relevant patterns. Choose a passage or the whole recording, load the player, then press play. A passage stops and resets for replay. The time windows are broad listening comparisons, not exact phrase or formal-section boundaries.

The pilot's recording identities were checked on 26 October 2026. YouTube controls embedding availability, regional access, sign-in, and advertising; playback timing is approximate. If a video cannot play here, use its direct YouTube link, which starts at the selected passage's beginning. The stopping point applies to the embedded player; an external YouTube page may continue beyond it. The course's original piano-roll demonstrations remain separate from these recordings.

A reference is most useful when it raises a musical question that can be investigated. These studies identify specific official source destinations and offer bounded listening perspectives. They do not claim to establish an artist's private intentions or a single definitive analysis of a recording.

Source identities were researched for this draft on 23–26 October 2026 using artist releases, official-channel metadata, score editions, and creator interviews. Availability can vary by region or change later. The course hosts no commercial recordings, score images, or lyric excerpts. Porch Light and Platform Three are original; short classical note reductions are identified with their score references. Listening interpretations describe particular relationships rather than certifying a complete transcription.

## Primary technical and creative references

**Open Music Theory** is a multi-author educational textbook. The course uses its terminology as a reference, while qualifying style-specific expectations. Useful destinations include [fundamentals](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/part/fundamentals/), [major scales and key signatures](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/major-scales/), [triads](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/triads/), [compound meter](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/compound-meters-and-time-signatures/), [swing](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/swing-rhythms/), [modal mixture](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/modal-mixture/), and [verse–chorus form](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheorycopy/chapter/verse-chorus-form/).

**Ableton Learning Music** supplies introductory explanations of [pulse and tempo](https://learningmusic.ableton.com/make-beats/beat-and-tempo.html), [keys and scales](https://learningmusic.ableton.com/notes-and-scales/keys-and-scales.html), [diatonic triads](https://learningmusic.ableton.com/advanced-topics/diatonic-triads.html), [inversions](https://learningmusic.ableton.com/advanced-topics/inversions.html), [voicings](https://learningmusic.ableton.com/advanced-topics/voicings.html), and [modes](https://learningmusic.ableton.com/advanced-topics/modes.html). Those external lessons include their own interactive exercises; the listening cards above focus on selected recording passages.

**Dennis DeSantis, Making Music**, published by Ableton, provides creative discussions of [active listening](https://makingmusic.ableton.com/active-listening), [melodic contour](https://makingmusic.ableton.com/creating-melodies-1-contour), [lyrical rhythm](https://makingmusic.ableton.com/the-rhythm-of-lyrics), [subtractive arranging](https://makingmusic.ableton.com/arranging-as-a-subtractive-process), and [dramatic arc](https://makingmusic.ableton.com/dramatic-arc). Our worked examples are independently authored rather than reproductions of its recipes or illustrations.

**Berklee** supplies primary educational context for [speech stress and deliberate missetting](https://www.berklee.edu/berklee-now/news/berklee-riffs-natural-music-in-language) and [songwriting tools and expressive alignment](https://online.berklee.edu/courses/songwriting-tools-and-techniques). These links support terminology and further reading; enrollment in an external course is not necessary to use this one.

Sources are linked beside relevant explanations throughout the patterns. Original examples are illustrative musical choices, not empirical claims that a technique guarantees a particular emotional response.
