Patterns and structures in stand-up comedy.

These concise articles explain each structure, show it in comedians’ material and identify the turn. Browse by the mechanism you want to understand; the families organize the repertoire rather than prescribing a writing sequence.

- [[joke-mechanics-expectation-and-reinterpretation|Expectation and Reinterpretation]] — A later beat changes the audience’s understanding of what came before.
- [[joke-mechanics-language-and-meaning|Language and Meaning]] — The laugh depends on how words are interpreted, combined or misused.
- [[joke-mechanics-comparison-and-contrast|Comparison and Contrast]] — The laugh comes from a relationship between different things.
- [[joke-mechanics-scale-and-development|Scale and Development]] — The joke changes magnitude, builds a sequence or extends a premise.
- [[joke-mechanics-perspective-and-character-logic|Perspective and Character Logic]] — The laugh reveals a peculiar way of interpreting or responding to the world.
- [[joke-mechanics-joke-conventions-and-audience-inference|Joke Conventions and Audience Inference]] — The laugh depends on expectations about jokes, conversations or omitted information.
- [[joke-mechanics-bit-construction-and-performance|Bit Construction and Performance]] — These forms can carry mechanisms from any of the six families above.

## About the examples

Quotations retain the guide’s complete supplied passages and published wording. Longer routines use descriptive accounts alongside recordings. Sources distinguish supplied quotations, published attributions, transcriptions and performance descriptions; a printed attribution is not a newly verified performance. Existing checked passage times are retained. Examples may illustrate several structures, and the same routine can appear in more than one article. Musical comedy and adjacent television or written material are identified in context. Descriptive working labels are marked within their articles.
