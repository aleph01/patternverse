Recurring predicaments often arise from the relationships among requirements, resources, participants, and decisions. This field guide groups them by the mechanism that keeps the difficulty going.

Use the families as entry points, then compare neighboring articles. Several structures can coexist in one situation; a familiar label is a hypothesis to examine, rather than evidence of an inevitable outcome or a ready-made solution.

An improvement after a change can support further investigation without proving which original mechanism caused the difficulty, especially when several changes were made together.

[[understand-systems|Systems Thinking Foundations]] introduces concepts and methods for examining events, relationships, and feedback. The articles here apply a systems perspective to recognizable traps and dilemmas.
