Use this collection when you want more than another version of the same idea. Its techniques help you generate different possibilities, discover uses for resources already available, and learn from a small next step.

## Find a starting point

[[five-alternative-frames]] offers a simple exercise: describe a situation in five substantially different ways before evaluating any of them. If you want a more focused technique, [[reframing-look-outside-frame]] explores context your present view excludes, while [[reframing-escape-hammer]] checks whether a familiar tool is narrowing the possibilities.

[[reframing-find-hidden-properties]] looks for useful properties in things you already have. [[reframing-break-tradeoff]] questions an apparent either-or choice, and [[reframing-alternative-paths]] holds an outcome steady while looking for different ways to reach it.

## Develop and try an option

[[reframing-generate-real-options]] helps distinguish meaningful alternatives from cosmetic variations. [[reframing-validate-frame]] turns a promising interpretation into a small real-world check. If beginning still feels too large, [[reduce-the-first-step]] lowers the initial commitment until there is something manageable to try.

The order moves from opening possibilities toward action, but these are independent tools. You do not need to use every technique or produce a perfect idea before taking a useful step.

If you are unsure what problem you are trying to solve, begin with [[reframing]]. Together, the two collections support a cycle of reconsidering the problem, finding alternatives, and learning from what happens.
