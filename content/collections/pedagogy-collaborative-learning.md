These methods give learners distinct ways to prepare, contribute, listen, examine evidence, and
revise their thinking. They form a repertoire rather than a required sequence. The articles
preserve uncertainty about research findings and distinguish a useful participation structure
from a guarantee of learning.

- [[think-pair-share|Think–Pair–Share]] gives each learner time to form an idea before discussion.
- [[jigsaw-learning|Jigsaw Learning]] connects complementary contributions through peer teaching.
- [[snowball-groups|Snowball Groups]] develops individual ideas through progressively larger conversations.
- [[peer-instruction|Peer Instruction]] examines the reasons behind different conceptual answers.
- [[think-aloud-pair-problem-solving|Think-Aloud Pair Problem Solving]] separates solving from listening.
- [[gallery-walk|Gallery Walk]] connects work across groups and returns feedback to revision.
- [[fishbowl-discussion|Fishbowl Discussion]] makes observation and reflection part of learning to discuss.
- [[structured-academic-controversy|Structured Academic Controversy]] examines competing perspectives before judgment.
