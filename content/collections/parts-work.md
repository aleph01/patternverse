You want to move forward and keep pulling back. One response demands action; another anticipates a cost that the plan seems to overlook. Parts work offers ways to listen to these different perspectives and understand how they interact.

“Parts” can mean recognizable feelings, motives, or ways of responding. You do not need to experience them as separate personalities. This collection brings together approaches from applied rationality, Internal Family Systems, Gestalt therapy, and Emotion-Focused Therapy while keeping their differences visible.

## Start with a disagreement

[[internal-double-crux|Internal Double Crux]] explores an internal disagreement through alternating acknowledgment and one concern at a time. Its [[explore-an-internal-disagreement|guided writing practice]] provides space for a short exchange and a review of what remains uncertain. Understanding a concern, choosing a practical limit, or leaving a question open can all be useful outcomes.

## Make room to listen

- [[unblending|Unblending]] — Notice a perspective without being entirely absorbed in it, while staying connected to the experience.
- [[getting-permission-from-the-protector|Getting Permission from the Protector]] — Understand a protective objection before approaching material it is concerned about.

## Explore the interaction

- [[two-chair-dialogue|Two-Chair Dialogue]] — Give distinguishable inner positions separate voices through enactment; the clinical task depends on the particular conflict.
- [[mediating-polarized-parts|Mediating Polarized Parts]] — Examine how opposing protective strategies intensify each other and explore a less partisan relationship with both.

The articles are a repertoire, not a required sequence or a single treatment model. Internal Double Crux comes from CFAR's applied-rationality curriculum; it does not require the IFS concepts of Self or protective parts. The neighboring [[somatic-protection-and-inner-conflict|Protection and Inner Conflict]] collection places several of these patterns within a broader experiential-therapy context.
