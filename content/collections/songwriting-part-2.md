Rhythm is more than a drum pattern. The voice, bass, chords, and silence all contribute to how a song moves. This part separates pulse from activity, beat grouping from subdivision, and swing from placement around the beat.

The original examples count their time explicitly. A rest still occupies beats; a pickup does not move the bar line; a bass anticipation can cooperate with a stable drum reference. These distinctions help a writer describe an idea to another performer.

This part follows the opening miniature but can also be read independently when timing language is unclear. Use [[songwriting-reading-the-examples]] for chart conventions. Platform Three supplies a contrasting instrumental groove alongside the vocal song.

## Patterns in sequence

7. [[songwriting-pulse-and-tempo|Set the Pace for the Phrase]] — Choose tempo by singing the phrase, then adjust activity separately from the underlying pace.
8. [[songwriting-beats-bars-and-meter|Group the Beat Around the Phrase]] — Choose a recurring beat grouping that supports the phrase's accents and return points.
9. [[songwriting-subdivision-and-rests|Make Space Part of the Rhythm]] — Place and count silences as deliberately as notes so the next entrance has room to speak.
10. [[songwriting-syncopation-and-anticipation|Pull an Entrance Across the Beat]] — Move one entrance against a clear beat reference to create a pickup, a push, or a delayed confirmation.
11. [[songwriting-swing-and-timing|Give the Groove a Consistent Feel]] — Choose a repeatable timing relationship between parts instead of scattering random timing changes.
12. [[songwriting-interlock-the-groove|Interlock the Groove]] — Give rhythmic parts complementary jobs and room to be heard.
