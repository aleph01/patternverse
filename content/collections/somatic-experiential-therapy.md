A person may explain a difficulty accurately and still find that the explanation does not touch the experience. Somatic and experiential approaches attend to what happens while a person senses, speaks, moves, imagines, or encounters another person. This collection describes forty-six reusable moves drawn from several clinical traditions.

The nine families organize functions rather than treatment stages. A session may move among them repeatedly. Familiar words do not make the traditions interchangeable: each article preserves its lineage, distinguishes neighboring patterns, and describes relevant limits. Loving Presence is presented as a relational stance. Six narrower techniques remain inside their parent articles rather than becoming additional patterns.

This is an unpublished reading edition with no interactive practices, worksheets, or executable exercises. Examples are fictional teaching illustrations, not clinical case reports or promises of outcomes. Articles explain professional methods; they are not a trauma-treatment protocol. Evocative enactment, physical intervention, memory work, and work with dissociation require clinical judgment and an appropriately trained practitioner. Consent includes declining, changing, or stopping an intervention.

Source links identify how a tradition describes its methods. Evidence for a therapy package does not establish the effectiveness or proposed biological mechanism of each component. Feeling that an interpretation fits does not establish historical accuracy, another person’s intentions, or present safety. Current circumstances, physical health, culture, and material constraints remain relevant.
