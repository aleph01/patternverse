Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT) brings acceptance and change together. These twelve articles and accompanying interactive practices offer different ways to work with difficult moments. Choose the skill that fits your situation; this collection is not a course you must complete in order.

## Find a place to begin

[[dbt-wise-mind|Wise Mind]] brings feelings and practical information together. [[dbt-stop|STOP]] creates a brief pause before acting. [[dbt-identify-describe-emotions|Identify and Describe Emotions]] helps distinguish the parts of an emotional episode.

[[dbt-check-the-facts|Check the Facts]] examines interpretations and uncertainty. [[dbt-opposite-action|Opposite Action]] considers a safe alternative when an emotional urge would be unhelpful. [[dbt-self-soothe|Self-Soothe]] explores personal sensory support, while [[dbt-pros-and-cons|Pros and Cons]] compares immediate and later consequences of an urge and an alternative.

[[dbt-validation|Validation and Self-Validation]] helps acknowledge experience accurately. [[dbt-dear-man|DEAR MAN]] supports a clear request or refusal. [[dbt-cope-ahead|Cope Ahead]] rehearses a response before a foreseeable challenge.

[[dbt-radical-acceptance|Radical Acceptance]] acknowledges a present fact without requiring approval. [[dbt-behavior-chain|Behavior Chain and Solution Analysis]] looks closely at one episode to identify possible intervention points, prevention, and repair.

The articles and self-guided practices are original educational adaptations of the cited sources, not a complete DBT treatment program. Choose a manageable situation or use a fictional example, and keep control of pace and disclosure. Behavior involving serious harm is better explored with qualified support, with immediate safety and care taking priority.
