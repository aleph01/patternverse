Good decisions do not require perfect certainty. They require a useful question, attention to consequences and preferences, and a way to learn what matters.

These eight patterns cover different parts of that work: reversibility, framing, failure modes, experiments, trade-offs, learning over time, uncertain outcomes, and the value of information. The order follows the authored set, not a required sequence. Choose the move that fits the decision in front of you.

Each article includes the original practice instructions and an offline guided worksheet. Earlier responses remain visible as the steps unfold. Worksheet answers are not saved when you leave; copy anything you want to keep, especially a decision-journal snapshot. The worksheets do not make decisions for you, calculate authoritative scores, or create reminders.
