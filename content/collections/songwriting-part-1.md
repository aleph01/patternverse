A first song does not need to wait until every term in this course is familiar. These patterns begin with the musical material a reader can already notice: a fragment, a repeated gesture, a line of language, or the sound of a favorite recording.

The worked miniature in pattern 6 shows how a small idea can acquire a beginning, contrast, return, and ending. It is complete at its own scale. Later theory explains and expands choices that are already audible in that design.

Read this part first if the main difficulty is starting or finishing. The recurring examples are preserved in [[songwriting-example-songbook]], with note names and durations explained in [[songwriting-reading-the-examples]].

## Patterns in sequence

1. [[songwriting-what-makes-a-song|Protect the Song's Identity]] — Identify the relationship that makes the song recognizable and preserve it while other material changes.
2. [[songwriting-five-ways-to-begin|Begin with What You Have]] — Begin with the musical material that already suggests a next step.
3. [[songwriting-repetition-variation-surprise|Repeat with a Reason]] — Establish a recognizable idea and change it with a clear musical purpose.
4. [[songwriting-listen-like-a-songwriter|Listen Like a Songwriter]] — Turn listening observations into specific songwriting questions.
5. [[songwriting-capture-musical-seeds|Capture Musical Seeds]] — Preserve a fragment together with the context needed to develop it.
6. [[songwriting-finish-a-tiny-song|Finish a Tiny Song]] — Complete a small musical arc before expanding the scale of the work.
