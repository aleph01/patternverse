A language model can help you develop an idea, but fluency is not the same as good judgment. These eight articles describe ways to make the collaboration easier to steer and its results easier to inspect.

Each article starts with a recognizable situation, explains the core idea and why it works, describes how to use it, and develops a worked example. Limitations distinguish useful prompting from evidence that a result is correct.

Start with [[draft-the-prompt-together|Draft the Prompt Together]] when the request needs clarification, or [[work-in-inspectable-steps|Work in Inspectable Steps]] when a large task needs meaningful checkpoints. Explore alternatives before selecting; preserve essential properties during revision; investigate the unknown that could change the decision; communicate boundaries through contrast; choose a useful representation; and review important work through focused audit or attack-and-repair passes.

Use these approaches as a repertoire rather than a mandatory sequence. The task and its consequences determine how much structure and checking are useful. For broader approaches to choosing under uncertainty, explore [[decision-making|Decision Making]].
