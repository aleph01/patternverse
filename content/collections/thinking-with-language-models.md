A language model can help you develop an idea, but fluency is not the same as good judgment. These articles describe ways to structure the collaboration so that possibilities, assumptions, constraints, and weaknesses become easier to inspect.

Use them as a repertoire, not a mandatory sequence. Explore when the answer space is open; preserve essential properties during revision; find the missing information that changes the next step; show subtle boundaries through contrast; make structure visible; and challenge consequential proposals before repairing them.

For broader approaches to choosing under uncertainty, explore [[decision-making|Decision Making]]. The same questions about evidence, alternatives, and trade-offs matter whether you are thinking alone or with a language model.
