Lyrics are language performed in time. These patterns develop a specific situation, choose a speaker, select consequential details, examine sound and stress, and change what later returns can mean.

Porch Light provides complete original lines rather than borrowed commercial lyrics. Its first verse establishes a hesitant return; its second introduces a note; its bridge revises the speaker's assumption. The chorus can remain recognizable while its significance changes.

Writers who begin from words can enter here after Part I, using the rhythm and melody patterns as references. A lyric does not need every device in this part. Direct statement, imagery, rhyme, and ambiguity are choices to judge in their musical context.

## Patterns in sequence

26. [[songwriting-find-the-central-idea|Find the Central Idea]] — Turn a broad subject into a specific dramatic or emotional situation.
27. [[songwriting-choose-the-speaker|Choose the Speaker]] — Select a lyrical viewpoint that reveals the intended relationship and stakes.
28. [[songwriting-write-through-images|Write Through Images]] — Let concrete details carry meaning while retaining the direct statements the song needs.
29. [[songwriting-rhyme-and-lyric-sound|Let Sound Support the Lyric]] — Choose rhyme and vowel relationships that reinforce the speaker's thought without forcing the wording.
30. [[songwriting-words-against-music|Make Speech and Melody Agree]] — Place musical emphasis where the sung sentence needs it, revising either words or notes when they conflict.
31. [[songwriting-make-the-lyric-develop|Make the Lyric Develop]] — Give successive sections new information that changes the meaning of the return.
