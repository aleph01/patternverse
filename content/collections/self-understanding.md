Sometimes the useful first move is to find words for what is happening. These three patterns offer different ways into a situation: how a conclusion was reached, what assumption may be shaping it, and what matters to the person experiencing it.

[[cbt-thinking-patterns|Identify Cognitive Distortions]] helps recognize thinking habits such as mind reading or overgeneralization. [[identify-limiting-beliefs|Identify Limiting Beliefs]] explores propositions, expectations, and rules that may be operating beneath a reaction. [[identify-needs|Identify Needs]] offers vocabulary for concerns such as rest, belonging, choice, and clarity.

The same moment can be understood through more than one lens. “They have not replied, so nobody cares about me” may contain a leap in reasoning, an assumption about how care must be shown, and a wish for connection. Recognizing one does not settle the others. A need is not a cognitive distortion, and a painful belief may describe a real difficulty.

Use the reference material to explore possible wording, or open a pattern's interactive practice. Recognition is a starting point for understanding, not a diagnosis or a requirement to change what you feel.
