**Reference collection · Songwriting**

Use the material below for a quick lookup or comparison. The related patterns at the end provide practical routes back into writing.

A draft rarely announces which theory pattern it needs. Begin with the difficulty you can describe, then use the linked articles to separate possible causes. These routes are reading suggestions, not interactive assessments or diagnoses.

## I cannot get started

If the blank page feels larger than the available idea, begin with [[songwriting-five-ways-to-begin]]. If ideas appear but disappear between sessions, use [[songwriting-capture-musical-seeds]]. If starting is easy and finishing never happens, read [[songwriting-finish-a-tiny-song]] before expanding the project.

## I have a loop but no song

Read [[songwriting-repetition-variation-surprise]] to separate identity from development, then [[songwriting-choose-a-song-form]] to compare larger maps. [[songwriting-pacing-transitions-endings]] explains how the spans connect. Adding a new instrument every few bars is one possibility, not the only form of development.

## My chords work, but everything sounds the same

[[songwriting-progressions-and-arrival]] changes duration and phrase direction before adding harmonic complexity. [[songwriting-inversions-and-voice-leading]] changes the connection without necessarily replacing the chords. [[songwriting-borrow-a-chord]] and [[songwriting-point-to-a-temporary-home]] offer specific chromatic alternatives once the basic relationships are clear.

## I do not understand the circle of fifths

Read [[songwriting-pitch-and-intervals]] and [[songwriting-chord-families-and-numbers]] if the vocabulary is unfamiliar, then [[songwriting-circle-of-fifths]]. Its job is to map relationships and support transposition, not supply an automatic next chord.

## My melody sounds like an exercise

[[songwriting-start-with-a-motif]] develops a small identity rather than a stream of notes. [[songwriting-phrases-and-breathing]] supplies punctuation. [[songwriting-melody-and-harmony]] compares stable notes and deliberate color. A more unusual scale is not always the missing ingredient.

## My chorus feels like another verse

Use [[songwriting-jobs-of-song-sections]] to identify what should change, then [[songwriting-make-the-chorus-arrive]]. [[songwriting-write-a-hook]] helps clarify the focal feature. Compare the approach and arrival together; a chorus’s isolated sound does not reveal every boundary problem.

## My second verse adds nothing

[[songwriting-make-the-second-verse-count]] asks what new information matters. [[songwriting-make-the-lyric-develop]] follows the consequences across the full song. [[songwriting-write-through-images]] can make the new information specific without merely paraphrasing the original feeling.

## My words sound awkward when sung

[[songwriting-words-against-music]] checks syllables, stress, and duration. [[songwriting-rhyme-and-lyric-sound]] examines forced wording and vowel relationships. [[songwriting-vocal-delivery-and-harmonies]] separates a setting problem from a performance choice.

## My lyric has nice lines but no center

[[songwriting-find-the-central-idea]] narrows a subject into a situation. [[songwriting-choose-the-speaker]] clarifies perspective and address. [[songwriting-write-through-images]] tests whether an attractive detail contributes to that relationship.

## My groove is stiff or crowded

[[songwriting-swing-and-timing]] separates subdivision spacing from placement around the beat. [[songwriting-interlock-the-groove]] assigns complementary rhythmic roles. [[songwriting-write-a-bass-line]] gives the low part direction and intentional note endings.

## My bridge sounds like a different song

[[songwriting-earn-the-bridge]] asks what the section changes and whether it is needed. [[songwriting-develop-without-losing-the-idea]] supplies related musical material. [[songwriting-pacing-transitions-endings]] can solve a weak return without an additional full section.

## My arrangement hides the song

[[songwriting-give-each-part-a-job]] distinguishes useful layers from continuous activity. [[songwriting-choose-a-sonic-setting]] compares different presentations of the same writing. [[songwriting-vocal-delivery-and-harmonies]] checks whether doubling or harmony is obscuring the lead.

## The song has no overall arc

[[songwriting-shape-energy-over-time]] works with entrances, exits, density, register, and silence. [[songwriting-choose-a-song-form]] checks the map. [[songwriting-pacing-transitions-endings]] checks its durations. More loudness alone may leave the actual relationship unchanged.

## I keep revising and never finish

[[songwriting-revise-the-weakest-moment]] turns a vague complaint into a focused comparison. [[songwriting-finish-and-learn]] defines an intelligible finished version and a useful next lesson. The [[songwriting-example-songbook|original songbook]] shows how a base version can coexist with labeled alternatives.
