A song becomes clear through its presentation and its stopping decisions. This part follows sonic setting, instrumental roles, changing energy, vocal delivery, focused revision, and completion of a simple demo.

Arrangement is treated as compositional work: entrances, register, duration, and absence can matter as much as new notes. Revision distinguishes material, arrangement, performance, and recording problems so a writer can change the layer that needs attention.

The final pattern defines completion around an intelligible representation of the song. It does not require professional production or unanimous approval. The [[songwriting-troubleshooting|troubleshooting index]] offers direct routes back to relevant patterns when a specific draft problem appears.

## Patterns in sequence

44. [[songwriting-choose-a-sonic-setting|Choose the World Around the Song]] — Select a coherent sound setting that exposes the writing's essential relationships.
45. [[songwriting-give-each-part-a-job|Give Each Part a Job]] — Clarify an arrangement by assigning distinct musical functions to its parts.
46. [[songwriting-shape-energy-over-time|Shape Energy over Time]] — Build an expressive arc through entrances, exits, density, register, and silence.
47. [[songwriting-vocal-delivery-and-harmonies|Let the Voice Carry the Meaning]] — Choose delivery and backing voices around the lead sentence's meaning, adding layers selectively.
48. [[songwriting-revise-the-weakest-moment|Revise the Weakest Moment]] — Diagnose a specific listening problem and compare focused revisions against the song's intent.
49. [[songwriting-finish-and-learn|Finish and Learn]] — Complete a clear demo, gather useful feedback, and carry a specific lesson into the next song.
