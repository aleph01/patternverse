How should the organization’s major parts be arranged?

Explore patterns for grouping work, locating authority, and coordinating across organizational units. Arrangements can coexist at different levels of the same organization.

[[functional-organization|Functional Organization]] — Group people by specialist discipline and coordinate their contributions across functions.

[[divisional-organization|Divisional Organization]] — Group capabilities around products, markets, or regions and give each division responsibility for its results.

[[matrix-organization|Matrix Organization]] — Distribute formal authority across intersecting dimensions and make their shared decisions workable.

[[federated-organization|Federated Organization]] — Preserve member self-government while giving shared institutions a defined mandate and mutual obligations.
[[shared-services|Shared Services]] — Pool recurring services across organizational units, with clear service ownership and commitments to the units they serve.
[[front-back-organization|Front-Back Organization]] — Connect customer-facing units with shared product capabilities, balancing market needs with reusable offerings and scale.
[[ambidextrous-organization|Ambidextrous Organization]] — Run established and exploratory work with different operating arrangements, linked by shared leadership and deliberate resource decisions.
[[microenterprise-network|Microenterprise Network]] — Give small units entrepreneurial responsibility and connect them through explicit agreements, shared capabilities, and common rules.
