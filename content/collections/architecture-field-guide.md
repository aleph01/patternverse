A building is a place to arrive, move, work, rest and care for over time. This guide connects those lived situations with form, climate, construction and design decisions.

Start with a situation: a deserted courtyard, a gloomy room, a confusing entrance or an old building whose future is uncertain. Each guide explains a reusable idea, concrete examples and the conditions that can make it fail.

## Reading the evidence

Established names retain their authorship: Alexander and colleagues for selected patterns from *A Pattern Language*, Browning, Ryan and Clancy for Prospect and Refuge, and Brand after Duffy for Shearing Layers. Other guides are explicitly identified as modern syntheses. Historical propositions are evaluated rather than presented as universal rules.

Photographs show real buildings and preserve creator, source and license information. Original schematic plans and sections explain relationships; they are not measured drawings of the photographed precedents or construction details. Hypothetical examples are labeled as such.
