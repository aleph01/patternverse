This optional deeper part expands harmony once the basic song is already intelligible. It distinguishes inversions, voicings, bass lines, chord colors, parallel-mode borrowing, secondary dominants, modal centers, and established key changes.

Every harmonic substitution needs a melody check. A new accompaniment note can create an unintended clash with a previously comfortable vocal line. The examples therefore spell important pitches and distinguish a change of voicing from a changed progression.

Readers finishing a first simple song can move from Part V to Part VII and return here later. The material offers alternatives, not a requirement that a mature song contain more complicated chords.

## Patterns in sequence

32. [[songwriting-inversions-and-voice-leading|Connect Chords with Small Moves]] — Keep useful common tones and move the remaining voices deliberately while giving the bass its own path.
33. [[songwriting-write-a-bass-line|Write a Bass Line]] — Connect harmonic support and rhythmic identity through a purposeful low part.
34. [[songwriting-sevenths-added-notes-suspensions|Add Color to a Working Chord]] — Add or replace one chord tone under a fixed phrase, keeping the color only when its relationship helps.
35. [[songwriting-borrow-a-chord|Borrow a Chord]] — Use a chord from the parallel mode to change a particular harmonic moment.
36. [[songwriting-point-to-a-temporary-home|Point to a Temporary Home]] — Emphasize a destination chord with its own dominant before returning to the wider key.
37. [[songwriting-modes-drones-and-key-changes|Change the Tonal World Deliberately]] — Choose whether to keep the center, change its surrounding palette, or establish a new home during the song.
