Scheduling assumptions and limits

Validate takes 2 minutes; Prepare 3; Train 8 per attempt; Evaluate 4; Package 2; Release 1. Durations are fixed. Slots are identical, each step uses one slot, and there is no startup overhead, queueing from other runs, caching, resuming inside a failed attempt or resource contention. Ready steps start immediately in the listed priority order; Evaluate precedes Package when only one slot is available.

Train is allowed at most two attempts with an immediate retry and no backoff. A failed attempt consumes all eight minutes and publishes no model. Other steps do not fail here. Completed upstream outputs remain available within the run. The chosen evaluation decision is a preset report outcome, not a metric calculated from a trained model. Rejecting a model is not an execution failure and triggers no retry.

Packaging here only assembles existing artifacts and preserves their prediction behavior. A conversion that changes behavior would need evaluation afterward. Outputs are logical artifact labels only. No files are created, models trained, external services called or releases deployed. A production runner needs durable state, safe publication and idempotent external operations in addition to dependency scheduling.
