Exact rules and assumptions

R matches the whole English word “refund”; B matches “charged twice” with one or more whitespace characters between the words; D matches the whole word “delivered”. Matching ignores case. A nonmatch abstains. The copied source runs exactly R's rule; disabling R also disables its copy.

The first calculation counts every active column. The second counts the known R family once. Each sums the Refund weight and Other weight, divides Refund weight by total emitted weight, and selects Refund above 50% or Other below 50%. A 50% tie stays unresolved. No votes means no weak label.

The sample matrix uses A: “Refund please; tracking says delivered.” B: “Charged twice. Please refund.” C: “Package delivered, thank you.” D: “Where is my parcel?” Coverage, overlap, and conflict use all four rows as their denominator and count active columns, including a copy. They describe these fixed samples, not the editable ticket or population accuracy.

This is an aggregation baseline. It does not estimate weights, fit a probabilistic label model, train a downstream classifier, or measure predictive performance. Counting a known exact copy once does not solve unknown source dependencies.
