Objectives, exact fitting, and limits

Every A record has target 5 minutes. Half of B has target 7 − s and half 7 + s, where s is the selected spread. Prediction is 5 + θ for all records. Squared error is (prediction − target)². Group mean loss divides by that group's count; sample mean divides total loss by 100. Variance here uses the group's count as denominator, not count minus one.

Compute each group's mean residual m_g relative to 5 and variance v_g from the actual records. Its mean loss is (θ − m_g)² + v_g. The average-loss optimum is the pooled mean residual. The equal-group optimum is (m_A + m_B)/2. To minimize the maximum of these two quadratics exactly, evaluate their individual minima and their intersection, and choose the candidate with lowest maximum loss. Exact ties use smaller θ. This solves this specific convex constant-predictor problem; it is not an iterative group-DRO training algorithm.

The hypothetical mixture score is (1 − π_B)L_A + π_B L_B for each already fitted model. Changing π_B changes this calculation only. It does not create evaluation data or establish population generalization. Counts change the sample-average objective and estimation support; the specified maximum-group objective does not weight groups by count.

All offered groups are nonempty and all labels are correct by fixture construction. There are no confidence intervals, missing groups, learned features, subgroup intersections, gradient updates, adaptive adversarial-weight dynamics, regularization, or neural-network results in this simulation. High within-B variance is an unavoidable loss floor only for this constant predictor, not proof of inherently unpredictable outcomes.
