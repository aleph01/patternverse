Assumptions and optional detail

There are six Day 1 predictions and six Day 3 predictions. Each cohort has two routine and four complex cases. All have model M1 and transform T1. A cohort becomes eligible at prediction day + 2, including equality; this is a minimum-age policy, not proof that every outcome is complete. Integer days denote UTC boundaries. This example has no sliding-window overlap or changing model weights.

Original label arrivals for R01–R06 are Days 3, 3, 4, 3, 3, 3. For R07–R12 they are Days 4, 4, 6, 6, 7, and never. Early labels in the newer cohort are withheld from metrics until Day 5 eligibility. Alternate timing releases every known fixture outcome at its cohort's minimum age. It cannot create real missing labels; it is a hypothetical timing comparison. One label with unmatched ID X99 arrives on Day 4 and is reported separately, never joined by position.

Error rate = wrong predictions / usable joined labels. Coverage = usable joined labels / eligible predictions. Zero denominators display N/A. The policy waits for cohort eligibility, then requires at least four labels before treating observed error above 25% as an investigation signal. A below-limit result also needs at least 80% coverage to say no configured breach; otherwise evidence is insufficient. Equality at both thresholds passes. Coverage remains visible even when an observed breach triggers investigation.

These fixed thresholds are teaching rules with no statistical confidence guarantee. Duplicate prediction or label IDs are rejected rather than arbitrarily resolved. Later unavailable labels cannot affect an earlier report. No labels are imputed, no causal effect is estimated, and no alerts, requests, retraining or deployments are sent.
