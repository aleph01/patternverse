Exact fitting, metric, and binning assumptions

Each split has ten low-logit and ten high-logit cases, with distinct IDs. Each group has the selected number of positive labels, followed by negative labels. Base logits are fixed at ±ln(9). The mapping is p = sigmoid(z/T) with a shared positive T constrained to [0.25, 10]. The base model's original fitting data are outside this toy.

For these equal-magnitude logits, calibration negative log-likelihood depends only on q, the fraction of calibration labels matching the sign prediction (negative for the low group, positive for the high group). Its unconstrained optimum has high-group probability q. Clip q to [sigmoid(ln(9)/10), sigmoid(ln(9)/0.25)], then solve T = ln(9) / log(q/(1−q)). This is the exact bounded optimum for this special dataset, not a general fitting algorithm. Boundary optima are reported. Evaluation labels and bin counts are never supplied to fitting.

Brier loss is mean (p−y)²; log loss is mean −y ln(p) − (1−y) ln(1−p), in nats. Accuracy uses p ≥ 0.5. The decision count uses p > 0.85 (strictly positive benefit for a visit costing 85 and preventing loss 100 under the article's assumptions), allowing 1e-12 roundoff at the boundary.

For B equal-width bins use [j/B,(j+1)/B), including 1 in the last bin, with 1e-12 roundoff when assigning boundary probabilities. Each point uses that bin's mean positive-class probability and fraction of positive labels. Binned error is sum over nonempty bins of (bin cases / total cases) × absolute difference between those two means. It is not top-label multiclass ECE. One broad bin can cancel opposing errors. Changing bins never changes T, individual predictions, or proper losses.

Scores are not guaranteed to transfer between the synthetic calibration and evaluation scenarios. Twenty cases per split are too few to certify population calibration. There is no uncertainty interval, subgroup guarantee, class-prior correction, arbitrary-logit optimizer, or external model call. One temperature cannot fit every asymmetric pattern or reverse a wrong ordering.
