Fit two small least-squares models to fictional trip durations: separate car and wet indicators, or those indicators plus car × wet. Other targets stay fixed at bike–dry 20, bike–wet 40 and car–dry 30 minutes. Withholding car–wet keeps its target outside both fits.

Inspect encoded inputs, coefficients and fitted cells

c is 1 for car, otherwise 0. w is 1 for wet, otherwise 0. The product c × w selects car–wet only. Four joint one-hot slots could encode bike–dry, bike–wet, car–dry and car–wet; the fits instead use reference coding [1, c, w, cw]. Arbitrary category ID multiplication is not used.

Fitting assumptions and limits

Each available cell has equal weight. A small linear-equation solver minimizes the sum of squared residuals, with an intercept and no regularization. Mean absolute error is separately reported in minutes: sum the absolute prediction errors and divide by the number of evaluated cells. No noise, trip distribution, causal intervention or real travel dataset is modeled.

With all four cells, four crossed coefficients can reproduce four supplied targets exactly. With car–wet withheld, cw is zero in every training row; its coefficient is unidentified. The stated fallback fixes it to zero and fits the remaining three coefficients. This is not a generic minimum-norm or regularized solver default. The remaining rows have full rank for that three-column fit.

Only bike/car and dry/wet are supported; unknown category values are rejected. The explorer performs exact indicator crossing, not hashing. Changing the reserved car–wet target while it is withheld cannot change the fitted coefficients. A single synthetic held-out error does not estimate production performance.
