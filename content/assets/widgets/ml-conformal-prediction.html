Exact construction and scope of the guarantee

The base model is frozen and its training data are outside this toy. Nine calibration records C01–C09 have predictions [20,25,30,35,40,45,50,55,60] and outcomes [24,24,37,33,49,42,55,53,64]. Selecting n uses the first n records, changing both sample size and observed errors. Calibration and evaluation IDs do not overlap.

Compute each absolute residual, sort numerically, and use one-based rank k = ceil((n+1) × coverage / 100). When k ≤ n, q is the kth smallest residual. Otherwise q = +infinity, giving an unbounded interval. Endpoints are included; ties can make coverage conservative. No interpolation, rounding of q, or finite cap is applied. All intervals follow the real-valued mathematical rule; negative endpoints are not truncated to zero.

Evaluation predictions are 25,30,…,70 minutes. Fixed signed errors are [1,−2,3,−4,5,−6,7,−8,9,12]. The last two records are complex. The harder-complex scenario replaces their errors with 15 and −18. These artificial scenarios demonstrate arithmetic and potential mismatch, not a sampling model or verification of exchangeability. Changing evaluation outcomes or the new point prediction never recalibrates q.

For a fixed scoring rule, exchangeable calibration and future examples support a marginal coverage statement that averages over calibration and new observations. This is not a guarantee for each fixed calibration sample, input value, subgroup, or finite batch, and it is not a posterior probability for this particular interval. No uncertainty interval for the measured coverage is estimated. The unknown new point is not counted in evaluation.

Evaluation plots use a fixed 0–100 minute axis. Infinite intervals have arrows at both ends and extend beyond the displayed axis. The full real line trivially covers all outcomes but provides no finite planning bound. The base predictions, score family, and labels are synthetic; no external model is called and no model is trained.
