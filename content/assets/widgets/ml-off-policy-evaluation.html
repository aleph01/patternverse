Assumptions and exact rules

Eight fixed contexts contain four Routine and four Complex requests. With mixed coverage, the logger independently chooses A with probability 0.80 and B with probability 0.20. The displayed A/A/A/B sequence in each group is one possible realization, not a forced quota. The A-only scenario is a different synthetic collection policy: it assigns probability one to A and zero to B and logs A on every row. Changing the propensity assumption never rewrites the true logged probabilities or actions.

Rewards are immediate, complete, and deterministic in the toy world. The approximate reward model predicts Routine A/B as 0.60/0.40 and Complex A/B as 0.30/0.80. The flat model predicts 0.50. The exact toy model copies each row's potential rewards; it is an unrealistic oracle used to expose the estimator identities, not a trained model or a production option.

All targets here are fixed deterministic policies. DM averages q(x,target). Weight w is 1/p_used on matching rows and zero otherwise. IPS averages w × observed reward over all eight rows. DR averages q(x,target) + w × (observed reward − q(x,observed action)). The correct denominator is the logged chosen-action probability. The deliberately incorrect uniform assumption instead uses 0.50; it is a counterexample, not a recommended correction.

Support is checked against true logging probabilities for every target action in these contexts. Missing support blocks IPS and DR; DM remains visible but is labeled model extrapolation. Weight concentration is (sum w)²/sum(w²), zero when there are no matches. These diagnostics do not certify statistical significance.

Exact toy expectations use linearity: for each context, sum each possible action's IPS or DR contribution times its true logging probability, then average over contexts. The full-information target mean averages that target's potential rewards. This known toy truth is never fed into the sample estimates except when explicitly selecting the exact toy reward model.

No model fitting, uncertainty intervals, policy search, estimated-confounding corrections, repeated users, adaptive assignment, delayed outcomes, or sequential rewards are simulated. Double robustness needs support and appropriate sampling/model assumptions; it cannot restore unobserved actions or guarantee an accurate finite sample.
