Recovery assumptions and limits

The prediction is one dimensionless weight w. For a target y, half squared-error loss gives gradient g = w − y. Each update first sets v = 0.50 × v + g, then w = w − 0.10 × v using the new v. Initial w and v are zero. The data cursor selects the next target in the repeating array [1, 3].

Complete restoration keeps w, v, update count and data cursor. Weights + data position resets v but retains progress; weights + optimizer retains v and progress but resets the cursor. Weights only resets v, progress and cursor. The progress counter does not drive this fixed learning rate or reconstruct the cursor. Differences here describe recovery behavior, not which model predicts better. A match compares weight, momentum, counter and cursor at every recovery update with numerical tolerance 10⁻¹².

The checkpoint is an in-memory JSON snapshot with known toy run, data, algorithm and format identities and a completed marker. Invalid or incomplete snapshots are rejected by the loader. No disk write, storage failure, framework checkpoint, randomness, distributed coordination or asynchronous snapshot is simulated. A matching short trace cannot establish a complete recovery procedure in general.
