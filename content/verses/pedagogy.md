Pedagogy concerns the choices that make learning possible: how a question is framed, who has time
to think, how learners encounter other perspectives, and what happens after an explanation or
discussion.

Begin with [[pedagogy-collaborative-learning|Collaborative Learning]], eight field-guide articles
about structured ways to think and learn with others. Each explains a method through a concrete
classroom example, an illustration, useful adaptations, and its limitations.

Use [[think-pair-share|Think–Pair–Share]] when learners need time to form a contribution;
[[jigsaw-learning|Jigsaw Learning]] when complementary contributions need to become shared
understanding; or [[structured-academic-controversy|Structured Academic Controversy]] when competing
cases need careful examination before a judgment.

Choose a structure for the learning problem in front of you. Participation, agreement, and a
polished group product each need to be examined rather than treated as proof of understanding.
