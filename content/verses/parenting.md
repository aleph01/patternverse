Parenting brings recurring situations in which a child’s feelings, developing abilities, and wish for independence meet responsibilities that adults still need to carry. The same visible behavior can call for different responses: difficulty starting homework, for example, might involve a missing skill, fear of mistakes, or difficulty leaving a game.

The [[parenting-field-guide|Parenting Field Guide]] contains 27 articles organized around recognizable difficulties. Each guide helps you consider possible explanations, choose a response, and observe whether it is helping. Age and individual differences are addressed within the articles.

## Find a starting point

- [[parenting-emotions-frustration-and-confidence|Emotions, frustration, and confidence]]: Start here when blocked goals, losing, difficult tasks, or mistakes lead to distress or avoidance.

- [[parenting-worries-separation-and-avoidance|Worries, separation, and avoidance]]: Distinguish unfamiliarity, repeated worry, separation distress, and difficulties connected specifically to school.

- [[parenting-boundaries-and-cooperation|Boundaries and cooperation]]: Choose responses to instructions, repeated requests, disputed rules, or physical aggression.

- [[parenting-relationships-and-connection|Relationships and connection]]: Separate ordinary disagreements and access disputes from exclusion, repeated harm, and changes in connection.

- [[parenting-daily-routines-and-habits|Daily routines and habits]]: Investigate the practical, emotional, and physical barriers behind recurring difficulties with everyday routines.

- [[parenting-honesty-and-fairness|Honesty and fairness]]: Distinguish considerate speech from concealing wrongdoing, and respond to unfair play without reducing the child to a label.
