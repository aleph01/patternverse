# Musical Composition Collection Map and Coverage Review

Working proposal · 29 October 2026

See the [30 October curriculum integration review](curriculum-integration.md) for the implementation added after this baseline: three form perspectives, canonical pattern reuse, an original layered-entry comparison, and a shared-player whole-piece timeline. The counts below describe the original 60-candidate review; explanatory coverage of a formal model does not imply a new dedicated pattern article.

[Detailed candidate review](candidate-review.md) · [Structured collection map](collection-map.json)

The existing songwriting material provides a substantial foundation for Musical Composition, especially in harmony, melody, lyrics, and creative process. The broadest gaps are in creating with timbre, spatial relationships, recorded sound, and independent melodic voices. Those areas need compositional techniques and original demonstrations of their own. Adding more electronic listening references alone would not fill them.

All **49 existing patterns** have one proposed canonical collection. The **60 core candidates** from the discussion divide into **21 with dedicated coverage**, **22 embedded in broader articles**, and **17 gaps**. These counts describe the working shortlist, not the completeness or quality of the musical domain. The shortlist is selective: existing foundation and specialist patterns remain in the map even when they are not among those 60 candidates.

The 15 collections, musical levels, and learning journey are distinct ways to navigate the same material. Each pattern keeps one canonical home. Cross-references and learning paths can connect it to other collections without copying the article. All mappings below are editorial proposals; existing articles, live collections, routes, player behavior, and draft publication states are unchanged.

## Coverage definitions

| Status    | Meaning                                                                                                                                 |
| --------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Dedicated | An existing pattern has the same central creative situation and reusable move. Domain framing or demonstrations may still need work.    |
| Embedded  | An explicit developed passage or concrete seed exists within a broader article; this is not a dedicated pattern.                        |
| Gap       | No developed treatment of this candidate exists; incidental mentions and related foundations are recorded without counting as coverage. |

“Dedicated” describes the central writing decision, not a finished composition-wide edition or a verified playable demonstration. “Embedded” can justify developing an existing passage, exposing a named variant, or extracting a distinct pattern. It does not automatically justify another article. A related foundation or incidental technique name is recorded without being counted as dedicated coverage.

## Musical levels

These labels describe the material a pattern acts upon. They are neither difficulty levels nor musical pitch-scale types. Several levels may apply when the actual mechanism transfers. A creative process can be useful without fitting any audible timescale.

| Level       | Material being shaped                                    | Useful visual context                                     |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Sound       | A source, onset, sustain, release, or changing character | A sound comparison or a labeled envelope                  |
| Figure      | A motif, arpeggio, rhythmic cell, or repeated unit       | A short roll, beat grid, or loop lane                     |
| Phrase      | A musical thought and its continuation or arrival        | A phrase view with timing, contour, or both lines visible |
| Section     | A larger span with a recognizable role or world          | A layer timeline or a section comparison                  |
| Whole piece | The overall journey of development and return            | A playable section map with a persistent position         |

The existing Repeat with a Reason article already names figure, phrase, section, and whole-song relationships. It is a good anchor for explaining levels. Timbral Transformation would connect an individual sound with the development of a longer passage or ambient piece. Arpeggiation should begin at figure and phrase level; there is no need to claim it is itself a whole-piece form.

Finish a Miniature and Change the Starting Point are process moves. The miniature has a musical form, but finishing it is not a sixth musical level. Likewise, a waveform’s rapid periodic repetition is not automatically the same perceptual pattern as a recurring chorus.

## Overlaps and editorial boundaries

- **Strategic Silence / Negative Space.** Keep the timed-silence move in Rhythm & Groove. Acoustic spaciousness belongs in Space & Atmosphere only when perceived depth or spatial relationships, rather than rests, are the mechanism.
- **Layered Entry / Gradual Accumulation.** Treat layered entry as one implementation of accumulation unless a separate problem and boundary justify a pattern. Foreground/background and interlocking layers retain their distinct simultaneous-role decisions.
- **Textural Subtraction / Release Through Subtraction.** Use one canonical Textural Subtraction entry in Texture & Layering; link its dramatic release uses to Musical Drama.
- **Reserve the Peak / Give the Melody a Destination / Shape Energy over Time.** Keep Reserve the Peak as a named facet initially. Single-line contour stays in Melody; whole-piece contrast planning stays in Musical Drama.
- **Motivic Sequence / Fragmentation / Developing Variation / Develop Without Losing the Idea.** Reuse the existing exact sequence and fragment examples. Separate a technique only if it needs a genuinely distinct article. Check the historical developing-variation term before equating it with any selective variation.
- **Voice leading / Counterpoint / Bass line.** Map smooth chord voice leading to Harmony, bass roles to Texture, and independent melodic-line construction to Counterpoint. Related material is preparation, not complete counterpoint coverage.
- **Arpeggiation / Chord construction.** Keep the introductory arpeggio/block example in the chord foundation. A moving accompaniment through a progression is a distinct compositional application with one canonical home in Harmony.
- **Sonic Palette / Instrument Transformation / Sampling and Recontextualization.** Selecting a sound world is existing palette coverage. Altering source identity and reusing recorded material need their own decisions; changing an instrument preset does not cover all three.
- **Rhythmic Ostinato / Strategic Repetition / Phase a Repeating Figure.** An ostinato is the recurring anchor, strategic repetition chooses the role of returns, and phasing changes relative alignment. Do not count a loop switch as all three techniques.
- **Coda / Outro / ABA / AABA.** An ending passage can supply a coda seed, but the label alone does not teach its function. AABA coverage does not satisfy the proposed ABA pattern.
- **Continuous Evolution / Through-composed form.** Define the shared collection by musical continuity and change. A continuous timbral process does not necessarily introduce new thematic sections in the same way as a through-composed song.
- **One canonical home / Several musical levels.** Store one canonical collection per pattern and several meaningful musical levels when appropriate. Learning paths and secondary cross-references may point to it without duplicating the article.

Existing bundled articles deserve particular care. Develop Without Losing the Idea contains exact sequence and fragmentation demonstrations. Change the Tonal World Deliberately groups modes, drones/pedals, and modulation. Shape the Approach and the Ending groups pacing, joins, and closure. Preserve their useful explanation and route identity while deciding which distinct moves warrant their own entries.

### A common listening experience

Begin with a visible question and one short listen-for cue. Offer a reference and a clearly labeled changed version, highlight the event or relationship that changed, and choose the simplest view for that pattern. Keep the starting position and context comparable between versions. Add one bounded adjustment only when manipulating that relationship teaches something more than hearing a fixed comparison.

The initial pattern demonstration should select the relevant examples rather than asking a beginner to find the right item in a course-wide clip menu. A rhythm page should foreground the beat reference; a counterpoint page should foreground the two lines; a sound page should foreground the audible sound change. The full conceptual article remains readable independently of audio, while practice stays a separate optional activity.

For cross-level exploration, use a shared example identity and source position. A figure can then be heard inside its phrase and section where the authored material supports it. A short clip cannot be zoomed into a whole composition that has not been written. A true multi-level view needs appropriate longer examples and explicit relationships between their excerpts.

## Learning journey across the collections

The six phases are a recommended route, not six more content containers. Several ways to start should appear early. Polyrhythm, chromatic harmony, and strict counterpoint can be optional depth rather than entrance requirements.

1. **Discover musical possibilities.** Music as Sound in Time; Eight Ways to Start a Composition. These guide titles are proposals, not newly authored patterns or a replacement for existing introductory articles.
2. **Create a first idea.** Sonic Palette; Rhythmic Ostinato; Melodic Contour; Foreground and Background; Establish a Tonal Home. Offer a sound-led route and the existing five-pattern note foundation as equal optional entrances; do not require a theory course first.
3. **Develop the idea.** Question and Answer; Motivic Sequence; Fragmentation; Timbral Transformation; Loop Transformation; Harmonic Rhythm Variation.
4. **Combine material.** Interlocking Layers; Countermelody; Register Separation; Depth Layering; Textural Subtraction.
5. **Shape movement and drama.** Gradual Accumulation; Dynamic Contrast; Delayed Resolution; Envelope Shaping; Break and Return.
6. **Complete the piece.** Ternary Form / ABA; Theme and Variations; Continuous Evolution; Transitional Motif; Coda; Finish a Miniature; Revise the Weakest Moment. Completing a miniature is also an early option; phase order is guidance rather than a prerequisite gate.

After the shared introduction, offer Songwriting, Sound-First & Electronic Composition, Experimental & Process Composition, and Advanced Harmony & Counterpoint paths. Each reuses canonical patterns across collections. Progress should describe what a person can hear or shape, without requiring completion of every article in every collection.
