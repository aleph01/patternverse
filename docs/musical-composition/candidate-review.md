# Musical Composition Core Candidate Review

Working proposal · 29 October 2026

The 60 candidates below retain the proposed 15 canonical collections and their provisional priority order. Each entry records a creative situation, move, musical level, coverage judgment, source evidence, and original demonstration brief. These are planning records rather than finished articles or new media.

Read [the collection map and main findings](coverage-review.md) for the 49-source migration map, overlap decisions, playback coverage, learning journey, and development priorities. [The structured map](collection-map.json) records the same assignments and current source identities.

**Dedicated** means an existing pattern has the same central creative decision. **Embedded** means a developed passage or concrete seed exists inside a broader article. **Gap** means no developed treatment exists; an incidental mention is not counted as coverage. A dedicated article can still need new framing or an audible comparison.

Musical levels are Sound, Figure, Phrase, Section, and Whole piece. Creative process describes a method rather than an audible timescale. Source links identify current draft articles; their displayed order is preserved. No source has been moved, renamed, published, or rewritten for this review.

## Candidate situations and coverage

Each candidate has one proposed home, a recognizable creative situation, a reusable move, a musical scope, and an audio comparison brief. Exact manuscript headings are included with the source links. These comparisons are design briefs for later example work; they are not newly authored or auditioned media.

### 1 Melody & Phrasing

**Useful when:** A line lacks direction, punctuation, or a recognizable focal feature.

Shape the identity and articulation of a single musical line.

**1. Melodic Contour** — Dedicated · Figure, Phrase

**Situation and move:** A tune has motion but no clear destination. Reserve a rise, fall, or register peak for a consequential moment.

**Coverage:** The contour/register pattern gives original phrase and vocal-range comparisons; broadening beyond singers is an adaptation.

**Existing evidence:** [Give the Melody a Destination](../../content/patterns/songwriting-contour-and-register.md) — Give the high point a job

**Demonstration:** Keep rhythm fixed and compare a level contour with an arch aimed at the final note.

**2. Phrase Breathing** — Dedicated · Phrase

**Situation and move:** A phrase crowds its next entrance or is uncomfortable to perform. Treat sounding time and silence as parts of the same counted phrase.

**Coverage:** The breathing pattern already compares filled versus open endings and different phrase lengths.

**Existing evidence:** [Let the Phrase Breathe](../../content/patterns/songwriting-phrases-and-breathing.md) — Compare an open phrase and a reply

**Demonstration:** Keep the next entrance fixed and compare a filled last beat with a rest.

**3. Question and Answer** — Embedded · Phrase

**Situation and move:** Successive phrases feel disconnected or equally final. Give one phrase an open relationship and make the reply answer it.

**Coverage:** An original open-phrase/reply comparison exists inside breathing, with further motif replies; there is no dedicated pattern.

**Existing evidence:** [Let the Phrase Breathe](../../content/patterns/songwriting-phrases-and-breathing.md) — Compare an open phrase and a reply; [Start with a Motif](../../content/patterns/songwriting-start-with-a-motif.md) — Build a four-bar phrase

**Demonstration:** Compare two equally settled phrases with an open first phrase and a replying second phrase.

**4. Melodic Hook** — Dedicated · Figure, Phrase

**Situation and move:** The listener has no compact musical feature to recognize on return. Give a short identity room to register and a reason to return.

**Coverage:** Write a Hook explicitly compares melodic, rhythmic, and lyrical hooks; retain that breadth and use a melodic facet.

**Existing evidence:** [Write a Hook](../../content/patterns/songwriting-write-a-hook.md) — Compare three kinds of hook

**Demonstration:** Play the hook with competing accompaniment, then with space around its entrance.

### 2 Harmony & Tonality

**Useful when:** Pitches and chords need a clear relationship, color, or destination.

Organize pitch collections, centers, simultaneous notes, and harmonic movement.

**1. Establish a Tonal Home** — Dedicated · Figure, Phrase, Section

**Situation and move:** A familiar note collection has no convincing point of rest. Use a bass reference, repeated emphasis, and endings to orient the music.

**Coverage:** The focused tonal-home pattern and C/A original comparisons are already present.

**Existing evidence:** [Establish a Tonal Home](../../content/patterns/songwriting-scales-and-tonal-home.md) — Compare two homes with the same opening

**Demonstration:** Compare the fixed opening with C-centered versus A-centered bass references and endings.

**2. Functional Harmonic Motion** — Dedicated · Phrase, Section

**Situation and move:** A progression circles without arriving or closes a thought too firmly. Coordinate chord order, change rate, and phrase ending to choose a destination.

**Coverage:** Decide Where the Harmony Arrives covers this decision and also acknowledges nonfunctional loops; retain the practical title.

**Existing evidence:** [Decide Where the Harmony Arrives](../../content/patterns/songwriting-progressions-and-arrival.md) — Give movement a context

**Demonstration:** Keep the melody fixed while comparing settled, open, and continuing harmonic endings.

**3. Harmonic Rhythm Variation** — Embedded · Phrase, Section

**Situation and move:** A useful progression feels too slow, hurried, or mechanically regular. Change when chords change while preserving their order.

**Coverage:** Change how long a chord stays develops this inside the arrival pattern; the editor already permits varied durations.

**Existing evidence:** [Decide Where the Harmony Arrives](../../content/patterns/songwriting-progressions-and-arrival.md) — Change how long a chord stays

**Demonstration:** Use the same chord order with even durations and with an accelerated approach to arrival.

**4. Arpeggiation** — Embedded · Figure, Phrase

**Situation and move:** Block chords feel static or occupy too much space. Distribute a chord's notes through time to create a moving accompaniment.

**Coverage:** The arpeggio/block distinction and C-major/minor listening comparison exist; a reusable accompaniment figure through changing chords does not.

**Existing evidence:** [Choose Chords by Their Notes](../../content/patterns/songwriting-build-and-hear-chords.md) — Hear an arpeggio and a block chord as related

**Demonstration:** Keep harmony fixed and compare block chords with an up-and-down broken-chord figure.

**5. Pedal Point** — Embedded · Phrase, Section

**Situation and move:** Moving upper material needs a persistent pitch reference. Keep a pitch sounding or returning while the harmony or upper lines change.

**Coverage:** The tonal-world pattern explains drones and pedals; the bass pattern provides a stable reference. Neither is a dedicated pedal pattern.

**Existing evidence:** [Change the Tonal World Deliberately](../../content/patterns/songwriting-modes-drones-and-key-changes.md) — Hold a reference beneath movement; [Write a Bass Line](../../content/patterns/songwriting-write-a-bass-line.md) — Let a stable bass support change elsewhere

**Demonstration:** Hold a low reference while changing upper harmony, then compare a bass that follows every root.

**6. Borrow a Chord** — Dedicated · Phrase, Section

**Situation and move:** A particular harmonic moment needs a color beyond its starting palette. Choose a chord from a parallel palette and check the altered notes against the melody.

**Coverage:** The existing pattern has F–Fm–C, exact moving notes, and melody checks. The editor cannot currently insert Fm into a C-major sketch.

**Existing evidence:** [Borrow a Chord](../../content/patterns/songwriting-borrow-a-chord.md) — Follow the changed note

**Demonstration:** Compare matched F–C and F–Fm–C returns while highlighting A–A-flat–G.

### 3 Rhythm & Groove

**Useful when:** A recurring pulse or the events around it need more purposeful movement.

Shape pulse, grouping, subdivision, timing, and rhythmic reference.

**1. Rhythmic Ostinato** — Embedded · Figure, Phrase, Section

**Situation and move:** A passage needs an anchor against which other material can develop. Repeat a distinctive rhythmic unit while giving surrounding events different jobs.

**Coverage:** The motif, repetition, and groove articles develop recurring anchors; ostinato is not given a separate decision and boundary.

**Existing evidence:** [Interlock the Groove](../../content/patterns/songwriting-interlock-the-groove.md) — Identify the anchor; [Start with a Motif](../../content/patterns/songwriting-start-with-a-motif.md) — Hear the seed as a combination of features; [Repeat with a Reason](../../content/patterns/songwriting-repetition-variation-surprise.md) — Establish something the listener can recognize

**Demonstration:** Loop a fixed cell while adding a contrasting part, then vary the anchor too and compare the loss of reference.

**2. Strategic Silence** — Dedicated · Figure, Phrase

**Situation and move:** Continuous activity obscures a phrase or weakens the next entrance. Place and count rests as deliberately as sounding events.

**Coverage:** Make Space Part of the Rhythm provides the practical home; avoid a duplicate Negative Space article for the same move.

**Existing evidence:** [Make Space Part of the Rhythm](../../content/patterns/songwriting-subdivision-and-rests.md) — The move

**Demonstration:** Keep a bar's duration fixed while removing selected attacks and preserving counted rests.

**3. Syncopation** — Dedicated · Figure, Phrase

**Situation and move:** A phrase enters squarely or lacks a deliberate push against the beat. Move an accent or sustained entrance against an audible metric reference.

**Coverage:** The existing entrance pattern develops anticipation and contextual syncopation. Its broader offbeat-accent variants need a focused demonstration.

**Existing evidence:** [Pull an Entrance Across the Beat](../../content/patterns/songwriting-syncopation-and-anticipation.md) — Establish the expectation first

**Demonstration:** Keep the pulse fixed and move one entrance from the downbeat to the preceding offbeat.

**4. Swing** — Dedicated · Figure, Phrase

**Situation and move:** Even subdivisions do not provide the intended recurring feel. Choose a repeatable long–short subdivision relationship and distinguish it from shifting a whole part.

**Coverage:** The existing timing pattern distinguishes swing, part placement, and drift; it has no chapter-bound original swing comparison.

**Existing evidence:** [Give the Groove a Consistent Feel](../../content/patterns/songwriting-swing-and-timing.md) — The move

**Demonstration:** Compare straight paired subdivisions, uneven pairs, and an equally spaced part shifted late.

**5. Polyrhythms** — Gap · Figure, Phrase, Section

**Situation and move:** Two parts could create movement by dividing a shared span differently. Layer contrasting regular divisions, beginning with three attacks against two over one common duration.

**Coverage:** No dedicated polyrhythm treatment or original comparison exists. Interlocking attacks alone do not establish polyrhythm.

**Existing evidence:** No developed treatment in the current 49-pattern course.

**Demonstration:** Play the two and three layers separately, together, and against a shared cycle marker.

### 4 Timbre & Sound Design

Choose and transform the identity of sounds, including sources beyond pitched instruments.

**1. Sonic Palette** — Dedicated · Sound, Section, Whole piece

**Situation and move:** Available sounds pull a piece toward incompatible identities. Choose a coherent set of sounds around a focal relationship.

**Coverage:** Choose the World Around the Song already selects acoustic, band, and electronic settings. Extend the entry to sound-led composition.

**Existing evidence:** [Choose the World Around the Song](../../content/patterns/songwriting-choose-a-sonic-setting.md) — Decide what remains in front

**Demonstration:** Keep notes and timing fixed across three restrained sound palettes.

**2. Timbral Transformation** — Gap · Sound, Figure, Phrase, Section, Whole piece

**Situation and move:** A sound should develop while its pitch material remains recognizable. Change a sound's spectral or textural character over time as a compositional event.

**Coverage:** Instrumentation comparisons are present, but no pattern teaches a controlled evolving timbre or follows its trajectory.

**Existing evidence:** [Choose the World Around the Song](../../content/patterns/songwriting-choose-a-sonic-setting.md) — Decide what remains in front

**Demonstration:** Hold one pitch and rhythm while a sound changes from a dull sustained tone to a brighter evolving tone.

**3. Instrument Transformation** — Gap · Sound, Figure, Phrase

**Situation and move:** A familiar source needs to suggest new material or a less familiar identity. Transform a source's sound and compose from what the transformation reveals.

**Coverage:** Replacing a guitar with a synthesizer is orchestration; there is no developed transformed-source method.

**Existing evidence:** [Choose the World Around the Song](../../content/patterns/songwriting-choose-a-sonic-setting.md) — Decide what remains in front

**Demonstration:** Compare an original plucked source with a transformed version while preserving an audible identifying feature.

**4. Blend Noise and Pitch** — Gap · Sound, Figure, Section

**Situation and move:** A pitched sound needs a contrasting edge, body, or evolving texture. Combine pitched and noise material with deliberate balances and envelopes.

**Coverage:** No noise-source composition pattern or playable noise comparison is present.

**Existing evidence:** No developed treatment in the current 49-pattern course.

**Demonstration:** Keep pitch and duration fixed and add a controlled noise component.

### 5 Texture & Layering

**Useful when:** Simultaneous parts compete, obscure one another, or need complementary roles.

Organize which layers coexist and what each contributes.

**1. Foreground and Background** — Dedicated · Phrase, Section

**Situation and move:** Several parts compete for the same attention. Name each layer's contribution and make the intended foreground perceptible.

**Coverage:** Give Each Part a Job has a role map, supporting/answering parts, and removal decisions; preserve that canonical entry.

**Existing evidence:** [Give Each Part a Job](../../content/patterns/songwriting-give-each-part-a-job.md) — Name the contribution

**Demonstration:** Keep the lead fixed while reducing or relocating a competing background part.

**2. Register Separation** — Embedded · Figure, Phrase, Section

**Situation and move:** Different parts remain hard to distinguish despite different rhythms. Separate their useful pitch regions without changing their intended roles.

**Coverage:** The part-role article directly compares register and duration; the melodic-register article has a different single-line purpose.

**Existing evidence:** [Give Each Part a Job](../../content/patterns/songwriting-give-each-part-a-job.md) — Separate rhythm, register, and duration; [Connect Chords with Small Moves](../../content/patterns/songwriting-inversions-and-voice-leading.md) — Follow three voices; [Give the Melody a Destination](../../content/patterns/songwriting-contour-and-register.md) — Give the high point a job

**Demonstration:** Keep the notes' relationships and timing while moving one accompaniment layer into another register.

**3. Interlocking Layers** — Dedicated · Figure, Phrase, Section

**Situation and move:** Added layers obscure the combined pattern or occupy every gap. Coordinate complementary attacks, sustains, and spaces around a shared reference.

**Coverage:** Interlock the Groove already teaches the decision. Its mounted bass clip alone does not demonstrate the interaction between layers.

**Existing evidence:** [Interlock the Groove](../../content/patterns/songwriting-interlock-the-groove.md) — Compare support, response, and competition

**Demonstration:** Play each layer alone, then compare doubled attacks with complementary entrances.

**4. Textural Subtraction** — Embedded · Phrase, Section, Whole piece

**Situation and move:** Density hides an important line or a return has no remaining contrast. Remove a layer at a purposeful moment and hear what the remaining texture reveals.

**Coverage:** Arrange by removal as well as addition and the energy pattern develop this within broader articles.

**Existing evidence:** [Give Each Part a Job](../../content/patterns/songwriting-give-each-part-a-job.md) — Arrange by removal as well as addition; [Shape Energy over Time](../../content/patterns/songwriting-shape-energy-over-time.md) — Separate dimensions of energy

**Demonstration:** Keep the passage unchanged and remove one sustained layer at the focal entrance.

### 6 Space & Atmosphere

**Useful when:** The apparent distance, placement, or acoustic continuity of sounds should shape the music.

Compose perceived depth, spatial position, motion, and reverberant relationships.

**1. Depth Layering** — Embedded · Sound, Phrase, Section

**Situation and move:** Everything appears equally close or the focal sound loses presence. Place foreground and background at different perceived distances.

**Coverage:** The sonic-setting article describes dry/close and reverberant voices, but lacks a developed depth-layering example.

**Existing evidence:** [Choose the World Around the Song](../../content/patterns/songwriting-choose-a-sonic-setting.md) — Decide what remains in front

**Demonstration:** Keep a foreground sound close while placing a background layer farther away; match loudness carefully.

**2. Spatial Contrast** — Embedded · Phrase, Section

**Situation and move:** A section change needs a different sense of acoustic space. Change the apparent spatial setting at a meaningful boundary.

**Coverage:** Dry/reverberant settings supply a seed comparison, not a dedicated spatial-composition pattern.

**Existing evidence:** [Choose the World Around the Song](../../content/patterns/songwriting-choose-a-sonic-setting.md) — Decide what remains in front

**Demonstration:** Use the same phrase in a close, narrow setting and a more diffuse setting while keeping the musical material fixed.

**3. Spatial Movement** — Gap · Sound, Figure, Phrase, Section

**Situation and move:** A sustained or recurring source could develop through position. Move an audible source through space with timing that supports the phrase.

**Coverage:** No spatial-motion pattern or stereo position control exists in the music tools.

**Existing evidence:** No developed treatment in the current 49-pattern course.

**Demonstration:** Keep source and rhythm fixed while comparing a stationary sound with a timed left-to-right movement.

**4. Connect Sounds with Reverberation** — Gap · Sound, Phrase, Section

**Situation and move:** Separate events need continuity through an acoustic tail. Let a decay bridge events while preserving the intended clarity.

**Coverage:** Held notes and transitions are covered, but a reverberant bridge is not developed or playable.

**Existing evidence:** No developed treatment in the current 49-pattern course.

**Demonstration:** Compare separated dry events with the same events joined by a controlled reverberant tail.

### 7 Dynamics & Gesture

**Useful when:** A sound or phrase needs a more purposeful attack, continuation, release, or emphasis.

Shape the physical and temporal behavior of sounds and gestures.

**1. Envelope Shaping** — Gap · Sound, Figure, Phrase

**Situation and move:** The onset, sustain, or release gives a sound the wrong compositional role. Shape how a sound begins, continues, and ends while keeping pitch material fixed.

**Coverage:** Vocal attack and release are mentioned; synthesized envelopes are fixed internally. There is no educational envelope pattern or control.

**Existing evidence:** [Let the Voice Carry the Meaning](../../content/patterns/songwriting-vocal-delivery-and-harmonies.md) — Locate the center of a sung event

**Demonstration:** Play a fixed pitch with a short attack/release and a slow swelling envelope at matched reference level.

**2. Dynamic Contrast** — Embedded · Figure, Phrase, Section, Whole piece

**Situation and move:** A phrase or section needs an audible difference in intensity. Choose a controlled loudness relationship and hear it in its musical context.

**Coverage:** Energy is explicitly separated from loudness; dynamic contrast is one bundled dimension rather than a dedicated pattern.

**Existing evidence:** [Shape Energy over Time](../../content/patterns/songwriting-shape-energy-over-time.md) — Reserve contrast; [Make the Chorus Arrive](../../content/patterns/songwriting-make-the-chorus-arrive.md) — Working choice

**Demonstration:** Keep notes, timbre, and activity fixed while comparing an even level with a purposeful dynamic change.

**3. Articulation Contrast** — Embedded · Sound, Figure, Phrase

**Situation and move:** Identical pitches and rhythms need different kinds of gesture. Change connection, separation, or attack while preserving the written idea.

**Coverage:** The vocal-delivery pattern supplies early/late releases and soft attacks; instrumental and electronic articulation need broader treatment.

**Existing evidence:** [Let the Voice Carry the Meaning](../../content/patterns/songwriting-vocal-delivery-and-harmonies.md) — Locate the center of a sung event

**Demonstration:** Compare a connected phrase with a detached phrase using the same notes and onset rhythm.

### 8 Motifs & Thematic Development

**Useful when:** An idea needs recognizable continuation, variation, or transformation.

Develop relationships within and between recognizable units of material.

**1. Strategic Repetition** — Dedicated · Figure, Phrase, Section, Whole piece

**Situation and move:** A recognizable idea either disappears too early or returns without purpose. Preserve a useful reference and vary a consequential relationship when the music needs it.

**Coverage:** Repeat with a Reason already discusses repetition at several musical levels, including texture around a stable figure.

**Existing evidence:** [Repeat with a Reason](../../content/patterns/songwriting-repetition-variation-surprise.md) — Use the pattern at the right scale

**Demonstration:** Keep a motif fixed and change its surroundings, then compare changing the motif itself.

**2. Motivic Sequence** — Embedded · Figure, Phrase

**Situation and move:** A motif needs continuation at another pitch level. Move the motif by a defined interval or by scale steps and preserve the intended identity.

**Coverage:** Make a sequence precise spells exact and diatonic versions; there is no dedicated sequence entry or mounted transformation comparison.

**Existing evidence:** [Develop Without Losing the Idea](../../content/patterns/songwriting-develop-without-losing-the-idea.md) — Make a sequence precise

**Demonstration:** Compare the seed, an exact transposition, and a diatonic sequence under stated harmony.

**3. Fragmentation** — Embedded · Figure, Phrase, Section

**Situation and move:** A complete motif is too long or familiar to serve the next passage. Develop a smaller recognizable fragment and decide whether its missing completion returns.

**Coverage:** Fragment and extend contains an exact two-note fragment and rhythmic alternatives.

**Existing evidence:** [Develop Without Losing the Idea](../../content/patterns/songwriting-develop-without-losing-the-idea.md) — Fragment and extend

**Demonstration:** Compare the full seed with its first two notes repeating before the complete idea returns.

**4. Developing Variation** — Embedded · Figure, Phrase, Section, Whole piece

**Situation and move:** Successive ideas need to grow from earlier material while remaining connected. Derive further material by selective transformations rather than merely replaying a reference.

**Coverage:** The existing article teaches selective variation. The historically specific term developing variation needs a provenance review before it becomes an exact synonym.

**Existing evidence:** [Develop Without Losing the Idea](../../content/patterns/songwriting-develop-without-losing-the-idea.md) — Keep variation subordinate to purpose

**Demonstration:** Follow a short chain of related transformations and identify the feature linking each stage.

**Editorial decision:** Check the historical definition before deciding whether the existing broad variation pattern is sufficient or a distinct technique is needed.

### 9 Counterpoint & Voice Interaction

**Useful when:** A second line needs its own musical identity while belonging with the first.

Combine independently meaningful melodic lines; distinguish this from chord voicing.

**1. Countermelody** — Gap · Phrase, Section

**Situation and move:** A second line needs identity without obscuring the lead. Write a complementary line that is meaningful alone and coherent against the first.

**Coverage:** A countermelody is mentioned and bass/response roles are developed, but no two independent lines are constructed and checked together.

**Existing evidence:** [Write a Hook](../../content/patterns/songwriting-write-a-hook.md) — Compare three kinds of hook; [Give Each Part a Job](../../content/patterns/songwriting-give-each-part-a-job.md) — Separate rhythm, register, and duration; [Write a Bass Line](../../content/patterns/songwriting-write-a-bass-line.md) — Let a stable bass support change elsewhere

**Demonstration:** Solo two lines, then compare a doubling with a rhythmically and melodically independent second line.

**2. Contrary Motion** — Gap · Figure, Phrase

**Situation and move:** Two lines move together so consistently that their independence is unclear. Let one line rise while another falls where the combined notes suit the passage.

**Coverage:** Smooth chord voice leading and held common tones are present; they do not supply a dedicated contrary-motion or counterpoint lesson.

**Existing evidence:** [Connect Chords with Small Moves](../../content/patterns/songwriting-inversions-and-voice-leading.md) — Follow three voices

**Demonstration:** Compare parallel-direction and opposite-direction versions of two short lines with their intervals visible.

**3. Imitation** — Gap · Figure, Phrase, Section

**Situation and move:** Related voices need a clear connection without identical simultaneous doubling. Let a recognizable idea enter in another voice after a deliberate delay.

**Coverage:** Motivic identity and borrowed timing are covered, but staggered imitation between voices is not developed.

**Existing evidence:** [Start with a Motif](../../content/patterns/songwriting-start-with-a-motif.md) — Find motifs outside pitched melody

**Demonstration:** Play a figure, introduce it later in another line, and vary the entry delay.

### 10 Tension, Release & Musical Drama

**Useful when:** A passage needs expectation, a consequential arrival, or a convincing trajectory.

Plan the experience of preparation, intensification, withholding, and release.

**1. Gradual Accumulation** — Embedded · Phrase, Section, Whole piece

**Situation and move:** A persistent idea needs development through an increasing number of contributions. Introduce layers or events in a deliberate order while retaining an audible reference.

**Coverage:** The energy pattern develops this with Platform Three and Says, but no standalone pattern or authored accumulating playback exists.

**Existing evidence:** [Shape Energy over Time](../../content/patterns/songwriting-shape-energy-over-time.md) — Says: intensity grows around a persistent figure

**Demonstration:** Keep the reference figure fixed while successive layers enter on a labeled timeline.

**2. Delayed Resolution** — Embedded · Figure, Phrase, Section

**Situation and move:** An arrival settles before its anticipation has become meaningful. Sustain an unresolved relationship and choose when its resolution earns its place.

**Coverage:** Harmonic endings and melody/harmony tension are treated, but delayed resolution remains a local technique within them.

**Existing evidence:** [Decide Where the Harmony Arrives](../../content/patterns/songwriting-progressions-and-arrival.md) — Change how long a chord stays; [Support or Challenge the Melody Note](../../content/patterns/songwriting-melody-and-harmony.md) — The move

**Demonstration:** Compare immediate and delayed resolutions with the preparation and final target kept comparable.

**3. Reserve the Peak** — Embedded · Phrase, Section, Whole piece

**Situation and move:** Early maximum intensity leaves later moments with little contrast. Save a consequential register, density, or exposure change for its intended destination.

**Coverage:** The contour and energy patterns already teach reserving contrast. Treat this as a facet of those patterns initially, rather than adding a synonymous article.

**Existing evidence:** [Give the Melody a Destination](../../content/patterns/songwriting-contour-and-register.md) — Give the high point a job; [Shape Energy over Time](../../content/patterns/songwriting-shape-energy-over-time.md) — Reserve contrast

**Demonstration:** Keep the final event fixed and compare versions that reveal or reserve its defining feature earlier.

**Editorial decision:** Keep as a named facet of contour and energy planning initially; do not commission a duplicate article.

### 11 Musical Form & Structure

**3. Theme and Variations** — Gap · Section, Whole piece

**4. Continuous Evolution** — Embedded · Section, Whole piece

**Situation and move:** A piece needs a larger arc without recurring song sections. Develop material through a planned sequence of continuous changes.

**Coverage:** Continuously developing form and stable-center growth are discussed, but no complete worked sound-led evolution is authored.

**Existing evidence:** [Choose a Form That Serves the Song](../../content/patterns/songwriting-choose-a-song-form.md) — Consider forms without a recurring chorus; [Change the Tonal World Deliberately](../../content/patterns/songwriting-modes-drones-and-key-changes.md) — Choose a relationship that fits the song; [Shape Energy over Time](../../content/patterns/songwriting-shape-energy-over-time.md) — Separate dimensions of energy

### 12 Transitions & Endings

**Useful when:** A boundary feels pasted together or an ending does not complete the intended experience.

Shape entrances, joins, departures, and final gestures.

**1. Transitional Motif** — Embedded · Figure, Phrase, Section

**Situation and move:** A join needs continuity between otherwise different passages. Carry a recognizable figure through the boundary while other features change.

**Coverage:** A continuing instrumental figure at a boundary is described, but not developed as a separate motif-transition pattern.

**Existing evidence:** [Shape the Approach and the Ending](../../content/patterns/songwriting-pacing-transitions-endings.md) — Connect sections through a specific event; [Start with a Motif](../../content/patterns/songwriting-start-with-a-motif.md) — Find motifs outside pitched melody

**Demonstration:** Keep two sections fixed and compare an abrupt join with a figure carried across the boundary.

**2. Break and Return** — Embedded · Phrase, Section, Whole piece

**Situation and move:** A return lacks impact or the texture needs a deliberate interruption. Prepare a break and coordinate the next entrance against the established reference.
