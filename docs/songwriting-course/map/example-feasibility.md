# Songwriting example selection and feasibility review

Initial source snapshot · 29 October 2026

Follow-up: the first four acquisition targets have now been added to the local demo. See the [second batch review](../../musical-composition/repertoire-batch-2.md); that batch brought the library to 19 excerpts. The third-batch update above brings it to 22. The 15-file assessment below is the earlier review snapshot.

The strongest first library combines original concept demonstrations, six open-edition repertoire sources, and a small group of familiar songs pursued for public-app permission. The existing 15 local repertoire files all parse into nonempty excerpts with positive note durations. That establishes technical availability, not complete transcription accuracy or permission to publish.

Every one of the 49 lessons now has an original-first demonstration and a selected context route below. A named song is optional for the first explanation of scales, intervals or a creative process. Lyrics, vocal delivery and production also need words or actual audio; an instrumental MIDI file cannot replace the relationship those lessons teach.

## What can be used

**Six sources have explicit open-edition terms.** Four Mutopia editions label the contribution Public Domain; two specify CC BY-SA 3.0. They are candidates for hosted playback under those terms, with the final passage and annotations still to check. None is marked publication-ready by this review. [Mutopia licensing](https://www.mutopiaproject.org/legal.html).

**Nine familiar-song files already work locally, but public-app reuse is unconfirmed.** MIDI DB grants permission to download demos. That is not an explicit app-redistribution grant. Hit Trax's published terms restrict copying and redistribution without an applicable license, so buying a full backing track would not by itself resolve this use. The community arrangements likewise have no confirmed public-app grant in the inspected source records. [MIDI DB example](https://www.mididb.com/stevie-wonder/superstition-midi/), [Hit Trax terms](https://www.midi.com.au/terms-and-conditions/).

**Original teaching music is the dependable first demonstration.** Focused interval, scale, tonal-home, triad, syncopation, swing, sequence and borrowed-third comparisons already exist. Most other controlled lesson comparisons are still written examples or briefs that need focused playback assets. The complete sixteen-bar Porch Light miniature also needs a dedicated playback arrangement; fragments and prose are not that finished asset.

## Videotape remains a separate priority

Keep **Radiohead at Bonnaroo, 17 June 2006** as a desired syncopation example. The [Bonnaroo-version drum-chart page](https://supersimpledrum.com/songs/videotape-bonnaroo-version-radiohead) advertises MIDI and MusicXML, but its full page could not be refreshed in this pass and its notes have not been inspected. It is a lead rather than a verified combined example.

What is still needed is a specific piano passage aligned against the actual live beat, a checked piano-and-reference arrangement and a source basis for its intended public use. An album piano MIDI, a Bonnaroo drum chart and a publisher's piano arrangement are different resources. Neither one alone verifies the combined live rhythm. Use Joplin and the original shifted-beat comparison while this remains open.

## Asset work before expanding the library

First make the existing open-edition excerpts exact teaching cases: annotate the beat in Joplin, the motive and answering entry in Bach's Invention, and the arpeggiation in the Prelude. Select later Mozart and Bach passages only for the specific harmonic outcomes they demonstrate. Retain the source edition and its terms with every extracted or adapted asset.

For familiar-song hosting, prioritize Seven Nation Army, No Surprises, Lean on Me and Superstition. They offer distinct, understandable mechanisms and already have usable local reductions. Ask for the intended app playback, excerpting, notation display, isolation and teaching transformations explicitly when seeking permission; an ordinary backing-track purchase is not substituted for that scope.

For lyrics, sound and vocal delivery, keep original interactive examples and official listening references as the immediate route. Source or create suitable audio rather than treating every missing recording as a missing MIDI file. Full-work form examples need a section map, not just another short loop.

The remaining candidates in the earlier shortlist are editorial alternatives, not additional ready files. Their selection should follow a particular passage and listening question. This keeps the library small enough to verify while preserving familiar music where it adds explanatory value.
