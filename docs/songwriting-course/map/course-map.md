# Songwriting course learning map

Review proposal · 29 October 2026

Integration update · 30 October 2026: [Curriculum integration review](../../musical-composition/curriculum-integration.md) records the six topic entry points, editorial entity roles, three form perspectives, and the shared-player whole-piece/layer comparison now implemented. The 49-lesson inventory and existing routes remain the canonical sequence. Formal models are explained within the existing form article; their names do not require separate pattern articles.

The course needs an explicit learning route alongside its pattern collections. A novice should understand the musical relationship before being asked to hear it in a song. The 49 existing lessons contain much of the needed theory, but several lessons combine foundational vocabulary with a composition decision, and the first miniature arrives before the concepts its written example uses.

The proposed route keeps the existing material and gives each lesson a teaching responsibility. Six lessons primarily explain foundations, 36 primarily teach composition choices, and seven teach creative processes. These are editorial roles, not changes to article routes or the application's entity schema. Some composition lessons also introduce a small foundation before applying it.

## Three different maps

**Collections organize subject matter.** The earlier composition map's 15 collections remain useful homes for harmony, rhythm, counterpoint, sound, form and creative process. A pattern keeps one canonical home and can appear in other learning paths through links.

**The course orders understanding.** The route below describes what a beginner needs before each demonstration. It does not require every reader to complete every earlier lesson or all of music theory. Harmony depth, lyrics, vocal delivery and independent-line techniques can branch according to the music someone wants to make.

**Musical scale describes what changes.** A pattern can act on a sound, figure, phrase, section or whole piece. Those levels are not difficulty levels or pitch scales. Creative processes such as capturing or revising can operate across them.

A beginner's core route can move from Melody and development directly to Layers and sound, then whole form and finishing. Harmonic movement and colour is optional depth. Words and musical meaning is a parallel vocal branch after basic phrasing and rhythm; it does not require advanced harmony. The later verse, bridge and vocal-harmony lessons are conditional on the piece being written.

The first miniature moves from chapter 6 to the end of Notes keys and chords in this proposal. Its current example uses sixteen bars, quarter-note durations, chord symbols and chord tones. An optional opening listen can still provide a destination, with ordinary labels and no demand to interpret its notation.

## What counts as enough theory

Enough means the learner can notice or explain the relationship the example asks them to hear. It does not mean mastering every possible interval name, all key signatures, formal counterpoint rules or every form of minor before composing.

For syncopation, the learner needs a stable pulse, grouping, subdivision and an expected position. For borrowing, they need a tonic, a major or minor palette, chord quality and the distinction between parallel and relative keys. For a countermelody, they need a phrase, separate audible lines and the simultaneous intervals those lines create. Each route gets its own small foundation.

The circle of fifths deserves an explicit map-reading foundation after scales and key relationships. It is not a prerequisite for hearing every triad or writing a first melody. The current article can keep its stable identity while presenting key signatures and neighboring keys first, then transposition as the application.

## Before any song example

1. **Explain the general idea.** Use ordinary language and name the musical relationship.
2. **Show its structure.** Choose a visual that explains that relationship. Define the labels the learner will see.
3. **Hear a minimal original comparison.** Change one important variable and keep the listening reference clear.
4. **Hear it in context.** Introduce a selected song or work only after the question is understandable.
5. **Show transfer and limits.** Relate the pattern to a new original situation, including when another choice may work better.

The initial view follows the idea: a beat timeline for displaced entrances, a step diagram for intervals or scales, linked chord tones for borrowing or voice leading, separate lanes for counterpoint, a section map for form, and an audio or layer comparison for sound. A piano roll or score is available when it clarifies the example. Part and instrument presets belong to the lesson, so the learner does not need to configure the instrument before understanding the idea.

Staff reading belongs in an optional reference bridge that links a heard event to its piano-roll and score representation. Explain clefs, rests, ties and signatures when those symbols appear. Fluent notation reading is not a condition for hearing the concept or writing music.

## Coverage decisions

The detailed lesson inventory and foundation outcomes follow below. The findings distinguish existing coverage from missing material.

**Apply the concept before example sequence across the course.** The three guided pilots have concept openings. The shared article route still inserts the general player before article prose for other musical lessons. Use the required foundation and first visual fields as a pre-example check for every lesson. Apply the new sequence before expanding the repertoire.

**Move the first miniature.** The chapter uses bars, quarter-note durations, chord symbols and chord-tone relationships before chapters 7–19 introduce them. Place it after the basic notes-and-chords block; retain an optional unlabelled listening preview at the start.

**Teach relative and parallel keys together.** Relative keys appear in tonal home, while the explicit parallel distinction is developed later inside Borrow a Chord. Put a C-major/A-minor/C-minor comparison before borrowing; do not claim minor scales themselves are missing.

**Separate the key map from transposition.** The circle chapter teaches signatures, relatives, fifth-root movement and whole-song transposition together. Give the map its own foundation section, then use transposition as the composition or arrangement decision.

**Develop arpeggiation as a pattern.** A block-versus-broken chord comparison exists, but designing an arpeggiated accompaniment has no dedicated treatment. Develop an optional pattern after triads plus duration and subdivision; hold harmony fixed while changing order, register and rhythm.

**Add an independent-lines branch.** Voice leading and arrangement roles offer seeds, but countermelody, contrary motion and imitation have no developed dedicated treatment. Introduce independence and simultaneous intervals, then countermelody; add contrary motion and delayed imitation as separate patterns.

**Add polyrhythm after shared time is secure.** Triplets and compound subdivision are explained, but simultaneous contrasting divisions are not a developed lesson. Introduce shared span and 3-against-2; contrast syncopation, triplet rhythm, polyrhythm and polymeter.

**Split choices inside tonal-world changes.** Modes, drones, tonicization, transposition and modulation describe several different operations. Use a short comparison foundation, then separate keep-the-center/change-the-palette from establish-a-new-home; treat drone as a technique.

**Strengthen the sound and texture route.** A sonic-setting article and role-based arrangement exist, but the broader collection review finds timbral transformation, space and sampling thin or missing. Introduce sound-versus-note and role vocabulary early. Keep sound-led composition as an expansion branch beyond this songwriting core.

**Explain the score when notation is selected.** The course offers notation views and a reading guide, but no staged pitch-and-rhythm bridge into staff reading. Add an optional linked reading guide for clefs, rests, ties and key signatures. Staff fluency is not a gate for composing or hearing the concept.

**Make the cell phrase section distinction explicit.** Motif and phrase vocabulary is spread across miniature, motif and breathing chapters. Introduce a short hierarchy before the miniature, then reuse the same terms in development and whole-form lessons.

## Review and implementation order

First agree the foundation responsibilities and the first miniature placement. Then revise the core theory openings for rhythm and notes, separating circle-map reading from transposition and clarifying relative versus parallel keys. Next apply the concept-before-example sequence across the existing lessons. After that, develop arpeggiation, polyrhythm and independent lines as optional branches, and select repertoire against the now-explicit learning outcomes.

## Theory references

The curriculum order and editorial roles above are recommendations for this course. The following primary educational references support terminology and the boundaries between topics. They do not prescribe this course order.

- [Open Music Theory Fundamentals](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/part/fundamentals/). Vocabulary and scope for pitch rhythm scales keys intervals triads numbers and texture; not the proposed course order.
- [Open Music Theory Minor Scales](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/minor-scales/). Natural minor, relative and parallel relationships, and key signatures.
- [Open Music Theory Modal Mixture](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/modal-mixture/). Borrowing from a parallel mode.
- [Open Music Theory Counterpoint](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/part/counterpoint/). A reference for an optional independent-lines branch, rather than a compulsory species-counterpoint course.
