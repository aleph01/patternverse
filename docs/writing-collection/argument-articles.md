## Teaching boundaries and examples

### P40 — The identifiable proposition

A fictional anthology committee receives a paragraph about names, biographies, and selection that asks for discussion without stating the writer’s intended recommendation. A revised claim specifies anonymous first readings while retaining biographies for the final discussion. The article distinguishes identifying that position from proving anonymity improves selection or eliminates recognition.

A separate invented privacy story shows the difference between naming a theme and claiming that its narrator applies unequal standards to observation. Ownership, location, necessary conditions, provisional conclusions, and changed conclusions receive explicit treatment. A genuine request for discussion or an exploratory piece need not disguise itself as a settled argument.

P40 owns expression and identification of the central claim. [Minto Pyramid](../../content/patterns/minto-pyramid.md) owns the answer–reasons–evidence hierarchy; P35 owns the governing inquiry. The article does not recreate the hierarchy or impose first-sentence placement on every genre.

### P41 — The position being answered

A fictional reading-room committee proposes reserving all forty seats, with phone and online booking, to give travellers certainty. An initial response drops the phone option and substitutes administrative convenience as the committee’s motive. A fairer version retains the arrangement and reason while raising the unresolved question of provision for people who learn of the talk late.

The revision supplies a more accurate starting point without proving that a mixed allocation will work. The article distinguishes stated intent from consequences, source claims from improved possible defences, and a draft suggestion from an adopted rule. Relevant conditions must survive compression, but every source detail need not receive equal space. Accurate quotation fragments can still mislead through omission.

P41 establishes the relevant conversation; P43 examines a serious challenge to the writer’s own claim. Fair entry does not require inventing consensus, making every source more reasonable, endorsing the view summarized, or treating every possible position as equally supported.

### P42 — The inferential connection

An invented museum observation follows twenty new arrivals, twelve of whom ask about the cloakroom at the first junction. The existing direction is beyond that decision point. Moving from the observation directly to a recommendation leaves the spatial reasoning unstated. A fuller paragraph makes it available: directions for a choice should be usable where the choice occurs.

The example does not claim the sign causes every question or that relocation will remove the need for help. It identifies the assumption another observer could challenge and distinguishes further evidence from a more emphatic connective. A separate invented workshop passage contrasts shared language with actions centred on one person’s control, showing why quotation needs interpretive analysis rather than automatic symbolic decoding.

P42 owns why particular evidence bears on a particular claim. P44 owns the claim’s scope and confidence, while P46 owns source status and provenance in the written account. Neither is replaced by an explicit inference. The article links to Minto for message architecture, keeps audience knowledge in view, and does not require a fixed paragraph template or a complete Toulmin system.

### P43 — The consequential challenge

An invented editorial team proposes replacing its weekly production meeting with written updates. The serious objection concerns shared-resource decisions that the meeting also handles. Restating time savings does not answer the missing coordination task. A revised proposal separates routine reporting from decisions, while leaving its practical adequacy open to investigation.

The article allows a successful objection to change or withdraw the conclusion. It distinguishes anticipated from reported concerns, stated criticism from the writer’s reconstruction, and acknowledgement from substantive revision. P41 owns fair entry into the existing discussion; P43 owns a challenge to the writer’s own claim. No ritual paragraph position or exhaustive list of possible objections is required.

### P44 — The warranted reach

A hypothetical inspection checks all twenty printed object labels in Room B on 2 April and finds English only. A gallery-wide claim exceeds that record; a bounded report preserves the established fact. Automatic hedging obscures rather than repairs the result. All details and counts are invented.

A separate invented July roster distinguishes assigned coverage from guaranteed future attendance. The article separates breadth from confidence and keeps necessary limits attached to headlines, summaries, and recommendations. P42 owns the inferential connection; P44 owns scope, confidence, and conditions; P46 owns source status and provenance. No statistical procedure or universal requirement to hedge is taught.

### P45 — The particular significance

Two invented photography classes share the label “for beginners” but require different preparation. Explaining the consequence for someone choosing a session establishes why this distinction matters more specifically than praising clear information. Reading audience and affected people are distinguished, while possible effects remain conditional rather than presented as measured outcomes.

An invented story ends with a son accepting leftovers without answering an invitation to return. The distinction between care and agreement changes what the ending resolves, giving interpretation stakes without requiring a policy consequence or universal lesson. P35 owns the inquiry; P40 the proposition; P45 why assessing this answer matters. Stakes do not replace evidence, and significance does not require inflated urgency.

### P46 — The status and origin of a statement

An invented cinema history joins a scheduled final screening, a closure letter, a later usher’s recollection, and unsupported claims about finances and private knowledge. Revision separates the source roles and preserves what remains unknown. A programme’s listing is also distinguished from evidence that an event occurred. All dates, documents, people, and words are invented.

A separate invented memoir passage preserves a remembered exchange without presenting it as an exact transcript, and distinguishes the narrator’s later interpretation from another person’s private thoughts. The article addresses attribution verbs, conflicting accounts, and local distinctions that a general source note cannot supply.

P46 owns provenance and statement status in the finished prose. P42 owns the inferential bridge; P44 owns scope and confidence. The published [Ladder of Inference](../../content/patterns/ladder-of-inference.md) and [Separate Observation, Meaning, and Action](../../content/patterns/separate-observation-meaning-and-action.md) were inspected locally and linked for their distinct reasoning and debrief functions. Their models, diagrams, and practices are not retaught or reproduced.

## Sources and metadata

[Argument metadata](argument-articles.json) records use cases, goals, mechanisms, limits, ten distinct recommendation queries per article, related IDs, explicit visual decisions, and inspected-source provenance. These fields remain outside strict frontmatter and do not introduce a recommendation feature. The existing published Minto article was inspected locally to preserve its distinct scope and confirm the cross-link.

For P40, Harvard College Writing Center’s [Thesis](https://writingcenter.fas.harvard.edu/thesis) distinguishes a central claim, its question, descriptive summary, and supporting evidence. Its academic scope is identified. No Sandel example, exercise, medical or policy claim, universal first-sentence rule, or whole-argument hierarchy is adopted. Professional recommendations, ownership, placement alternatives, and provisional conclusions are editorial development.

For P41, the Harvard Guide to Using Sources’ [Summarizing, Paraphrasing, and Quoting](https://usingsources.fas.harvard.edu/summarizing-paraphrasing-and-quoting), specifically the summary and paraphrase guidance, supports accurate representation, relevant selection, and clear source identification before response. No Milgram or O’Brien example, quotation, empirical claim, or exercise is reproduced. Motive–effect distinctions, proposal status, possible defences, and the boundary with answering objections are editorial development. They Say / I Say remains a book lead rather than a claim of direct passage inspection.

For P42, Purdue OWL’s [Toulmin Argument](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/general_writing/academic_writing/historical_perspectives_on_argumentation/toulmin_argument.html) supplies the prose distinction among claim, grounds, and warrant. Its examples, diagrams, and broader treatment are not reproduced. Harvard College Writing Center’s [Anatomy of a Body Paragraph](https://writingcenter.fas.harvard.edu/anatomy-body-paragraph) distinguishes evidence from analysis. Its literary and student-paper examples are not adopted, and no fixed paragraph formula is imposed. Objectives, interpretive alternatives, audience needs, and Minto relationships are editorial development. Toulmin’s original book was not reviewed.

For P43, Harvard College Writing Center’s [Counterargument](https://writingcenter.fas.harvard.edu/counterargument) supports reasonable challenges, responses, flexible placement, and revising a thesis when an objection succeeds. No source example, exercise, empirical meeting claim, or fixed placement rule is adopted. Task analysis, anticipated versus reported concerns, and acknowledgement versus revision are editorial applications.

For P44, the University of Manchester Academic Phrasebank’s [Being cautious](https://www.phrasebank.manchester.ac.uk/using-cautious-language/) supports avoiding unwarranted certainty and generalization, including explanations, implications, and future outcomes. No source phrase list, exercise, empirical finding about academic writing, or climate or clinical example is reproduced. Confident bounded reporting, scope versus certainty, and consistent qualification throughout a piece are editorial development.

For P45, Harvard College Writing Center’s [Introductions](https://writingcenter.fas.harvard.edu/introductions) supports explaining why a particular thesis deserves consideration rather than relying on broad topic importance or a compulsory dramatic hook. No source example, exercise, legal claim, or empirical education finding is adopted. Practical choices, conditional consequences, interpretive stakes, and proportionate urgency are editorial development.

For P46, Harvard Guide to Using Sources’ [The Nuts & Bolts of Integrating](https://usingsources.fas.harvard.edu/nuts-bolts-integrating), specifically framing and signal phrases, supports clear ownership of statements and accurate attribution verbs. No source example, exercise, quotation list, fixed paragraph template, or empirical historical claim is adopted. Memory, reconstruction, document status, and conflicting accounts are editorial applications. This is not a complete reporting or citation method, and no full book-passage review is claimed.

Sources were inspected on 25 October 2026. Book assignments elsewhere in the index remain research leads. The invented cases demonstrate writing choices and do not establish claims about actual anthology selection, booking practices, museum behaviour, research findings, team coordination, gallery provision, course selection, or cinema history.
