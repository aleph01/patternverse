# Writing collection: redundancy review

Review date: 25 October 2026. This is the historical review of the original sixty-candidate edition, covering its principles, use cases, boundary notes, and proposed scope.

**Applied:** The accepted recommendations are now incorporated into the [annotated index](annotated-index.md): 54 standalone patterns, three thematic guides, and one foundations tip. Numbers below retain their original meanings; use the [original-to-current mapping](annotated-index.md#original-to-current-mapping) for current P, G, and T references. This remains an editorial review, not passage-level verification of the source books or an audit of completed articles.

## Finding

There are no verbatim duplicates. The more important risk is **repeating the same teaching under different titles**, especially when one entry is a broad purpose and another is a narrower application. The current boundary notes identify many differences, but a written distinction is not enough if the resulting worked examples and revision moves would be substantially the same.

Two pairs are strong consolidation candidates in their current formulations: **13 with 38**, and **10 into 14**. Four other entries raise a granularity question rather than a duplicate claim: **12** is a small wording heuristic, while **28, 40, and 45** are broad synthesis topics. Several further entries should be narrowed before drafting. Most of the remaining overlaps are useful relationships and should stay linked rather than merged.

A balanced proposed revision would leave **54 standalone patterns, three overview guides, and one tip within the foundations reference**. That is an editorial option, not a required numerical target or a claim that six topics have no value. The arithmetic is sixty candidates minus two merged entries, minus three reclassified guides, minus one reclassified tip. No subject coverage needs to disappear.

## Review test

For each potentially overlapping pair, ask:

1. Does it diagnose a different failure in the text?
2. Does it prescribe a different concrete revision?
3. Can one be applied successfully while the other problem remains?
4. Can each support a substantially different worked example and exercise?

Shared vocabulary, source books, or desired effects do not alone establish duplication. A parent principle and its application can both deserve articles, but only when the application introduces enough distinct decisions to teach.

## Strong consolidation candidates

### 13 + 38: showing and the allocation of scene and summary

**13. Show, Don't Tell** currently asks the writer to dramatize a consequential moment when experiencing it matters more than receiving its summary. **38. Alternate Scene and Summary Deliberately** asks the writer to expand meaningful moments and compress connecting intervals. Both therefore answer much the same question: **what deserves dramatized attention, and what can be compressed?**

The index says that one concerns technique and the other the distribution of time. That distinction could support separate articles, but the current principle for 13 is itself a selection rule about which moments to dramatize. A likely worked example in both would summarize months of routine and expand the consequential conversation. The limits would also repeat: showing uses space; summary remains essential.

**Recommendation:** Combine them into one pattern, provisionally **Show What Matters, Summarize the Rest**, retaining **Show, Don't Tell** as a familiar title or search alias. Teach two connected decisions: choose the level of narrative attention, then render the selected moment concretely. Make this the primary home for scene-versus-summary choices and their pacing cost.

**Alternative if keeping both:** Rewrite 13 around rendering an abstract emotion or relationship through observable evidence, including examples that remain summary. For example, “Every Sunday she hid the good cups before her brother arrived” can imply a relationship without becoming a full scene. Restrict 38 to narrative duration and transitions between compressed and expanded time. That would be a defensible split, but it also requires tightening 18 so all three do not teach the same inference lesson.

### 10 → 14: replacing cliché through particular observation

**10. Replace Cliché with Observed Particulars** and **14. Choose the Telling Detail** both lead the writer toward a distinctive, revealing observation. Replacing “he was sweating bullets” with a specific nervous action would naturally appear in either article.

Cliché is a recognizable symptom, but the proposed entry does not yet have an intervention distinct from the other wording and detail patterns. Different clichés require different repairs: an empty stock phrase may need cutting, a stale image may need replacement, and conventional dialogue may intentionally reveal a character.

**Recommendation:** Put the main treatment of stock description inside 14. Route word-choice problems to 07, empty repeated phrasing to 09, and stale comparisons to 17. Preserve “my writing is full of clichés” as a troubleshooting entry, with 14 as the primary destination, rather than duplicating the replacement examples across a full standalone article.

**Alternative if keeping it:** Narrow 10 to diagnosing automatic figurative language and choosing among deletion, literal restatement, fresh comparison, and intentional reuse. That is more distinct than its present instruction to substitute observed particulars.

## Existing-library overlaps

The closest existing articles inspected are neighbors, not automatically duplicates of every writing application. This was a targeted review of likely matches rather than a claim to have semantically audited the whole library.

- **[Minto Pyramid](../../content/patterns/minto-pyramid.md)** already teaches a reader's question, an answer, supporting reasons, evidence, and examination of weak joins. Entries 39, 40, 46, 48, and 50 must not each retell that same method. Link to one canonical pyramid article from the writing collection. Retain the narrower writing interventions only with the boundaries above.
- **[Separate Observation, Meaning, and Action](../../content/patterns/separate-observation-meaning-and-action.md)** already distinguishes observable records, interpretations, uncertain recollections, and responses within a debrief. Entry 52 needs to teach attribution and provenance in a written account rather than reproduce that reflection procedure.
- **[Ladder of Inference](../../content/patterns/ladder-of-inference.md)** already traces selected observations through assumptions to conclusions. Entries 48 and 52 should refer to that reasoning model where helpful while demonstrating the distinct task of exposing an inference or source status in prose.
- **[Listen Like a Songwriter](../../content/patterns/songwriting-listen-like-a-songwriter.md)** and **[Capture Musical Seeds](../../content/patterns/songwriting-capture-musical-seeds.md)** are close process relatives of 55 and 56. The transferable ideas are shared; textual analysis, source quotation, and a prose notebook justify writing-specific treatments.
- **[Revise the Weakest Moment](../../content/patterns/songwriting-revise-the-weakest-moment.md)** and **[Finish and Learn](../../content/patterns/songwriting-finish-and-learn.md)** include focused revision and audience feedback. Link them as cross-domain applications rather than copying their process sections into 58 and 60. These songwriting neighbors currently have draft status and are not necessarily available in a production reading path.

## Repetition to prevent during authoring

The index is a plan; actual article redundancy can only be assessed after drafting. Use these guardrails to avoid reproducing the same material across otherwise distinct topics:

- Assign each major worked transformation a primary article. A neighboring article can link to it, then demonstrate a different failure and intervention.
- Use recurring passages only when the new article changes a different feature and explains a different result. Reusing the passage without new analytical work adds repetition.
- Keep shared explanations of scene, summary, viewpoint, and evidence in the foundations reference. Articles should introduce only the local distinction they need.
- Centralize general source-integrity guidance. Retain local reminders when a specific technique could otherwise invite invented dialogue, interior thoughts, or causal claims about real people.
- Give the three broad guides a comparative role. They should help choose among tools rather than repeat each tool's full explanation.
- Permit short treatments. A precise technique should not acquire padded examples and repeated caveats merely to meet a common article length.
- After the first representative drafts, compare their actual before/after moves and practices. Merge again only if the teaching remains substantially interchangeable.

The accepted dispositions have been applied to the index, including the two mergers, three guide conversions, the foundations tip, and the eleven narrower pattern scopes. The current index includes a complete original-to-current mapping and revised reading routes. Alternatives above remain part of the historical reasoning rather than pending decisions.
