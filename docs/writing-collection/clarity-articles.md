## Distinct teaching roles

- **[P01 — Move from Familiar to New](../../content/patterns/writing-move-from-familiar-to-new.md):** Local information progression between sentences. A library-service notice demonstrates retrospective assembly versus an available connection; a separate narrative arrival compares discovery with recognition. The article distinguishes cohesion from transition words, exact repetition, and rigid topic chains.
- **[P02 — Put Actors in Subjects and Actions in Verbs](../../content/patterns/writing-put-actors-in-subjects-and-actions-in-verbs.md):** Grammatical packaging of actors and actions, especially when an account needs to expose responsibility. A community-hall update develops through revision, legitimate passive focus, useful nominalization, and an unresolved approval role. This is distinct from choosing a more precise verb in P08.
- **[P03 — Keep Subjects and Verbs Close](../../content/patterns/writing-keep-subjects-and-verbs-close.md):** Placement of qualifications around the main statement. A tenants' report compares hierarchy and placement without deleting relevant history; a restricted-group example preserves which people the claim concerns; a family scene shows when delaying the answer has a purpose. No universal distance limit is proposed.
- **[P04 — Make References Unambiguous](../../content/patterns/writing-make-references-unambiguous.md):** Identifying the target of pronouns, demonstratives, recurring labels, and attributed judgments. A museum update separates caption approval from image permission; related passages examine the judgment carried by a label and the difference between unclear reference and purposeful narrative uncertainty. Distinct from simplifying vocabulary or exposing grammatical agency.
- **[P05 — Give Each Paragraph a Main Job](../../content/patterns/writing-give-each-paragraph-a-main-job.md):** Local paragraph contribution rather than shared subject alone. A repair-café proposal separates evidence of unmet need from proposed capacity, without treating white space as sufficient revision. A station scene shows a break shaping a change in experience. The article distinguishes paragraph purpose from a compulsory topic sentence and from the whole work's structure.
- **[P06 — Signal the Real Relationship](../../content/patterns/writing-signal-the-real-relationship.md):** Accurately expressing addition, qualification, consequence, and other relationships. An arts-centre report retracts an unsupported causal conclusion; a magazine proposal distinguishes scheduling caution from opposition and examines clause emphasis; a closing café shows an implication that need not be explained. A connector cannot supply missing evidence or reasoning.

The library policy, community hall, tenants' report, family situations, museum correspondence, shop scene, repair café, station encounter, arts-centre figures, magazine proposal, and closing café are invented teaching examples. Their details are not claims about real organizations, procedures, permissions, or events. Each example establishes its own context; no recurring cast or assumed prior reading is required.

## Sources and metadata

[Clarity article metadata](clarity-articles.json) records use cases, goals, mechanisms, limits, ten distinct recommendation queries per article, related IDs, visual decisions, and inspected-source provenance. This is editorial material outside the strict article frontmatter, not a new runtime search feature.

- **P01:** Duke Graduate School's [Cohesion, Coherence, and Emphasis](https://sites.duke.edu/scientificwriting/lesson-2-cohesion-coherence-and-emphasis/), specifically its guidance on familiar/new information and deliberate passive voice.
- **P02:** Boston University's [Sentences Tell Stories: A Principle of Clarity](https://www.bu.edu/teaching-writing/resources/essential-lesson-10/), including its conceptual framework, explanation of nominalizations, and acknowledgment of Joseph Williams. The university lesson was inspected; a complete passage from Williams's books was not.
- **P03:** Duke Graduate School's [Subjects and Actions](https://sites.duke.edu/scientificwriting/lesson-1-subjects-and-actions/), Principle 3, and Michael Simon's [MLA Style Center discussion of subject–verb separation](https://style.mla.org/comma-separates-subject-and-verb/).
- **P04:** Purdue OWL's [Improving Sentence Clarity](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/general_writing/mechanics/sentence_clarity.html), specifically the section on unclear pronoun references. Stable-label, stance, and narrative applications are editorial extensions rather than attributed source examples.
- **P05:** Harvard College Writing Center's [Anatomy of a Body Paragraph](https://writingcenter.fas.harvard.edu/anatomy-body-paragraph), on main points, evidence, and analysis; UNC Writing Center's [Paragraphs](https://writingcenter.unc.edu/tips-and-tools/paragraphs/), on unity, length, and forms of development. Their academic-argument guidance is distinguished from this article's original narrative application.
- **P06:** Harvard College Writing Center's [Transitions](https://writingcenter.fas.harvard.edu/transitions), specifically choosing signals after identifying a relationship and checking causal connections. Clause emphasis and narrative implication are editorial extensions.

These primary educational resources support craft guidance, not numerical claims about processing speed or a guarantee of clarity for all readers. Examples, passage structures, and commentary are original. The books in the annotated index remain further research leads unless their actual passages have been checked.
