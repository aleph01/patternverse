## Teaching boundaries and examples

### P47 — A purpose for choices

A hypothetical pottery cooperative describes its history and values without helping a first-time visitor distinguish independent studio access from a taught class. Defining the reader by relevant experience and reading situation changes the opening and exposes missing information about prerequisites. It does not supply those prerequisites or establish that the revised page works for real visitors. Material central to a members’ funding discussion may belong later on the visitor page.

An invented story about sisters clearing their father’s house compares uncomplicated reconciliation with an ending that leaves the cost of accepting help unresolved. Generous and intrusive actions can coexist. An intended effect gives the writer choices to examine without turning a character into a spokesperson or requiring a single moral verdict.

P47 owns the working intention: reader, reading situation, and intended understanding or experience. P35 owns the governing inquiry; P10 vocabulary; P45 argumentative significance. P54 owns actual reader feedback. An imagined reader is provisional, not empirical evidence of reception; exploratory writing need not settle its purpose before it begins. Changing intention should reach the whole draft.

### P48 — Room for generation when assessment blocks it

An invented writer repeatedly polishes the stairwell before a key-return scene without discovering what the exchange means. Provisional action and questions expose alternatives; a later revision selects a wish for a clean goodbye that the character cannot carry through. The developed scene then makes assessment of the original opening more meaningful. The example is an invented possible outcome, not proof that rough writing reliably discovers a story.

A separate hypothetical theatre essay keeps an unknown date, proposed explanation, and interview question visibly unresolved. Continuing provisionally does not permit invented evidence, and missing central material can justify investigation before more drafting. Immediate revisions that clarify thinking remain legitimate.

P48 owns distinguishable attention to generation and assessment when their overlap causes repeated blockage. It does not ban editing during composition, impose a timer or quota, or require a complete first draft. P52 owns prioritizing kinds of revision after material exists. P47 gives later judgment a purpose; P35 helps identify an emerging inquiry; P46 protects the status of factual gaps and provisional explanations.

### P49 — The choice behind a reading experience

An invented repair-café passage offers practical attention without promising a successful repair. Analysis connects one possible reassuring response to the sequence of invitation, bounded offer, and the visitor’s decision to stay. Alternative versions distinguish a concise explanation of the arrangement from staging the encounter and compare naming an emotion with leaving its interpretation open.

A separate invented musician scene transfers the relationship between limitation and care into original circumstances. It does not copy the model’s objects or wording, and the transfer may fail if it softens a character the new work intends to make withholding. The article preserves context, preparation, alternative reader responses, and the distinction between a response and knowledge of the author’s private intentions.

P49 owns craft learning from reading and testing a comparable move. P47 supplies intended effect, P11 the expansion or compression choice, and P50 preservation of the source and craft question. No passage proves a universal technique, and reading for pleasure need not become continuous analysis.

### P50 — Particulars retained with a route back

A hypothetical costume-store visit contrasts an atmospheric impression with a note preserving a repair sequence, date, place, object, and open question. The developed example keeps observation, paraphrase, exact wording, recollection, and the writer’s interpretation distinct. Every detail and note is invented.

A second hypothetical programme note preserves a temporary school-hall arrangement, its page and date, and the route to the accessible copy. The condition would be lost in a note saying only that the group moved. Retrieval can use modest meaningful labels and a provisional use without requiring an elaborate permanent system. Factual and fictional uses of collected material retain different obligations.

P50 owns keeping potential material retrievable before its final use. P12 selects telling details for the passage; P46 makes provenance readable in the finished account. Collection does not establish wider claims, supply an interpretation, or justify indefinitely postponing the draft. No memory-performance benefit or mandatory notebook is claimed.

### P51 — An account of the draft that exists

A hypothetical workshop-storage proposal promises an allocation arrangement, mainly illustrates crowding, and concludes with more shelves. A condensed account of the actual contributions exposes the mismatch without supplying the missing investigation or deciding the solution. Topic-only labels would conceal much of the problem.

A separate invented boat story shows why ordinary maintenance after a decision to sell can carry attachment rather than merely advance an argument. Similar summaries may conceal different development, so diagnosis returns to the passages before a revision is chosen. Paragraphs, sections, encounters, and partial drafts can be appropriate units at different scales; associative work need not become a claim-and-proof outline.

P51 owns the diagnostic map. P05 concerns a unit’s contribution, P35 coherence around inquiry, and P47 the working purpose. P52 owns revision priorities; G02 remains planned for comparing structures. Mapping actual function must not silently become an ideal outline or a substitute for missing evidence.

### P52 — Revision priorities and dependencies

An invented evening-bell essay opens with a universal shared-day claim, but its two interview accounts place the bell at different points in working routines. Polishing the installation history cannot settle what account the material supports. Reconsidering purpose changes selection, relative emphasis, paragraph contributions, and finally the work of a parallel sentence. All historical material and accounts are invented; the new direction still requires support rather than replacing one unsupported generalization with another.

A separate invented reading-room proposal exposes a conceptual ambiguity through local wording about free availability and member reservations. The article treats revision as iterative: a sentence can expose an unresolved larger choice, different sections can need different attention, and obvious corrections can be made when useful. Priorities do not require rebuilding an already coherent work or making narrative and associative prose resemble an argument.

P52 owns which interventions deserve attention first. P51 diagnoses the actual structure, P48 protects discovery when evaluation blocks it, and P47 supplies the provisional purpose. Dependencies rather than a rigid stage order explain the priority; stopping criteria concern a sufficiently developed and supported work rather than exhausting every alternative.

### P53 — An audible clue that needs interpretation

Hypothetical equipment-return instructions omit a case reference and bury an ordered sequence inside a clause. An audible reading makes the retention burden and possible attachment harder to glide past. Revision supplies the missing object and exposes the sequence; shorter sentences alone would not settle the underlying arrangement.

An invented packing scene repeats a sentence ending. Retaining that shape can make packing mechanical; interrupting it at a photograph can shift attention. Neither is automatically better. The article distinguishes the words present from an expressive performance that supplies missing information, and treats a stumble as a clue with several possible causes rather than a fault proved by sound.

P53 owns listening as diagnosis. P17 owns variation, P20 purposeful repetition, and P04 reference. Human and automated readings have different limits; hearing and speaking are not equally useful or available to everyone. Another accessible method can serve the task. Spoken and silently read prose have different resources, and audible fluency does not verify evidence or larger design.

### P54 — Responses as evidence for revision

A fictional print-exchange call elicits praise and a proposed questions section. A more specific, non-leading request reveals that a reader expected to buy prints rather than contribute an edition. The writer can consider an opening that establishes the exchange before adding a new format. The candidate revision still needs accurate requirements and further assessment; the example is not a report of actual user testing.

A separate invented story ends with a former owner’s reflection in an empty house’s back door. Different readings can develop the intended unsettled departure rather than establish a need for consensus. A suggestion to make the character cry is distinguished from the reader’s reported experience of emotional absence. The writer investigates that experience before selecting a remedy.

P54 owns actual encounters with drafts, complementing P47’s imagined reader and intended effect. P52 prioritizes resulting changes; P49 connects textual choices to responses. Suitable readers, stage-specific questions, praise, and unexpected difficulties can all inform revision. Intention is neither immunity from criticism nor an obligation to adopt every fix, and no response substitutes for factual verification.
