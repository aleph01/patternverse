## Distinct teaching roles

- **[P12 — Choose the Telling Detail](../../content/patterns/writing-choose-the-telling-detail.md):** Selection of a particular that changes the reader's understanding. A residents' meeting compares appearance-based stock description with a chair's use of a speaking timer; contextual alternatives keep the incident from becoming a complete judgment of character. A lunchroom's anticipated meal makes a social relationship visible without treating one practice as evidence about every visitor. The article distinguishes revealing selection from lexical specificity, sensory presence, and mandatory dramatization; it also addresses contrived symbolism and cliché replacement.
- **[P13 — Make Abstractions Tangible](../../content/patterns/writing-make-abstractions-tangible.md):** Connecting a named concept to an inspectable arrangement and explaining the relationship. Two hypothetical scheduling systems clarify whose control “flexibility” describes. A film club's preparation and public acknowledgment clarify one meaning of invisible work. The article moves back to useful general terms, uses contrasts to expose boundaries, and distinguishes an illustrative case from evidence of generality or causation. Its main concern is conceptual understanding rather than difficult vocabulary, scene construction, or a later evidence pattern.
- **[P14 — Engage the Relevant Senses](../../content/patterns/writing-engage-the-relevant-senses.md):** Physical legibility through an observer's encounter with a situation. A ferry corridor changes from a visual inventory into a route negotiated while carrying a sleeping child; an audition example examines selective attention. The article treats perception verbs as choices rather than banned words, avoids a five-sense checklist and fixed emotion codes, and distinguishes an observable sensation from an interpretation. Photographs and later visits are discussed as sources with limits rather than permission to invent past experience.
- **[P15 — Use Metaphor to Reveal a Relationship](../../content/patterns/writing-use-metaphor-to-reveal-a-relationship.md):** Borrowing a useful shape from a different subject. An archive guide compares a window with a patchwork and examines what each implies about selection and gaps; a fictional dinner uses a customs inspection to express a viewpoint. The article distinguishes metaphor, simile, and developed analogy without making terminology the main lesson. It addresses imported assumptions, stale or mixed comparisons, and the boundary between an illuminating image and evidence.
- **[P16 — Remove Explanatory Echoes](../../content/patterns/writing-remove-explanatory-echoes.md):** Deleting a gloss after the material already supplies the intended inference. A former festival organizer's arrival distinguishes repeated role explanation from necessary badge context and a new internal contradiction. A theatre survey distinguishes analysis of a timeline from an immediate paraphrase of that analysis. The article preserves legitimate interiority, narrative voice, and argumentative explanation; it does not prescribe withholding information or avoiding all telling.

The committee, phone glance, lunchroom, schedules, film club, ferry, audition, archive, dinner, festival, and theatre survey are original, hypothetical teaching examples. Their circumstances, procedures, and interpretations are not claims about real people, policies, or events. The nonfiction comparisons explain source and inference boundaries without presenting invented reporting as fact.
