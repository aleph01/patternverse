# Stand-up comedy: three pilot articles

These are complete editorial drafts for review, saved as draft content rather than published Commons
entries. The articles contain six professional performance passages, three original worked revisions,
and explicit distinctions between neighboring techniques. No executable practices were added: this
pilot is an article commission.

## Verification scope

Each selected upload was opened in the browser. The visible uploader identity and public,
timestamped transcript were inspected. The JFL description identifies the stage year; the Apollo
upload date is not used as a stage date. Izzard's [official stand-up archive](https://www.eddieizzard.com/en/standup)
identifies _Unrepeatable_ with the 1994–95 period and the Albery Theatre. This corrects the tempting but
incorrect attribution of the advertising passage to _Dress to Kill_.

The research is caption-based, rather than an independent acoustic or visual-performance audit.
Automatic captions can mistranscribe words and group several seconds into one segment. Therefore:

- The articles use short, intelligible excerpts and concise paraphrase rather than full transcripts.
- No exact pause durations, facial expressions, or gesture descriptions are attributed to the performers.
- The JFL card-tattoo example identifies the caption's audience-response marker as such. It does not
  equate that marker with a measured laugh, or claim that every listener predicted a particular card.
- Interpretations of setup, payoff, and tag boundaries are editorial analyses, not statements about
  what the comedian intended or how the material was originally written.
- Original revisions are labeled as constructed, untested material. Their proposed performance cues
  are staging suggestions, not observations of the professional clips.

A subsequent audiovisual editorial pass could add verified performance notes. The present articles
do not depend on those observations being true.

## Practitioner references and overlap decisions

- Jared Volle's [Joke-Structure Guide](https://creativestandup.com/wp-content/uploads/manual/joke-structure-guide.pdf)
  supplies terminology around assumptions and omitted punchlines. It is a practitioner framework,
  not a universal empirical taxonomy. The Izzard example is checked against the artist's official clip.
- Neil Thornton's [Comedy Writing Workbook](https://www.nzcomedyschool.com/blog/writingworkbook)
  supports revision and further exploration of functioning material. Its prescriptive wording is not
  adopted as a universal rule about what all stand-up must do.
- Jerry Corley's [getting-started article](https://www.standupcomedyclinic.com/3-cool-ways-to-get-started-writing-your-comedy-act/)
  informed the earlier taxonomy. It is background rather than a performance source for these pilots.
- Misdirection and reversal overlap, but the first article focuses on the audience's inference.
  Add Tags and heightening overlap, but a tag need not increase stakes. An omitted punchline differs
  from a pause that delays a necessary verbal payoff; the third article makes the boundary explicit.

## Editorial review

Each draft has a concrete opening, an explanation of the move, two professional passages, an original
weak-to-revised example, useful situations, failure modes, and related-pattern links. Tables carry
the comparison and sequence explanations; there are no decorative images or new dependencies.
The performer material is attributed and kept separate from the original illustrations.

The pilot does not create all nine proposed taxonomy collections or imply that the rest of the
stand-up library has been authored. Those names are package metadata for future organization.
