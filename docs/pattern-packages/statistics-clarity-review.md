# Statistics clarity and accessibility review

Reviewed and revised all 42 statistics articles on 25 October 2026, following the accuracy review. This pass focuses on readers meeting the ideas for the first time: explain unfamiliar terms, make the calculation’s purpose visible before its details, and connect each result to what a reader can reasonably conclude.

## What changed

- Defined terms at the point of use, including estimand, standard error, residual, offset, margin, risk set, and intracluster correlation. Where symbols are useful, their names and roles are explained in words.
- Expanded worked examples where a beginner previously had to infer missing steps. The distribution and case-mix articles now reproduce their interactive starting cases in ordinary article text. Other additions explain a seven-point difference-in-differences result through its untreated comparison, a single simulation’s coverage result, and the weighted arithmetic behind partial pooling.
- Explained how to read the graphics. All nine interactive examples now have meaningful starting-chart alternative text and specific reading cues; the reused case-mix example is explicitly distinguished from the causal article’s different training dataset. Readers can follow the central results without running JavaScript or relying on color.
- Broke up dense passages and replaced abstract wording with concrete explanations. Technical assumptions and advanced notes remain available; simplifying the prose does not remove the conditions needed for a valid interpretation.
- Replaced generic “Technical reference” link labels with descriptive names and refreshed article word counts in the package metadata.

The review preserves the 42 article identities, numerical input files, all 84 SVG assets, widget settings, and the 16 guided applications. It is an editorial and reader-layout review, not a formal accessibility certification or a user study.
