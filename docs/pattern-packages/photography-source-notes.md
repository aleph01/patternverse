# Photography technical and editorial source notes

Reviewed 2026-10-23. Explanations and examples are newly written. Technical descriptions are
distinguished from artistic choices; source preferences such as softer portrait light being
universally preferable are not adopted as rules. No tutorial photographs are reused.

The reference separates physical capture exposure from rendered brightness. Highlight recovery
is described in terms of whether recorded distinctions survive clipping. No exact compensation
values, unreported EXIF, guaranteed shutter-speed thresholds, or capture histories are invented.

The photographic sources are individually listed in `photography-provenance.json`, with
creator, source page, applicable permission, modifications, and relevant capture limitations.
Source pages and their license sections/wikitext were read before approval of those assets.
Public-domain government sheets are identified through their archival descriptions; sheet
processing dates are not silently converted into per-frame exposure times.

## Collection review

The drafts distinguish intention, viewpoint, visual separation, focus distance and depth of
field. Shooting variation, holding a frame, anticipating an event, choosing a frame, and learning
from frames have separate articles with explanatory cross-links. Each article includes several
developed contexts, tradeoffs and exceptions. Portraits include collaboration and the person's
preferences. Sharpness, blur, brightness and contrast are evaluated against intention rather
than ranked universally. Phones and cameras receive relevant capability distinctions without
requiring expensive equipment or manual mode.

Generated lighting and composition examples illustrate appearance. Real photographs provide
the optical, exposure and observed-event comparisons. Native SVG overlays do not transform
generated material into evidence of exact photographic settings. Editorial crops and the
restrained tonal edit are labelled as such.
