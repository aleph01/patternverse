# Somatic and Experiential Therapy

Reading edition: **46 articles**, grouped into nine functional families, with **six embedded subpatterns**. All content is saved as draft. No interactive practices, widgets, or executable exercises are included. Original reference 52 is excluded.

The expanded articles are original educational explanations with substantial fictional examples, distinctions between neighboring methods, source links, and relevant limits. Each is approximately 1,000–1,600 words and follows the shared structure below. They describe therapeutic methods rather than provide a treatment protocol. The categories describe functions, not a fixed sequence.

## Core-pattern drafts

The revised editorial direction centers on twelve recurring patterns of experiential change. **[Give Experience a Form](../../content/patterns/give-experience-a-form.md)** is the first completed pilot draft. It brings together Speak From the Position, Address It Directly, Give It Bodily Form, Amplify What Is Emerging, and Build a Symbolic Scene within one article organized around a shared core move. Enactment and constructed symbolic representation belong to this broader family.

The expanded pilot is approximately 5,100 words and uses the newly agreed structure: an opening scene followed by the difficulty, core move, experiential unfolding, worked example, rationale, conditions and limits, wider process, and expressions and sources. Within experiential unfolding, substantial sections explain implementations in Coherence Therapy, Gestalt therapy, Hakomi, individual Emotion-Focused Therapy, psychodrama, and sandtray and Sandplay approaches. Each implementation includes a fictional example, its particular aim, and the feedback or witnessing that shapes the work. The sand-work section distinguishes conversational sandtray processing from Kalffian Sandplay. The central worked example moves between words, gesture, and facial expression.

The pilot includes **one original light editorial illustration**, with a separately composed phone version, comparing the five forms. It follows the approved Architecture and Filmmaking language: fixed white surfaces, regular Helvetica Neue, fine lines, quiet gray context, and restrained mint and green emphasis. A real sandplay photograph from Akimoto and colleagues (2021), reproduced unchanged under CC BY 4.0 with source and author credits, replaces the abstract overhead scene diagram. The photograph is bundled as a local content asset. Its caption distinguishes the photographed scene from the fictional example that follows. Interactive practices remain excluded. ## Relational Contact and Repair

Establish connection and address disruptions in the relationship.

- **40. [Loving Presence](../../content/patterns/loving-presence.md)** — Cultivate appreciative attention that supports an unhurried, responsive therapeutic relationship.
- **18. [Undoing Aloneness](../../content/patterns/undoing-aloneness.md)** — Notice whether difficult experience can be felt in the company of an emotionally available other.
- **26. [Turning Toward the Other](../../content/patterns/turning-toward-the-other.md)** — Bring an accessible vulnerable feeling into a direct exchange with a partner.
- **39. [Repairing the Rupture](../../content/patterns/repairing-the-rupture.md)** — Explore relational strain and restore collaboration through acknowledgment and a changed response.

## Pacing and Contact with the Present

Keep experience approachable through manageable intensity and contact with the current setting.

- **16. [Orienting](../../content/patterns/orienting.md)** — Recover contact with present surroundings through exploratory sensory attention.
- **10. [Clearing a Space](../../content/patterns/clearing-a-space.md)** — Acknowledge several concerns without having to enter or solve them immediately.
- **5. [Titration](../../content/patterns/titration.md)** — Approach charged experience in manageable increments with room to settle.
- **1. [Pendulation](../../content/patterns/pendulation.md)** — Allow movement between activation and experiences of support or settling.

## Editorial structure

Every article begins with an unheaded, recognizable moment and then uses these eight sections:

1. **Core idea** — the source tradition, defining move, and possibility it opens.
2. **What changes in the experience** — how attention, participation, and feedback develop. Embedded subpatterns appear here under their own subheadings.
3. **A worked example** — a substantial fictional encounter, including correction, adjustment, and a specific outcome.
4. **Why the move may help** — the experiential rationale, distinguished from model-specific explanations and uncertain mechanisms.
5. **When it helps—and what can get in the way** — recognizable indications, contrasting situations, and conditions that affect fit.
6. **Boundaries and common misunderstandings** — the pattern's particular failure modes and limits.
7. **Neighboring patterns** — explained relationships and distinctions, with links for further reading.
8. **Lineage and sources** — attribution, source links, and the status of the examples and editorial naming.

The process descriptions remain explanatory prose rather than guided exercises. Relational stances such as Loving Presence describe the practitioner's attention and responses. A useful outcome may be a clearer objection, a revised understanding, or a decision to stop; the examples do not require relief or a dramatic breakthrough.

The original numbers remain stable. References 31, 34, 44, 46, 47, and 49 are subpatterns rather than separate articles. Embodying Emotions is added as reference 53, leaving excluded reference 52 unchanged. Loving Presence is a relational stance. Each article links to three neighboring patterns; those links are navigational, not a required treatment order.

The current edition uses prose and original narrative examples. Illustrations and interactive practices are not part of this edition. The JSON manifest records the category, source tradition, original number, subpatterns, and visual decision for each article.
